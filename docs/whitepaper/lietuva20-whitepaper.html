Parliament monitoring and public engagement

See how the Seimas votes. Then cast your own vote.

Lietuva 2.0 turns every bill in the Seimas into something a citizen can read in two minutes, vote on, or hand to someone they trust. Then it shows, vote by vote, how far Parliament is from the people it represents.

01 · The idea

A parliament monitor with a vote attached

Lietuva 2.0 does four things, in this order. Each one works without the next, so the product is useful from the first release.

Follow

Every bill registered in the Seimas appears automatically, with its stage, its next vote and how each MP and faction voted so far.

Seimas open data, e-seimas.lrs.lt
Understand

An AI summary explains what changes, who is affected and the main arguments on both sides, with every claim linked to the article it comes from.

Generated once per bill version, editable by the community
Decide

Vote yourself, or let people you trust vote for you on topics you don't follow. Your own vote always wins.

Delegation per topic: people first, then MPs or parties
Compare

See where the Seimas and its citizens agree, which factions vote as a block, and which MPs vote the way you would.

Most useful in the months before an election
02 · Why this, why now

Everything is public, yet almost nobody reads it

The Seimas already publishes a lot. Bills, amendments, agendas and every roll-call vote are online, some of them as machine-readable open data. But a bill is published as a legal diff against a law most people have never read. A single bill can be voted on a dozen times, from presentation (pateikimas) through consideration (svarstymas) to adoption (priėmimas), plus separate votes on amendments. The information exists, but few people can follow it.

So for four years most citizens judge Parliament from headlines, and then vote on one day. Politicians in turn hear from the loudest voices, not from a broad and steady signal.

Three things have changed since the first Lietuva 2.0:

  • Language models can read legislation. A neutral, sourced summary of a 40-page bill now costs cents and takes seconds. Ten years ago it needed a volunteer lawyer.
  • Delegation is a proven idea. "Liquid democracy" tools have been tried by parties and cities across Europe. What was missing is a link to real parliamentary votes, so that a delegation actually means something.
  • The data exists. The Seimas open-data services expose MPs, factions, sittings and individual votes. That lets us import Parliament's record directly instead of typing it in by hand.
03 · From 2.0 then to 2.0 now

Same name, same mission, rebuilt from scratch

The original Lietuva 2.0 already had the core loop: problems, ideas, issues to vote on and an "official result" to compare against. What held it back was manual work and heavy identity checks.

We keep the ideas that worked: topic categories that people follow, arguments for and against attached to comments, versioned summaries, and the link from a citizen's idea to the vote that decides it. We do not port the old code.

04 · Life of a bill

The public vote runs alongside the Seimas

A bill opens for public voting the day it is registered and closes when the Seimas holds its final vote. That timing decides what "your vote" means at each moment.

06 · Vote delegation

Trust someone per topic, and still keep the last word

Nobody can read every bill. For each topic you choose whose judgement to follow: first people you trust on the platform, then MPs or parties as a fallback. If you vote yourself, your vote replaces all of it.

Topic of the bill
Try a scenario
How your vote is resolved

    Your own vote always wins

    Voting yourself, even at the last minute, replaces every delegation for that bill. You can also change a vote until voting closes.

    Two levels, in order

    Level 1 is people and organisations on the platform. Level 2 is MPs, factions or parties. Level 2 is used only if nobody at level 1 voted.

    Weights split one vote

    A level can hold several delegates with weights, for example 60% an MP and 40% a party. If one of them is absent, their share is spread over the others.

    No chains

    If your delegate delegated their own vote, it does not pass on to you. You get your fallback instead. This keeps results explainable and stops power piling up invisibly.

    Delegates act in public

    Anyone can become a delegate, but delegates' votes are public. The people who delegate to them stay private, and only the total count is shown.

    Nothing resolves, nothing counts

    If no delegate voted, your vote is not counted. We never assign a vote by default.

    FOR EVERY POLITICIAN

    Explain your vote where people see it

    A short explanation attached to each recorded vote appears on the bill page and in every delegator's notification. It reaches people who chose to trust you, without ads and without an algorithm deciding who sees it.

    FOR EVERY POLITICIAN

    A continuous poll of your constituency

    Aggregated opinion of users in your constituency or municipality, by topic and by bill. Groups under 50 people are never shown, so no individual can be identified.

    FOR EVERY POLITICIAN

    A visible mandate between elections

    "2,140 citizens trust me on the economy" is a number that grows or shrinks with each vote. It rewards consistency and good explanations, not the loudest voice.

    FOR EVERY POLITICIAN

    Test a proposal before registering it

    Post a draft or an idea and get structured feedback: arguments for and against, the objections that come up most, and early support by topic and region.

    FOR CANDIDATES

    Get into the election match

    Candidates without a Seimas record answer how they would have voted on 20–30 key bills. Their answers are compared with voters' positions, so newcomers are on equal terms with MPs.

    FOR EVERY POLITICIAN

    A right of reply

    Politicians can respond to any AI summary or statistic about them. The reply is shown next to it. The methodology behind every number is public.

    Verification. MPs are verified through their official lrs.lt email address, council members through their municipality's address, and candidates against the Central Electoral Commission (VRK) list with confirmation from their party. Parties, NGOs, unions and business associations can get a verified organisation badge and act as delegates.

    10 · Beyond the Seimas

    One model for every body that votes

    A municipal council, the Government and the European Parliament all do the same thing: a group of people votes on a proposal. We store them in one shared format and write a small adapter for each source.

    First release

    Seimas

    apps.lrs.lt/sip · e-seimas.lrs.lt

    Official XML open data for MPs, factions, sittings and per-MP votes; bill texts and versions from e-seimas.

    Pilot, 2–3 councils

    Municipal councils

    council portals, PDF minutes

    Varies across the 60 municipalities. Start where results are published in a structured form; elsewhere extract from minutes with AI and a human check.

    Later

    European Parliament

    EP roll-call open data

    Roll-call votes are public. Useful for following Lithuania's 11 MEPs and their groups.

    Later

    Government

    TAR, Government sittings

    The Government decides collectively and has no individual votes, so we compare the public vote with the decision only.

    11 · Trust and privacy

    Open accounts, careful results

    We are dropping bank-level login so that joining takes a minute. That makes the platform an opinion signal, not a referendum, and we present it that way.

    Keeping results honest

    • One account per verified phone number, plus rate limits and bot detection.
    • Results can be filtered to accounts older than 30 days, which damps sudden campaigns.
    • Coordinated bursts, such as thousands of new accounts voting on one bill, are flagged publicly on the bill.
    • Delegate totals are public, so a concentration of delegated votes is visible.
    • An optional stronger identity tier can be added later without changing the model.

    Protecting voters

    • Political opinions are special-category data under GDPR. Votes are private by default and stored with explicit consent.
    • No personal codes, addresses or document numbers are stored.
    • Aggregates by constituency or group appear only above 50 people.
    • Data is hosted in the EU. Users can export or delete everything they have given us.

    Keeping AI accountable

    • Every AI text is labelled, versioned and linked to the articles it is based on.
    • Prompts and the neutrality rules for summaries are published.
    • Community editors and politicians can correct or reply, and every change is visible in the history.

    Staying neutral

    • No advertising and no paid promotion, including during election campaigns.
    • Every politician and party gets the same tools on the same terms.
    • The code stays open source under GPL-3.0, as it is today. The methodology behind every statistic is public.
