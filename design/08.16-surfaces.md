# 8.16 Surfaces: what lies on a sheet and what on the ground

**[Decided by the owner on 2026-11-15, on the built start page: asked why some sections are white and others grey, and
that the answer be applied to the whole site. The rule is the designer's.]** The stylesheet has three surfaces. Until now
nothing said which is for what, and the start page had its main content on the ground and a lesser block on a sheet.

- **Ground** (`--ground`, the page itself) carries what frames the content: the page's title and lead, the way back,
  notices (`.note`), an introduction, a plain row of links, the footer.
- **Sheet** (`.sheet`, white) carries the content: what a person came to the page to read or to act on. A list, a
  table, the parts of a bill's page, a profile, a form, a panel of the person's own things, an item of a queue. One
  unit of content is one sheet; a sheet is never put inside a sheet.
- **Quiet sheet** (`.sheet--quiet`) is only ever inside a sheet: a part that opens within the content and steps back
  from it, such as the consent before a first side, a report form, a restriction form. It never stands by itself on
  the ground.
- **The test for a page:** its main content is on a sheet, and nothing lesser than the main content is on a sheet while
  the main content lies on the ground. Controls that only filter or sort a list lie on the ground directly above the
  list, and the list is on a sheet (or each of its items is), the same way on every list page.
- **Notices are not surfaces.** `.note` and `.note--quiet` keep their own colours wherever they stand.
- **One exception:** the start page's introduction for a visitor ("Kaip tai veikia", 8.7) stays on the ground: it
  frames the agenda, which is the page's content and is on a sheet.
- **The start page by this rule:** "Jūsų projektai" on a sheet, the agenda's timeline on a sheet. (Its "Organizacijos"
  block, plain on the ground, was removed by the owner on 2026-11-16; see 8.7.)
- **Pages of several parts** (a bill, an MP's profile, an organisation, a vote) give each part its own sheet; a text
  that is one document (the methodology, the moderation rules) is one sheet.
- **A control drawn in the sheet's own colour** (the "Man svarbu" mark) takes the tint when it stands on a sheet, so it
  keeps its shape.
- **Floating panels** (the feedback form, 8.10) are overlays above the page and not part of this.
- **Comments (8.23, proposed)** lie on their target's sheet, below the target, and are not a sheet themselves; their
  writing box is a quiet sheet inside it.
- **Dark theme:** the quiet sheet's colour lies between the ground and the sheet, as in the light theme, so it steps
  back there too (until now it was the lightest of the three).
- **Choices [decided by the owner on 2026-11-17, from the designer's samples: "Let's change those for arguments, but keep
  the frequences in settings as is, a normal radio"].** A choice between a bill's sides, or a statement's stance, is the
  joined-button control (`.switch` over real radio inputs, so it works without script, by keyboard and with the page's
  focus ring, and stays disabled while the island is locked): the side of an argument, an MP's response's stance ("už" in
  the side's green, "prieš" in its red, "Dar neapsisprendžiau" neutral, the second ink), an organisation's statement
  ("Pastabos" neutral) and the date flag ("Data neteisinga", "Balsuos anksčiau"). Three options stand one under another
  where their fieldset is narrower than 34rem (`.switch-field`, `.switch--stack`). Every other choice stays a radio (how
  often the MP's summaries and the member's emails come, the feedback's kind), and consents and settings stay plain
  checkboxes (every consent box, the email switches, "Viešas profilis", report grounds, the merge target).

**Done when:** every page of the site (public, a member's, an organisation's, the moderators' and the staff's) has been
looked at in the light theme against the test, with the result listed page by page; no quiet sheet stands on the
ground; no sheet is inside a sheet.

## 8.16.1 The action row under a text [the designer's proposal, 2026-11-17; settled by the owner the same day, on a desktop, below]

The owner, from a phone (~390 px), 2026-11-17: under an argument the name stood on its own line, "Man svarbu · 0" and
"Pranešti" on the next, "Aptarimas" on a third and further left than the rest, and for a moderator "even more
complicated": "Can we design a whole non breaking line for commands, or icons, or something to make it nice?"

- **One row under every member's text, the same everywhere:** arguments, organisations' statements, MPs' responses and
  comments. Line 1: the author's name (a link with its card for a public profile, 8.25.4), for a comment its time; for a
  statement and a response the line with the dates as before. Line 2: one row that never wraps, from 360 px up and in a
  side's column on a wide screen: "Man svarbu · n" (arguments only; a toggle as before, filled when marked),
  "Aptarimas (n)" (8.23.6), "Sutinku" (comments only, 8.23.10) and "⋯" at the end. Every item is 44 px tall and they
  share one baseline; the name and the row stand on the text's own left edge (an MP's response, drawn mirrored, on its
  right edge), so nothing under a text is indented differently.
- **"⋯" ("Daugiau veiksmų") holds the rarer actions:** "Pranešti", the author's own "Atsiimti" (an argument) or
  "Ištrinti" (a comment), and for a moderator in a staff-passkey session "Moderuoti", which opens the moderator's actions
  for that text below the row: the filled main action and "Kiti veiksmai", the same popover menu as on every moderation
  screen (8.3, "The screen", owner 2026-11-17). It is the browser's own popover, as a hint is (8.15): it
  opens without JavaScript (then in the middle of the screen), a press elsewhere or Escape closes it and the focus stays on
  or goes back to "⋯"; a small script places it beside "⋯". A chosen action closes the menu and its form opens below the
  whole row as a quiet sheet (8.16), as report forms did. A visitor's "Pranešti" note works without script; a member's
  items stay disabled until the island runs; the trigger itself is never disabled (it only shows the menu).
- **"Aptarimas" opens below the row at its full width:** the `<details>` holds only its summary, which stands in the row;
  the discussion is the panel right after it, shown only while the `<details>` is open, and laid below the whole row by
  the row's own layout (no `display: contents` on `<details>`, which browsers treat differently). The summary keeps its
  expanded state for assistive technology and names the panel it controls.
- **Words with small icons:** a heart beside "Man svarbu", a check in a circle beside "Sutinku", a speech bubble beside
  "Aptarimas", three dots for "⋯", drawn inline in the text's colour; no icon library and nothing fetched. "Man svarbu"
  always keeps its words (a term the icon alone would not say). [Decided by the owner on 2026-11-17: "by the arguments there should be an
  explanation".] One hint beside the arguments' heading, "Kas yra „Man svarbu“?" (8.15), says when it can be marked: either
  side's arguments, not one's own, counted only on the side one asks for (owner, 2026-11-18; until then: after saying what
  one asks for, only one's own side); "Plačiau" to `/metodika#argumentai`. The same
  for everyone the server serves (a disabled button's title is shown by few browsers); the MP's own account, which gets no
  "Man svarbu" (8.5.4), loses it once the island runs. **The word "Aptarimas" shows at desktop widths, closed
  and open alike [decided by the owner on 2026-11-17, settling the open choice]:** the arguments run at the page's full
  width below the bill's top part (8.12.1), so a side's column is wide enough; only where the row is narrower than about
  20rem (a 360 px phone) is the word kept for screen readers alone, with the bubble and the count shown.
- **What opens stays where the person pressed [owner, 2026-11-17].** "Aptarimas" opens in place under its own text, in
  that side's column; nothing else moves (the two sides no longer stand one above the other while one is open, 8.23.6).
  A chosen action of "⋯" ("Pranešti", the author's "Atsiimti" or "Ištrinti", "Moderuoti") and a confirmation open
  directly under the row, **above** an open discussion, not after it.
- **The name first [owner, 2026-11-17].** Arguments, statements, responses and comments all read: the name (with its card
  where there is one), then the text, then the row.
- **"Man svarbu" and "Sutinku" are toggles of one look [owner, 2026-11-17].** For a member the button stays neutral; only
  its icon fills, in the brand's green, while it is marked, so the count stays readable; `aria-pressed` says the state;
  one's own argument keeps its disabled "Man svarbu" with the reason. "Sutinku" keeps its word in both states (no
  "Atsiimti „Sutinku“"), and the first one still asks inline that the name will be shown (8.23.10). A visitor's "Man
  svarbu" is a link to sign in that comes back to the bill (6.9), of the same look, not a grey label that looks like a
  button.
- **The served page is the same for every member and visitor** as before: what is the caller's own (their "Atsiimti",
  "Ištrinti", "Sutinku", a moderator's "Moderuoti") comes from the island in the browser.

## 8.16.2 What changed between two versions [the owner's request of 2026-11-18; the details are the designer's, 12-open]

Every public version history shows what changed rather than whole texts side by side: a bill's description
(`/seimas/{n}/istorija`, 8.13), an organisation's statement (on its profile, 8.14.14), an MP's response ("Ankstesnės
versijos", 8.5.5) and the introduction letter (`/suvestines/pristatymas`, 8.19.5). An organisation's earlier names and
websites stay "A → B": they are short.

- **Newest first, each version against the one before it [owner, 2026-11-18].** Every history, the names and websites included, lists the newest first; the oldest is shown whole. Where the text in force is shown above the
  history (a response, a statement, the letter), it also opens the list with its own changes, so the last
  edit is visible too (a withdrawn response opens it with the withdrawal, "atsakymas atsiimtas {data}", its text struck through): "dabartinė versija" (a response "dabartinė versija, parašyta {data}", the letter "Dabartinė
  versija, išsiųsta {data}"). A version whose text did not change (only the side or the subject did) is shown whole,
  unmarked.
- **Word by word, within the layout.** Both texts are laid out first (8.2, "A text's layout") and compared line by line:
  an unchanged paragraph or list item stays as it is; one line in place of one line, or two lines that share at least
  two fifths of their words, are compared word by word; any other line is removed or inserted whole, where it stood (so a
  moved paragraph is removed in one place and inserted in the other). The newer version's paragraphs and lists keep their
  layout, numbers as typed; a removed list item stays in its list, struck through. Words, runs of spaces and each
  punctuation mark are compared on their own, so nothing typed is lost; a space between two changes joins them.
- **How it looks.** Inserted words are underlined on a light slate ground, removed words struck through in the muted
  ink: neutral marks, never the red and green of a warning (the wording rule on neutral chips). Each mark begins with
  hidden "pridėta:" or "pašalinta:", so a screen reader says what it is. Under each changed version, "Rodyti
  tekstą prieš pakeitimą" (the one collapsing pattern, 8.15) holds the text before that change whole [owner,
  2026-11-18, after seeing it]: the text after it is already on the page (the text in force, or the "before" of the next
  newer change), and so the original stays reachable in full even when it was never stored as a version of its own (an
  edit's old text is the original). A version with no earlier text has no such disclosure. No script.
- **A limit.** The comparison does at most about two million steps per pair of versions; past that (a very long
  paragraph changed at both ends) the changed part is shown as removed and inserted whole.

## 8.16.3 One green, the sides' pair and straight bubbles [the owner's requests of 2026-11-18; the values are the designer's]

The owner, 2026-11-18: the "už" buttons in the links' green and a red made to match it; the threshold's bar the same
green, lighter; the bubbles straight; the tail pointing to the name; the menu's chosen item in the same green; the
outlined side button's hover not cut where its border is not. `design/ui/lt2.css` holds the tokens; the site's
stylesheet copies them.

- **"For" is the light theme's link green, "against" a red of the same lightness and strength** (OKLCH; `#1b6034` /
  `#85322f`; brighter in the dark theme, below), **with white words on them in both themes** (owner, 2026-11-18, the
  second request of the day; the first build used the dark theme's own link green, `#82d196`, with a dark word),
  wherever a side is a colour: the side buttons on a bill and in the agenda's rows, the chosen half of a switch, the big
  figures, the split bars, the seat map, the squares beside "už" and "prieš". Until now "for" was a yellower moss so that it did not read as
  Lietuva 2.0 taking a side (`design/ui`'s "Don't" list); the owner chose one green. As before, the word always goes
  with the colour: the pair differs by hue, not by lightness, as the moss and the dusty red did.
- **The dark theme has brighter versions of the pair, `#217740` and `#b4433f`, and no edge [decided by the owner on
  2026-11-18, later the same day].** The light pair is about 2:1 against the dark sheet and ground, under the 3:1 a
  graphic needs; it was first kept there with a light 1px edge round every shape in a side's colour (`--side-edge`).
  The owner chose brighter colours that meet 3:1 instead, and the edge is gone. The brighter pair keeps the hue and the
  white words (5.6:1 and 5.5:1) and is 3.0:1 against the dark sheet, 3.35:1 and 3.4:1 against the ground and 3.2:1
  against the quiet sheet, so the split bars' parts, the seat map's seats, the squares beside "už" and "prieš", the
  outline of the side not chosen (its own 2px outline in the side's colour) and the big figures stand on their own.
  Against the bars' dark track and the seat map's "did not vote" seats (`--absent`, `#38433d`, kept as it is) they are
  about 1.9:1. The hover tints of the side not chosen stay `#23462d` and `#59302d` (1.6:1 and 1.5:1 against the dark
  sheet, more than the light theme's tints on white, 1.2:1; the ink on them 9:1).
- **The threshold's progress** (`--progress`) is the same green, lighter, `#3a8e54` in both themes: 3.15:1 against the
  light track, 3.46:1 against the dark one. Reached, the ruler gives way to words (8.12.2).
- **The menu's chosen item**, the "Projektai" drop-down's chosen link and the chosen tab of "Projektai" are underlined
  in the links' green (they were the logo's leaf, under 2:1 on the ground). In the dark theme that is the dark theme's
  own link green, `#82d196`: the light theme's is 2.5:1 on the dark ground.
- **A bubble is a straight box with a small radius** (`--cut-s`, 0.5rem, the size of the portrait's cut) and a small
  tail on its top edge, 1rem from its left edge, pointing up to the name above it (8.16.1: the name first). An MP's
  response's bubble, mirrored until now, has its tail on the left too, where its name stands. The tail stays because
  the bubble is the logo's speech bubble: a person's words, under their name; without it the box would read as a card.
  The bubbles are the only rounded shapes.
- **The line right above a bubble is always its author's name [owner, 2026-11-18, later the same day: on an MP's
  response the tail pointed at "Balsuos už", on a statement at "Patvirtinta"].** Wherever a bubble stands (arguments,
  statements, MPs' responses, comments, the moderators' screens), the order is: the side or stance, if the item shows
  one, as a small line above the name ("■ Balsuos už", "Prašo balsuoti už"); then the name, with only "Nauja" beside
  it; then the bubble; then, under it, the marks and dates: an MP's faction, "Seimo narys arba jo biuras" and
  "Paskelbta …"; an organisation's "Patvirtinta" / "Nepatvirtinta" (its hint opens there as before), the link, the
  goal and the dates; a comment's "Organizacija" with the label, or "Seimo narys arba jo biuras", and its time. Nothing
  stands between the name and the bubble, and the name line holds nothing that could wrap onto a second line at 375 px
  except a long name itself. On a profile the bill heads each item and the name is the profile's own (an MP's
  response, an organisation's statement), not a link. A bubble with no name above it has no tail: the summaries'
  copies of arguments, which carry no author, and one's own hidden comment or statement, shown only to its writer. The designer's choices: the chips go under the bubble rather than beside the name, so that a long name or
  a phone's width never pushes one onto the line above the bubble; the eyebrow is the stance, since it is what the
  words say and reads first.
- **Buttons are straight [decided by the owner on 2026-11-18, settling 12-open].** Every button, filled or outlined, is
  a plain rectangle; its fill and outline share one shape. The cut corner stays only on the page's main sheet
  (`.sheet--cut`) and the portraits, where the logo's shape reads. The front page's step numbers ("Kaip tai veikia")
  lost their cut too: they are neither.
- **Figures side by side line up at the top [owner, 2026-11-18: on an MP's profile at 375 px the two figures stood on
  their bottoms, so a wrapped label pushed the other one down].** Wherever figures stand in a row (an MP's profile, a
  bill's "Ko prašo", a summary's copy, a vote's and a closed bill's totals, `/statistika`'s four figures) their labels
  share one line across, their numbers the next and the lines under them the next ones (a CSS subgrid of up to five
  lines, each as tall as the tallest in the row); a browser without subgrid aligns them at the top.
