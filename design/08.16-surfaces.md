# 8.16 Surfaces: what lies on a sheet and what on the ground

The stylesheet has three surfaces, each for one thing.

- **Ground** (`--ground`, the page itself) carries what frames the content: the page's title and lead, the way back,
  notices (`.note`), an introduction, a plain row of links, the footer.
- **Sheet** (`.sheet`, white) carries the content: what a person came to the page to read or to act on. A list, a
  table, the parts of a bill's page, a profile, a form, a panel of the person's own things, an item of a queue. One
  unit of content is one sheet; a sheet is never put inside a sheet.
- **Quiet sheet** (`.sheet--quiet`) is only ever inside a sheet: a part that opens within the content and steps back
  from it, such as the consent before a first side, a report form, a restriction form. It never stands by itself on
  the ground. A component used both inside and outside a sheet drops its own sheet inside one.
- **The test for a page:** its main content is on a sheet, and nothing lesser than the main content is on a sheet while
  the main content lies on the ground. Controls that only filter or sort a list lie on the ground directly above the
  list, and the list is on a sheet (or each of its items is), the same way on every list page.
- **Notices are not surfaces.** `.note` and `.note--quiet` keep their own colours wherever they stand.
- **One exception:** the start page's introduction for a visitor ("Kaip tai veikia", 8.7) stays on the ground: it
  frames the agenda, which is the page's content and is on a sheet.
- **The start page by this rule:** "Jūsų projektai" on a sheet, the agenda's two lists (the planned days, the voted
  days) each on a sheet of its own.
- **Pages of several parts** (a bill, an MP's profile, an organisation, a vote) give each part its own sheet; a text
  that is one document (the methodology, the moderation rules) is one sheet.
- **A control drawn in the sheet's own colour** (the "Man svarbu" mark) takes the tint when it stands on a sheet, so it
  keeps its shape.
- **Floating panels** (the feedback form, 8.10) are overlays above the page and not part of this.
- **Comments (8.23)** lie on their target's sheet, below the target, and are not a sheet themselves; their writing box
  is a quiet sheet inside it.
- **Colours of the three:** in the light theme the quiet sheet (`--sheet-2`) lies between the ground and the white
  sheet; in the dark theme it is a little lighter than the sheet, the ground the darkest.
- **Choices.** A choice between a bill's sides, or a statement's stance, is the joined-button control (`.switch` over
  real radio inputs, so it works without script, by keyboard and with the page's focus ring, and stays disabled while
  the island is locked): the side of an argument, an MP's response's stance ("už" in the side's green, "prieš" in its
  red, "Dar neapsisprendžiau" neutral, the second ink), an organisation's statement ("Pastabos" neutral) and the date
  flag ("Data neteisinga", "Balsuos anksčiau"). Three options stand one under another where their fieldset is narrower
  than 34rem (`.switch-field`, `.switch--stack`). Every other choice is a radio (how often the MP's summaries and the
  member's emails come, the feedback's kind), and consents and settings are plain checkboxes (every consent box, the
  email switches, "Viešas profilis", report grounds, the merge target).

## 8.16.1 The action row under a text

- **One row under every member's text, the same everywhere:** arguments, organisations' statements, MPs' responses and
  comments. Line 1: the author's name (a link with its card for a public profile, 8.25.4), for a comment its time; for a
  statement and a response the line with the dates. Line 2: one row that never wraps, from 360 px up and in a side's
  column on a wide screen: "Man svarbu · n" (arguments only; a toggle, filled when marked), "Aptarimas (n)" (8.23.6),
  "Sutinku" (comments only, 8.23.10) and "⋯" at the end. Every item is 44 px tall and they share one baseline; the name
  and the row stand on the text's own left edge (an MP's response, drawn mirrored, on its right edge), so nothing under
  a text is indented differently.
- **"⋯" ("Daugiau veiksmų") holds the rarer actions:** "Pranešti", the author's own "Atsiimti" (an argument) or
  "Ištrinti" (a comment), and for a moderator in a staff-passkey session "Moderuoti", which opens the moderator's actions
  for that text below the row: the filled main action and "Kiti veiksmai", the same popover menu as on every moderation
  screen (8.3, "The screen"). It is the browser's own popover, as a hint is (8.15): it opens without JavaScript (then in
  the middle of the screen), a press elsewhere or Escape closes it and the focus stays on or goes back to "⋯"; a small
  script (`lt2-menus.js`) places it beside "⋯", where it stays while the page scrolls (a menu longer than the room
  below goes on below the screen's edge, reached by scrolling). A chosen action closes the menu and its form opens below the whole row
  as a quiet sheet. A visitor's "Pranešti" note works without script; a member's items stay disabled until the island
  runs; the trigger itself is never disabled (it only shows the menu).
- **"Aptarimas" opens below the row at its full width:** the `<details>` holds only its summary, which stands in the row;
  the discussion is the panel right after it, shown only while the `<details>` is open, and laid below the whole row by
  the row's own layout (no `display: contents` on `<details>`, which browsers treat differently). The summary keeps its
  expanded state for assistive technology and names the panel it controls.
- **Words with small icons:** a heart beside "Man svarbu", a check in a circle beside "Sutinku", a speech bubble beside
  "Aptarimas", three dots for "⋯", drawn inline in the text's colour; no icon library and nothing fetched. "Man svarbu"
  always keeps its words (a term the icon alone would not say). One hint beside the arguments' heading, "Kas yra „Man
  svarbu“?" (8.15), says when it can be marked: either side's arguments, not one's own, counted only on the side one
  asks for; "Plačiau" to `/metodika#argumentai`. The same for everyone the server serves (a disabled button's title is
  shown by few browsers); the MP's own account, which gets no "Man svarbu" (8.5.4), loses it once the island runs.
- **The word "Aptarimas" shows at desktop widths, closed and open alike:** the arguments run at the page's full width
  below the bill's top part (8.12.1), so a side's column is wide enough; only where the row is narrower than 20rem (a
  360 px phone) is the word kept for screen readers alone, with the bubble and the count shown.
- **What opens stays where the person pressed.** "Aptarimas" opens in place under its own text, in that side's column;
  nothing else moves (8.23.6). A chosen action of "⋯" ("Pranešti", the author's "Atsiimti" or "Ištrinti", "Moderuoti")
  and a confirmation open directly under the row, **above** an open discussion, not after it.
- **The name first.** Arguments, statements, responses and comments all read: the name (with its card where there is
  one), then the text, then the row.
- **"Man svarbu" and "Sutinku" are toggles of one look.** For a member the button stays neutral; only its icon fills,
  in the brand's green, while it is marked, so the count stays readable; `aria-pressed` says the state; one's own
  argument keeps its disabled "Man svarbu" with the reason. "Sutinku" keeps its word in both states (no "Atsiimti
  „Sutinku“"), and the first one asks inline that the name will be shown (8.23.10). A visitor's "Man svarbu" is a link
  to sign in that comes back to the bill (6.9), of the same look, not a grey label that looks like a button.
- **The served page is the same for every member and visitor:** what is the caller's own (their "Atsiimti",
  "Ištrinti", "Sutinku", a moderator's "Moderuoti") comes from the island in the browser.

## 8.16.2 What changed between two versions

Every public version history shows what changed rather than whole texts side by side: a bill's description
(`/seimas/{n}/istorija`, 8.13), an organisation's statement (on its profile, 8.14.14), an MP's response ("Ankstesnės
versijos", 8.5.5) and the introduction letter (`/suvestines/pristatymas`, 8.19.5). An organisation's earlier names and
websites stay "A → B": they are short.

- **Newest first, each version against the one before it.** Every history, the names and websites included, lists the
  newest first; the oldest is shown whole. Where the text in force is shown above the history (a response, a statement,
  the letter), it also opens the list with its own changes, so the last edit is visible too: "dabartinė versija" (a
  response "dabartinė versija, parašyta {data}", the letter "Dabartinė versija, išsiųsta {data}"); a withdrawn response
  opens it with the withdrawal, "atsakymas atsiimtas {data}", its text struck through. A version whose text did not
  change (only the side or the subject did) is shown whole, unmarked.
- **Word by word, within the layout.** Both texts are laid out first (8.2, "A text's layout") and compared line by line:
  an unchanged paragraph or list item stays as it is; one line in place of one line, or two lines that share at least
  two fifths of their words, are compared word by word; any other line is removed or inserted whole, where it stood (so a
  moved paragraph is removed in one place and inserted in the other). The newer version's paragraphs and lists keep their
  layout, numbers as typed; a removed list item stays in its list, struck through. Words, runs of spaces and each
  punctuation mark are compared on their own, so nothing typed is lost; a space between two changes joins them.
- **How it looks.** Inserted words are underlined on a light slate ground, removed words struck through in the muted
  ink: neutral marks, never the red and green of a warning. Each mark begins with hidden "pridėta:" or "pašalinta:", so
  a screen reader says what it is. Under each changed version, "Rodyti tekstą prieš pakeitimą" (the one collapsing
  pattern, 8.15) holds the text before that change whole: the text after it is already on the page (the text in force,
  or the "before" of the next newer change), so the original stays reachable in full even when it was never stored as a
  version of its own. A version with no earlier text has no such disclosure. No script.
- **A limit.** The comparison does at most two million steps per pair of versions; past that (a very long paragraph
  changed at both ends) the changed part is shown as removed and inserted whole.

## 8.16.3 One green, the sides' pair and straight bubbles

`design/ui/lt2.css` holds the tokens; the site's stylesheet copies them.

- **"For" is the light theme's link green, "against" a red of the same lightness and strength** (OKLCH; `#1b6034` /
  `#85322f`; brighter in the dark theme, below), **with white words on them in both themes**, wherever a side is a
  colour: the side buttons on a bill and in the agenda's rows, the chosen half of a switch, the big figures, the split
  bars, the seat map, the squares beside "už" and "prieš". The word always goes with the colour: the pair differs by
  hue, not by lightness.
- **The dark theme has brighter versions of the pair, `#217740` and `#b4433f`, and no edge** round a shape in a side's
  colour. The brighter pair keeps the hue and the white words (5.6:1 and 5.5:1) and is 3.0:1 against the dark sheet,
  3.35:1 and 3.4:1 against the ground and 3.2:1 against the quiet sheet, so the split bars' parts, the seat map's seats,
  the squares beside "už" and "prieš", the outline of the side not chosen (its own 2px outline in the side's colour) and
  the big figures stand on their own. Against the bars' dark track and the seat map's "did not vote" seats (`--absent`,
  `#38433d`) they are about 1.9:1. The hover tints of the side not chosen are `#23462d` and `#59302d` (1.6:1 and 1.5:1
  against the dark sheet, more than the light theme's tints on white, 1.2:1; the ink on them 9:1).
- **The threshold's progress** (`--progress`) is the same green, lighter, `#3a8e54` in both themes: 3.15:1 against the
  light track, 3.46:1 against the dark one. Reached, the ruler gives way to words (8.12.2).
- **The menu's chosen item**, the "Projektai" drop-down's chosen link and the chosen tab of "Projektai" are underlined
  in the links' green (`--link`). In the dark theme that is the dark theme's own link green, `#82d196`: the light
  theme's is 2.5:1 on the dark ground.
- **A bubble is a straight box with a small radius** (`--cut-s`, 0.5rem, the size of the portrait's cut) and a small
  tail on its top edge, 1rem from its left edge, pointing up to the name above it (8.16.1: the name first). An MP's
  response's bubble has its tail on the left too, though its name and row stand on the right. The tail is there because the bubble is the
  logo's speech bubble: a person's words, under their name; without it the box would read as a card. The bubbles are
  the only rounded shapes.
- **The line right above a bubble is always its author's name.** Wherever a bubble stands (arguments, statements, MPs'
  responses, comments, the moderators' screens), the order is: the side or stance, if the item shows one, as a small
  line above the name ("■ Balsuos už", "Prašo balsuoti už"), since it is what the words say and reads first; then the
  name, with only "Nauja" beside it; then the bubble; then, under it, the marks and dates: an MP's faction, "Seimo narys
  arba jo biuras" and "Paskelbta …"; an organisation's "Patvirtinta" / "Nepatvirtinta" (its hint opens there), the
  link, the goal and the dates; a comment's "Organizacija" with the label, or "Seimo narys arba jo biuras", and its
  time. The marks go under the bubble so that a long name or a phone's width never pushes one onto the line above the
  bubble: nothing stands between the name and the bubble, and the name line holds nothing that could wrap onto a second
  line at 375 px except a long name itself. On a profile the bill heads each item and the name is the profile's own (an
  MP's response, an organisation's statement), not a link. A bubble with no name above it has no tail
  (`bubble--nameless`): the summaries' copies of arguments, which carry no author, and one's own hidden comment or
  statement, shown only to its writer.
- **Buttons are straight.** Every button, filled or outlined, is a plain rectangle; its fill and outline share one
  shape. The cut corner is only on the page's main sheet (`.sheet--cut`) and the portraits, where the logo's shape
  reads. The front page's step numbers ("Kaip tai veikia") have no cut either.
- **The compact button.** The follow control (8.11.2) is an outlined button about a link's height beside the small text
  round it: 0.875rem words, padding 0.15rem 0.6rem, a 1px outline (pressed or not; the word says the state), drawn
  24px tall (`.btn--compact`, `min-height: 1.5rem`), so the box alone meets WCAG 2.5.8's 24 × 24 px. An invisible area
  8px above and 12px below it makes the press target 44px tall without making it look bigger; it stays inside the gaps
  round it (8px between wrapped lines, 16px under the row), so it covers no other control. Other buttons keep their
  size.
- **Figures side by side line up at the top.** Wherever figures stand in a row (an MP's profile, a bill's "Ko prašo", a
  summary's copy, a vote's and a closed bill's totals, `/statistika`'s four figures) their labels share one line across,
  their numbers the next and the lines under them the next ones (a CSS subgrid of up to five lines, each as tall as the
  tallest in the row), so a wrapped label never pushes the other figure down; a browser without subgrid aligns them at
  the top.

## 8.16.4 Showing more of a long list

- **One "show more" everywhere** (`ShowMore`). Under a long list, on the same sheet, one line: "Rodoma N iš M" where the
  total is known, then the link "Rodyti daugiau"; further links of the same list (an older page: "Senesni balsavimai",
  "Tolesni projektai") follow on that line. Each is a text link in the site's normal link look, not a button's, so it
  does not clash with the buttons under a list ("Parašyti argumentą", the comment box). It is always a link to the same
  page with the list grown by the address, landing on the first row added, so it works without script; no hidden rows
  are in the page. A list of members' texts, each of which may have its own "Rodyti daugiau" (8.2), names what it adds:
  "Rodyti daugiau argumentų", "Rodyti daugiau komentarų", "Rodyti daugiau atsakymų".
- **How much a press adds.** The first page stays as each list states it (small, light for most readers, who never
  press); every further page is larger, since whoever presses wants more. A list of rows adds **50** a press: the
  agenda's and the front page's days (10 and 5 first), `/projektai` (20), the history of votes (30), a vote's ballots
  (20), an MP's votes (50), a member's texts (10), `/statistika`'s bills (10). A list of members' long texts adds **20**:
  the arguments (2 a side first), the comments (10), the MPs' responses (10). Where a page has a cap (300 votes, 200
  bills, 300 bills of the statistics, 500 of a member's texts) the last press before it adds what is left up to the
  cap. One place in the code holds both numbers (`ListPaging.RowStep`, `ListPaging.TextStep`); they are the design's,
  not settings. A link to one comment shows its discussion through it on the thread's own pages (10, 30, 50, …; 8.23.6).
- **How it grows.** On a server-rendered page the site's enhanced navigation fetches the link and swaps the page in
  without a reload (a normal load without script). In an island (the arguments, the comments), once the island runs, the
  island reads the next page from the API (`limit` and `cursor`, as the API's other lists), adds it in place and moves the
  focus to the first item added; the link keeps pointing to the grown address. Before the island runs it is the plain
  link. A running island never lets enhanced navigation fetch its own list (`data-enhance-nav="false"`): the island
  would keep what it shows and nothing would change.
- **What is paged by the server.** A list that may be long comes from the API a page at a time and the page holds only
  what it shows: the arguments (each side, 8.12.1), the comments (8.23.6), `/projektai`, the history of votes, an MP's
  votes, a member's texts, the statistics' bills. A list bounded by the parliament (a bill's MPs' responses, a vote's
  141 ballots, an MP's answers) is read whole by the server, which still renders only the page asked for. The agenda's
  and the front page's days (8.7, 8.11.1) are read whole too and grow by this "show more" as well: "Rodoma 10 iš 40 ·
  Rodyti daugiau" under a longer day, 50 more a press, by the address (`?diena=2026-11-24:60`).

## 8.16.5 A list's heading stays in view

- **Every public list's heading row stays at the top of the screen** (owner, 2026-11-21) while its own list scrolls
  past, at every width, phones included: the list's heading and the link beside it, where it has one (the list's long
  form: "Visa darbotvarkė →", "Ankstesni balsavimai →"). It stays within its sheet and leaves with it, so the next
  list's heading takes its place. It has the sheet's colour, so the rows pass under it, and a thin rule (`--rule`) under
  it. The site's header does not stay. CSS only (`position: sticky`, `.list-head`), no script, so it works on
  server-rendered pages without JavaScript.
- **On a list page whose list's sheet has no heading of its own, the page's head stays instead** (owner, 2026-11-21):
  the page's title and the tab row right under it ("Darbotvarkė · Projektai · Balsavimai · Suvestinės"), on the ground's
  colour with the same rule under it, while the page's content scrolls past (`.page-head`). The label above the title,
  the way back, the lead, the list's own tabs, the search and the filters scroll away as they are; the count line in the
  sheet ("Rasta projektų: 412") is an ordinary line. On a narrow screen (under 54rem) the title in that head is the
  smaller heading size, so the head stays compact: about 105px on a phone with the tab row, 46px without.
- **At rest everything stands where it would:** a row or a head takes its padding out of the room round it.
- **A day's date stays just under the row or the head** from 54rem (the timeline's dates stand beside the rows there),
  on the agenda, the front page, the history of votes and an MP's votes; under 54rem a day's head is a band that
  scrolls with its rows (8.11.1). Each kind has its height (`--list-head` for a heading row or a smaller heading's row,
  `--page-head` for a page's head, with or without the tab row), so the dates meet it whichever it is.
- **A link that lands in the list stops below the row or the head:** a grown list's first added row
  (`#agenda-2026-11-24-…`, `#recent-…`, `#bill-…`, `#balsavimas-51`, a response, a ballot), an older page's first row
  and a day's own address, with room on a narrow screen for a heading that takes a second line and the link under it.
- **What has a heading row:** the front page's two lists (8.7) and a member's "Jūsų projektai" (its failed state too);
  the agenda page's two (8.11.1); `/seimo-nariai`'s groups; an MP's recent votes (7.3) and responses (8.5.6); a
  faction's positions and members, a committee's members and the former members (7.3); a vote's factions and ballots
  (8.17); a bill's votes, MPs' responses (8.5.6) and summaries; a bill's history of edits (8.13); an organisation's
  statements and history (8.14.14); a member's texts on a public profile (8.25.2); the statistics' faction unity and
  "Kas Seime balsuoja taip, kaip prašote jūs" (8.26).
- **What has a sticky page head:** `/projektai` (8.12.5) and the history of votes (8.18), with the tab row; an MP's
  votes, an MP's bills (8.5.7) and `/organizacijos`, the title alone.
- **What does not stay:** a page's head where its lists have headings of their own (`/darbotvarke`, the front page, a
  profile); the lead, the tabs of a list and the filters above a sheet; prose and a single record's details (a bill's
  "Apie ką projektas", its sides, the Seimas's result and its stages; an MP's record, mandate, roles and contacts; an
  organisation's "about" and the blocked notice; a vote's result and seat map; a summary's bill); forms; the bill
  page's two side columns (the arguments, the organisations' statements); lists on the ground (the bodies on
  `/seimo-nariai`, the earlier summaries); the statistics' comparison of bills, whose heading and filters stand on the
  ground above its sheet on a page of several parts; staff and account screens.
