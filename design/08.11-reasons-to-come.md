# 8.11 Reasons to come before an appeal exists

Everything in 8.1–8.7 has value only once other people are there. This section gives the site a use for one person
alone. In one line, for the texts: find out what the Seimas will vote on, tell every MP what you ask for, and see how
they voted. Every active bill is open (8.12).

## 8.11.1 The agenda page

A public page, `/darbotvarke` ("Seimo darbotvarkė"), server-rendered, readable without JavaScript: the first tab,
"Darbotvarkė", of the menu item "Projektai", which leads here.

- **Coming up** ("Ką Seimas planuoja svarstyti"). Every current planned item (5.1 `planned_item`, a moderator's row
  first) from today on, grouped by day, earliest first. Per bill: number, title, stage, both sides, one number each (no
  count in brackets: the bill's page carries the counts for accounts older than 30 days), the progress to the threshold
  (once it is reached, the day of the summary planned or last sent, else "riba pasiekta": 8.12.2, "The rows of a list"),
  and a link to the bill's page (its title); a bill
  nobody took part in shows the sides to take instead of saying so (below, "The rows"). A bill whose record is hidden
  says "Dalyvavimas uždarytas" with a link to it.
- **Recently voted** ("Dėl ko Seimas balsavo per pastarąsias 7 dienas"). The stage results of the last seven days from
  open data: bill, stage, day, the result (or "bendru sutarimu") with the majority of those who voted (8.18, "The
  majority"), linking to the vote page; beside it the bill's counts. Beside its heading "Ankstesni balsavimai →" leads
  to the history of votes (8.18).
- **Two sheets.** Each list is a sheet of its own; its heading row (the heading, and for the recent votes the link beside
  it) stays at the top of the screen while its sheet scrolls past (8.16.5). The coming list's heading has no link: this
  page is the whole agenda.
- **Days and their length.**
  - **The order within a day.** The agenda gives no time or order within a day, so a day's bills go by activity
    (participants on both sides together plus published arguments, the plain sum of `/projektai`, 8.12.5), the most
    first; then by how far the bill may go that day: priėmimas, svarstymas, pateikimas, none (a combined stage such as
    "Pateikimas, svarstymas, priėmimas" counts as its furthest, and the row's stage tag shows it so); then by number.
    A voted day's votes go the same way, by the vote's stage (adoption first). Nothing else ranks a bill: not how many
    follow it (8.11.2), and not whether the reader follows it, since the server's page is the same for everyone ("Jūsų
    projektai" on the front page is where a member's own bills are). The rule is one pure function (`AgendaRules`).
  - **Bills the open data does not know yet** go after every known bill of their day, folded into one line, "Dar 12
    projektų, kurių atviruosiuose Seimo duomenyse dar nėra", that opens without script to list each with its number,
    stage and a link to the agenda source (the browser's own `details`, 8.15). Such a bill (for example one presented
    for the first time) has no title and no page here. Nobody can take part on it or follow it until the import knows
    it.
  - **Each day is a block of its own** (the timeline of 8.7): its head says "Planuojama" or "Balsuota", the date in
    words, the weekday and how many the day holds ("antradienis · 40 projektų", "ketvirtadienis · 26 balsavimai"),
    beside the rows on a wide screen and as a band of its own above them on a narrow one, so the next day's rows are
    never read as this day's.
  - **Ten rows a day, 50 more a press** (`AgendaRules.PagePerDay`, `MorePerDay`; 8.16.4). The page holds only the rows
    it shows. Under a longer day stands the shared line "Rodoma 10 iš 40 · Rodyti daugiau": the link leads to the same
    page with that day grown by 50, `/darbotvarke?diena=2026-11-24:60` (the day, a colon and
    how many of its known rows to show; a voted day `?balsuota=2026-11-17:60`), as the history of votes grows (8.18). The days already grown stay so (the parameter repeats); a value the page
    would not make (a bare day, a word, a number not above the day's first rows) is left out, never an error, and a
    number beyond the day shows the day whole. Without script the server renders the page with the day grown; with
    script the site's enhanced navigation fetches it and swaps it in, without a reload, the members' islands of the
    rows added starting in place. **"Rodoma N iš M"**: M is the day's head count, the bills the open data does not know
    yet included; those are folded into one line after the day's last known row, shown once every known row shows, and
    from then counted as shown, so the last page reads "Rodoma 40 iš 40" and has no link. A day whose rows all fit its
    first page has no such line (its unknown bills, if any, show at once). Each day's block has an address of its own,
    `#day-2026-11-24` (a voted day `#voted-day-…`). The front page's days grow the same way in place, from five (8.7).
  - **The rows are the front page's (8.7), one component** (`TimelineItem`, `VotedItem`). A coming bill with an open
    record offers "Prašau balsuoti" with "už" and "prieš" in its row, as on the front page ("Taking a side in the
    row"): a visitor's are links to sign in that come back to the bill's page; a member's side column and the mark under
    the title are islands filled in the browser from the member's own answer (taking, changing and taking back a side,
    the positions consent asked in the row), disabled until they start and, without JavaScript, beside the "needs
    JavaScript" note. The title is the way to the bill's details: no other link in the row, and beside the buttons no
    "Niekas nenurodė, ko prašo". A voted row keeps "Kaip balsavo Seimo nariai" and, for a member, the mark under its
    title: "Sekate", their own side and, on a followed bill with something new, what is new as a small link to the
    bill, the same line as "Jūsų projektai" (8.11.8). A first side taken in a row follows the bill and the mark's
    "Sekate" lights up, with no line saying so. The page asks `/me/bills` about the rows it shows (ten a day; each
    "Rodyti daugiau" adds its rows), thirty bills a request, side by side; the front page's "Jūsų projektai" and
    greeting are not on this page. The page's HTML is the same for every member and holds nothing of a member's own
    (8.7, "Privacy").
  - **Cached.** The page reads the front page's copy of the agenda, kept a minute (8.7, "Cached"), which a side taken,
    changed or withdrawn marks out of date for both pages; `GET /api/v1/agenda` and the bill's page stay live.
- Dates are "Planuojama …", never a countdown. Under the heading the page says "Darbotvarkių projektai dažnai keičiasi,
  todėl datos gali pasislinkti.", when the agenda was last read from lrs.lt and the link "Darbotvarkė lrs.lt". Without
  any planned item it says so and still shows the recent votes. A bill's own page says where its date comes from beside
  it, with a hint and the same agenda link (8.4).
- **Nothing stands under the lists:** no note on the numbers, no hint ("Kas yra riba?", the majority) and no "not a
  representative poll" notice (owner, 2026-11-21). The bill's page, the summaries, the statistics page and the
  methodology say what the numbers are and are not.
- One public, cacheable answer feeds it (`GET /api/v1/agenda`), composed by the host from the Parliament and Appeals
  modules' interfaces (and, per bill, the organisations with a public statement, and for a reached bill the summary's day
  from the Notifications module, read once for all the rows); it holds nothing about the caller.

**The bill page** (`/seimas/{number}`) is the one page of 8.12.1, with the planned date and the "Sekti" control
(8.11.2).

**The front page** (8.7) shows, under the welcome, two sheets: "Artimiausiu metu Seime", with "Visa darbotvarkė →"
beside its heading, the next two sitting days with five bills of each in the agenda's order and "Rodoma 5 iš 40 · Rodyti
daugiau" adding 50 of a day in place (8.16.4); then "Neseniai balsuota", with "Ankstesni balsavimai →" beside its
heading, the last two voting days the same way. `/projektai` is the list of bills by activity.

## 8.11.2 Following a bill or an MP

A member can follow a bill and an MP ("Sekti" / "Nebesekti"), from the bill page and the MP's profile (organisations,
8.14.14, and members, 8.25.5, the same way). A visitor sees a link to sign in instead.

- **What a follower gets**, by email through the outbox, each kind switchable on the account page (on at first), in the
  person's rhythm (8.24):
  - **planned:** a followed bill has a planned date: number, title, stage, "Planuojama …", that dates move, and a link
    to the bill;
  - **voted:** the Seimas held a stage vote on a followed bill: stage, day, the result with the official note if any,
    a link to the vote page, and how each MP the person follows voted (or did not take part).
- **Rules** (pure functions with unit tests, `FollowRules`):
  - an event is a bill with a current planned date (key: the date) or a stage-result vote (key: the vote); it is
    recorded once, when the job first sees it;
  - it is sent to members who followed the bill before it was first seen; a planned date already in the past and a
    vote held more than three days ago are recorded and never sent (so a backfill sends nothing);
  - a vote is sent only to those who followed the bill before the vote was held as well as before it was first seen
    (an import runs some minutes after a vote, and someone who follows in between would otherwise be told about a vote
    from before they followed); a planned date's moment is when it is first seen;
  - at most one "planned" email per bill per Vilnius day under "Iš karto": a date that moves again the same day is
    sent the next day, with the date current then;
  - an event is in one email per person only (8.24 gathers a run's or a period's events); nothing about the person's
    side, the bill's counts or other people is in it.
- **Taking a side follows the bill.** There is one notion: a bill a person follows is theirs, on their list, and they
  hear about it. Taking a side is one way to start following it.
  - When a member takes a side on a bill they do not follow, the bill is followed for them in the same action, on the
    bill's page and in a row of the front page or the agenda alike (`TakingSides`). The page shows it at once: the
    button reads "Nebesekti" and the row carries "Sekate", and nothing else says so. Changing the side changes nothing
    about the follow.
  - "Sekti" is for following without a side. "Nebesekti" stops the emails and keeps the side: the two are undone
    separately, and withdrawing a side does not unfollow (the person may still want to hear; one press unfollows).
  - The side is never refused or delayed because of the follow: if following fails, the side is saved and the bill is
    simply not followed.
  - What they then get is the two emails above under the same rules and the same switches: from that moment on, never
    about an event from before. This delivers, for a person with a side, the "planned" and "voted" notices of 8.6
    without a second mechanism, so a person who both follows a bill and took a side on it gets one email per event,
    not two.
  - An MP's first response follows the bill the same way for the account that wrote it (8.5.7): the MP's account and
    office members take no side, so this is how an answered bill becomes theirs.
  - "Jūsų projektai" (8.7) lists the bills the member follows, and only those: "Nebesekti" takes the bill off the list,
    and its side stays, shown in the bill's own row of the timeline and on its page.
- **The account page** ("Sekimas ir laiškai", `/paskyra/sekimas`) lists the followed bills (planned date or last stage
  result), MPs, organisations and members, with "Nebesekti", and the switches. Each email's footer links to the same
  screen's choice of how often and to the stop page (8.24.1).
- **Links in emails** are full addresses built from `Site:Address` (5.6). Every email to a member (codes, account and
  moderation notices, the follow and gathered emails, the office invitation, the take-over link) keeps its plain-text
  part as "text: address" and, in its HTML part, shows a line that ends "text: https://…" as that text (its last
  sentence) as a link, bold in the site's green, and nothing more; every link in the footer, the privacy notice's
  included, is the footer's grey and underlined, so it reads grey in every mail program, the MP footer's too; any other
  address in a line is a plain link. One helper renders it (`EmailLayout`), and a template writes such a line with
  `EmailLayout.LinkLine`. The links are the addresses themselves: nothing is rewritten, shortened or tracked. MPs'
  summary emails (8.19.3) keep their own shape.
- **Who follows is private.** Who follows what is shown to nobody: no page, API answer, email, admin screen or log says
  it, to the followed party (an organisation's representatives, an MP's linked account and office, a member with a
  public profile) and to staff alike; there is no list of followers and no data about one follower in any answer. A
  follow holds nothing about a side, also when taking a side made it, and follows are never combined with sides or
  invitations on any screen or in any answer about other people (the person's own answer, `/me/bills`, gives them
  both); they are in the person's export and go with the account. **There is no limit on how much a person follows.**
  A plain request limit guards against a runaway script, which no person meets and which is not a rule of the product:
  taking, changing or withdrawing a side, following and unfollowing, "Man svarbu" and changing the display name share
  one limit of 60 requests a minute per account (`RateLimits:Participation`, 6.4).
- **How many follow is shown.** Every thing that can be followed shows how many members follow it: a bill, an MP, an
  organisation (8.14.14) and a member with a public profile (8.25.5), as one plain line, "Sekėjai: 12". The number is
  the count of that thing's follows and nothing else: it names nobody and says nothing about any follower's side, and
  everyone, the followed party and staff included, sees the same number and nothing more. Following is the ground a
  delegation would stand on if one is designed (it is not, 1.1), and a number of people choosing someone is what a
  delegation would show anyway. This
  is the one place the rule is written; the other sections refer here.
  - **Counts, not rankings.** The number stands only on the thing's own page: the bill's page (beside "Sekti", apart
    from the participants' figures), the MP's profile, the organisation's profile, the member's page. It is not in list
    rows (the agenda, the front page, `/projektai`, the MPs' list, `/organizacijos`) or a member's card (8.25.4);
    nothing is sorted, ranked or filtered by it; there is no "most followed" list of bills, MPs, members or
    organisations; and no number is added up (no total per faction, party, committee or kind of organisation). Reason:
    a number on a profile says how many chose to hear about someone; a table of MPs ordered by it is a league table,
    which the tone of connection and explanation, not control (`AGENTS.md`), and 7.1 guard against.
  - **Zero.** With no followers nothing is shown rather than "Sekėjai: 0", so a new profile, an MP nobody has followed
    yet or a quiet bill does not open with a verdict.
  - **Exact from 1.** The party group rule (`Digest:PartyGroupMinimum`, 8.2, 6.3, 8.4) is for group numbers that can
    reveal a side, such as a party's supporters on one side of a bill. A follower number is about the followed thing,
    names nobody and tells no side, so that rule does not reach it. (This is the one place the argument is made; 8.25.5
    refers here.)
  - **Beside a bill's participants.** Taking a first side follows the bill (above), but neither number bounds the
    other: a member may follow without a side, keep the follow after withdrawing the side, or unfollow and keep the
    side; and the MP's own account follows but takes no side (8.5.4). So a bill can have more followers than
    participants, or fewer. The number stands apart from the figures (`RecordFigures` and the split bar are about
    sides), so it is not read as a third side or a total, and the methodology explains the two side by side:
    participants took a side and are counted per side; followers asked for emails about the bill, whatever their side
    or none.
  - **What the number is called.** Like every number on the site it counts self-selected "Lietuva 2.0 naudotojai", not
    the public or voters, and it is not support: people follow those they disagree with, to watch them. The
    methodology's part on following (`/metodika#following`, in "Kas matoma viešai, o kas ne") says so, with "tai nėra
    reprezentatyvi apklausa".
  - **One compact line.** On every page that has "Sekti" (a bill, an MP, an organisation, a member) the follow is one
    line under the title: the page's own tags first (a bill's stage and "Planuojama …", an MP's faction and committees,
    an organisation's label), then "Sekti" (for a visitor a link to sign in that comes back to the page), then
    "Sekėjai: n", then one hint, "Kas yra sekimas?". The hint says, per kind, what following sends, and what the number
    is ("Kas ką seka, niekas kitas nemato. „Sekėjai“ – tiek Lietuva 2.0 narių paprašė šių laiškų; tai ne palaikymas ir
    ne reprezentatyvi apklausa."), with "Plačiau" to `/metodika#following`; so the number beside "Sekti" carries no
    second hint. Where the number stands without the button (a closed organisation, the member's own page) it keeps
    "Kas yra sekėjai?". The state stays on the button ("Nebesekti", `aria-pressed`); the person's own "Sekti" moves the
    number at once.
  - **The compact button.** "Sekti", "Nebesekti" and a visitor's sign-in link are drawn as a compact outlined button
    about a link's height, staying on the row with "Sekėjai: n" and "Kas yra sekimas?" (8.16.3, "The compact button"),
    everywhere the control stands: a bill, an MP, an organisation, a member's page, and "Nebesekti" in the account's
    list of follows. The row stands one step (16px) above the page's first part (8.12.1).
  - **Data and API.** No table of its own: the number is counted from `notify.follow` (by `kind` and `key`) when the
    answer is made. Notifications owns follows and gives the host a number only, through `IFollowerCounts` in the shared
    kernel; the answers that carry it are `GET /bills/{number}/followers` (a small public answer of its own, so the
    bill's record answer stays about sides), `GET /mps/{slug}` (`followers`), `GET /organisations/{slug}` (`followers`)
    and `GET /members/{id}` (`followers`). A cached answer may be behind by its cache's life, as the other public numbers
    are. `follow` is withheld from `lt2_readonly`: the read-only role reads no follow and no number.
  - **Not a delegation.** A follow is a request for emails; it never turns into a delegation by itself (1.1).
- It lives in the Notifications module (user notification settings, section 2): tables `follow` and `bill_event` (5.4).
  The worker's follow job runs four times an hour (8.24), after the import and the agenda watcher.

## 8.11.3 Asking for an appeal, and appeals opened by moderators

There is nothing to ask for or to open: every active bill is open (8.12.4). A moderator writes or corrects the
description of any active bill, with the AI draft in that form (8.11.4, 8.12.3); the moderator who writes it also saves
it, and no second moderator approves it.

## 8.11.4 The AI draft of a description

Only in the moderator's form "write or correct the description" on any active bill (8.12.3). It drafts; a person decides.

- The moderator pastes the relevant part of the bill's explanatory note (aiškinamasis raštas) into "Šaltinio tekstas"
  (up to 20 000 characters; the e-seimas link is beside the field) and presses "Parengti juodraštį". The draft fills the
  description field, which the moderator edits and saves with a statement. Without a source text there is no draft: the
  assistant never writes from the title alone.
- What is sent to the provider: the bill's number and title and the pasted official text. No personal data; the form
  says to paste official text only.
- The prompt is a file in the repository and is public. It asks for two to four plain Lithuanian sentences on what the
  text says the bill changes: no evaluation, no arguments for or against, nothing that is not in the source, and to say
  so when the source is not enough.
- A description that started from a draft says "Sukurta DI · taisė žmonės" beside it for its whole life, also after a
  moderator corrects it. `bill_record.description_source` holds `moderator`, `ai-draft` or `ai` (the summary
  job's text nobody has read, 8.13) (5.3).
- Asking for a draft is audited (who, which bill, when; not the text), at most 20 an hour per moderator.
- **The provider: the Claude API (Anthropic, first-party),** for the draft and for the summary job (8.13), which sends
  the same request through the same assistant, with the official text from `IBillTextSource` as the user message; the
  worker is configured the same way as the web host. The model is the runtime setting `Ai:DraftModel` (default
  `claude-sonnet-5-5`, 5.6). The request sends only the number, the title and the pasted text (the public prompt is the
  system prompt, the source the user message, whole; over 20 000 characters is a validation error in the form, never a
  silent cut); effort `low`, no thinking setting, no sampling parameters, no forced tool choice, no prefill, and the
  server-side refusal fallback (`fallbacks: "default"`) on. A refusal or a cut-off answer is a failure the moderator is
  told about ("juodraščio parengti nepavyko"), never half a text; provider errors become the same plain refusal and a
  log line without the text or the key. It is configured by `Ai:Provider` = `claude` and a key from a secret file
  (`deploy/secrets/ai_api_key`); without both, `IAiAssistant` is the "not configured" one: the button is not shown,
  nothing in the product mentions a draft, and everything else in this section works. `api.anthropic.com` is added to
  the outbound allowlist only when configured. Tests use a fake; nothing in the tests calls the real API.
  Pre-screening of users' texts stays rule-based: those texts can hold personal data, and this API has no EU-only
  processing (inference is global or US-only, storage in the US); AI pre-screening would need an EU-region route first.
- Every draft request is a row in `appeals.ai_draft_request` (moderator, bill, time; never a text) besides the audit
  entry, so a moderator's claim that a description started from a draft is checkable; it serves the hourly limit, and a
  description is saved as `ai-draft` only when the same moderator asked for a draft on that bill within the last 24
  hours (otherwise `draft.none`). The rows go with the moderator's account and are in their export.

Not AI, on purpose: reasons for either side. They stay people's own (8.2); that is what the numbers and texts sent to
MPs stand on.

## 8.11.5 Sharing towards the threshold

- On an open bill a member sees, with the threshold right below the side buttons: "Iki ribos trūksta N dalyvių" (nothing
  once the threshold is reached: the threshold's own line says so, 8.12.2), their personal invitation link that lands
  on the bill's page, and "Kopijuoti nuorodą". This is the only place the share line stands.
- The link is the bill's own address with the member's personal invitation code (6.2) after the `#`:
  `https://…/seimas/XVP-0412#kodas=…`. So a link preview (8.22.5) shows the bill, and the code is never in a path or a
  query (6.8). Whatever page such a link opens, the browser takes the code into the tab's session storage under the one
  key the inventory lists for it (`lt2.invitation-code`, 8.22.1) and removes it from the address bar at once, so the address
  copied from there carries no code. The page reads as usual without script; carrying the code needs script. A sign-in
  link on the page ("Prašau balsuoti už", "Sekti", "Man svarbu" …, 6.9: `/prisijungti#tikslas=/seimas/XVP-0412`) then
  opens the registration with the code filled in and comes back to the bill. The code is forgotten when the
  registration is completed or the person signs in to a finished account. An organisation's links per bill (8.14.14)
  are made the same way with the organisation's code. Query forms (`?kodas=`, `?tikslas=`) are not read.
- After registering or signing in from a sign-in link that names a page (`tikslas`), the person lands on that page
  instead of the front page. Signing in, registering, starting a staff session and accepting the terms land on the
  front page `/` when there is no page to return to (`tikslas`, a page's own return, the staff page's `#grizti=`); the
  MP's take-over lands on "Dalyvavimas" (`/paskyra/dalyvavimas`, 8.21), where the MP's part is. A registration just
  completed (the name, age and terms step) without a page to return to lands on "Profilis" (`/paskyra#po-registracijos`),
  to fill it out; there the one button reads "Išsaugoti ir tęsti" and leads on to the front page (8.28.5).
- `tikslas` is accepted only as a path on this site (one leading slash, no scheme, host, backslash, dot segment, query
  or fragment, at most 200 characters) under `/seimas/`, `/paskyra` and the account screens under it (a visitor sent
  to sign in from one comes back to it), a member's page `/nariai/{name-slug}-{id}` (its "Sekti" for a visitor) and
  `/statistika` (8.26.4); anything else is ignored (`LandingPath`). It stays in the browser through the sign-in steps
  and is never stored on the server, so nothing ties an invitation to a bill. The share link is the same whichever side
  the sharer took, and nothing about a side is in it (6.2: invitations are never combined with sides).
- A member whose code is switched off or used up gets the plain address of the bill to share, with one line saying the
  code does not work now.
- The line needs script anyway (copying), so it is filled in once the island runs in the browser, not while the server
  prerenders the page.

## 8.11.6 Left for later

- **Not built.** Reading explanatory notes from e-seimas automatically: its pages have no API and their structure must
  first be confirmed against live responses (7.1). The summary job of 8.13 makes nothing until it can.
- **Not built.** Summaries of official positions with their source named (the proposers' reasons, the Legal
  Department's and the committee's remarks): they need the same documents.
- **Not built.** A weekly email about followed MPs.

## 8.11.7 Done when

- A visitor with no account sees what the Seimas plans to vote on and what it voted on last week, at 1280 px and
  390 px, without JavaScript.
- A member alone on the site follows a bill and gets an email when it is planned and when it is voted, and none about
  events from before they followed.
- No page, API answer, log line or email shows who follows what.
- Following and unfollowing move a follower number by one, and it is the same for everyone.
- The share link is the bill's own address with the code after the `#`; a new member who opens it and signs in from
  the page registers with the code filled in and lands on the bill's page; the code has left the address bar; a
  `tikslas` pointing off the site is ignored.

## 8.11.8 What is new since the last visit

What has been written on the bills a member follows since they last opened each bill's page.

- **What is kept.** On a member's follow of a bill (`notify.follow`, kind `bill`) one moment, `last_seen_at`: when they
  last opened that bill's page, starting from the follow's own time. Nothing for bills not followed, nothing about any
  other page, never combined with sides. It goes with the follow (unfollowing removes it; following again starts from
  the new follow's time), is in the export and is deleted with the account; `follow` is withheld from `lt2_readonly`.
  The privacy notice says it under "„Man svarbu" žymos ir sekimai".
- **A visit.** Once the bill's page runs in the browser, a signed-in member's island (`WhatsNew`) sends one write,
  `POST /api/v1/me/bills/{number}/seen`: on a followed bill the server keeps now (never earlier than the moment kept,
  `FollowRules.Seen`) and answers the moment before and the texts written after it, by id; on a bill not followed it
  keeps nothing and answers nothing. Never a GET, never while the server prerenders, nothing in the served HTML;
  antiforgery and the terms gate as for every write; under the per-account limit of a page's own answers (`RateLimits`
  `own-read`). The answer is shared by the page's islands (`MyBillState.News`, `NewSince`).
- **What is new** (`NewsRules`, Appeals, through `IBillNews` in the shared kernel; the host composes it with the moments
  from Notifications): a text the bill's page shows that was made public, or changed, after the moment: a published
  argument (made public: `first_public_at`, or edited by its author: `edited_at`), an organisation's statement shown on
  the page (made public or edited), a published MP's response (published or edited), a published comment under a shown
  text (written or edited). Never one on a bill whose record is hidden, never a hidden one, and never the member's own:
  what they wrote, the statements and comments of an organisation they represent, the responses and comments of the MP
  they speak for.
- **"Nauja".** A small tag beside each new argument, comment, statement and response (`NewMark`). The responses' part is
  server-rendered and the same for everyone, so each response carries a small island of its own for its tag; the other
  parts are islands and render it themselves.
- **"Rodyti tik naujus".** When anything is new, a plain checkbox on the ground above the texts (8.16: a filter, not a
  choice between sides). On: only the new texts and those whose discussion has new comments stand (an argument beyond
  the first two of its side included), and those discussions are rendered open with their comments through the last new
  one, as a link to a comment opens its discussion (8.23.6). Off: everything. Kept only on the page being read (no
  browser storage, 8.22.1), and no reload. The server-rendered responses are hidden by the stylesheet from a marker
  their island leaves (`.new-out`), and the part goes when none stands.
- **The lists.** In "Jūsų projektai" on the front page (`GET /me/bills`) and in the follow list on "Sekimas ir laiškai"
  (`GET /me/follows`) a followed bill with something new says what, for example "3 nauji argumentai · 5 nauji
  komentarai", then organisations' statements and responses when any, each in its Lithuanian number form (1 naujas
  argumentas, 2–9 nauji argumentai, 10 and 11–19 naujų argumentų; "1 nauja organizacijos pozicija", "1 naujas
  atsakymas"). Counted in one read per list, whatever its length. The member's mark under a title in the timeline's rows
  (the front page and `/darbotvarke`, 8.7, 8.11.1) says the same beside "Sekate" and their side, as a small link to the
  bill's page, from the same `/me/bills` answer the rows already read (no request of its own); filled in the browser
  only. A bill in "Jūsų projektai" and in the timeline shows it in both.
- **Wording.** "Nauja", "Rodyti tik naujus" and the counts are about texts that really are new to that person, which the
  rule against wording the product as new (`AGENTS.md`) allows.
