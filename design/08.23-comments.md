# 8.23 Comments: "Aptarimas" under arguments, statements and responses

Members comment in public under three things on a bill's page: an argument, an organisation's statement and an MP's
response. One implementation serves all three, with a runtime switch per kind. Nothing is moderated beforehand and
nothing reaches the moderators' list unless reported; every comment can be reported, and a moderator moderates a comment
on the bill's page too. Links in a comment are allowed and shown as links, with no card. "Sutinku" stands beside every
comment, public with the names of those who agreed. A comment's length is the general cap on members' texts.

Comments answer a gap: a text standing alone on a bill's page invites a reply that has nowhere to go. Their moderation
cost is kept small in four ways: comments wait for nobody and reach the list only when reported, pre-screening refuses
personal data instead of holding it (8.23.3), there are no replies to replies and "Sutinku" carries no text to moderate
(8.23.1, 8.23.10), and each kind can be closed to new comments at once (8.23.5).

## 8.23.1 What a comment is

- **Names.** The part under a text is **"Aptarimas"**, one item in it a **komentaras**. Never "atsakymas": that word is
  an MP's response (8.5.5).
- **A remark on one text.** A comment stands under one target: an argument (`reason`), an organisation's statement
  (`statement`) or an MP's response (`response`). Nothing else takes comments: not the bill, its description, an
  organisation, a profile, a vote, a summary or another comment.
- **One level, oldest first.** No replies to a comment; a comment may name whom it answers in its text. Never sorted by
  popularity: "Sutinku" changes no order (8.23.10), because a remark is read in the order the discussion went and an
  order by agreement would make the comment section a second contest beside the users' counts.
- **"Sutinku" beside every comment:** a button that lets a person show support without writing another comment, public
  with their name. Its rules are 8.23.10.
- **Never counted.** No count, threshold, the activity sort of `/projektai` (8.12.5), list, statistic, agenda answer,
  front page, summary or public copy changes with comments or their "Sutinku". Comments never go to MPs: not in a
  summary, its preview, its public copy, the MP's email or `/seimo-narys/projektai`. Two numbers only: how many comments
  a text shows, in "Aptarimas (n)", and how many agreed with a comment, beside it with their names (8.23.10). 8.14.3
  keeps statements free of a count of their own, so that no second popularity number stands beside the users' counts;
  the number of comments counts remarks, which disagree as often as they agree, and the number beside a comment is about
  that remark, not about the text above it.
- **Not a side.** A comment carries no side. Nothing public ties it to its author's side, and writing one needs no
  positions consent: like an argument, it is the author's own deliberate publication, public under their name (8.2).
  So is "Sutinku" (8.23.10).

## 8.23.2 Who writes, and in whose name

- **Any member** who has accepted the terms (8.22.2) and has no writing restriction (8.3), under their display name, as
  beside an argument; for a member with a public profile the name is a link to it, with its card and an initials badge (8.25.4). A visitor
  sees the comments and, in place of the writing box, a sign-in link that carries the page (`LandingPath.SignInLink`,
  6.9): "Prisijunkite, kad galėtumėte komentuoti".
- **An argument's author** may comment under their own argument, as anyone can; the same display name shows anyway, and
  nothing else marks it.
- **Under an organisation's own statement, its representative speaks as the organisation.** A current representative's
  comment under that organisation's statement is shown under the organisation's name with its label (8.14.3) and the
  mark "Organizacija", never the person's name: representatives' names are not public (8.14.2, 8.14.7). The writing box
  says so before sending ("Komentaras bus paskelbtas iš karto, viešai, organizacijos vardu – ne jūsų."). Who wrote it is
  kept as for statements (never public, emptied with the account). Under any other text a representative is a person
  like anyone, under their own name, and nothing ties the comment to the organisation.
- **Under an MP's response:**
  - Any member may comment under it.
  - The MP's account or an office member may comment **only under that MP's own response**, in a passkey session (as
    writing the response, 8.5.4), shown as the MP with the one mark "Seimo narys arba jo biuras", never under a person's
    name; who typed it is kept as for responses (never public, emptied with the account). A code session is refused
    (`mp.passkey-required`; the box says "Seimo nario vardu komentuoti galima tik prisijungus prieigos raktu."). An MP
    whose mandate has ended writes no comment as the MP (8.23.14).
  - The MP's account comments nowhere else (`mp.takes-no-part`, `MpAccounts.Refusal`): 8.5.4 stays, and this is its one
    exception. An office member under any other text is a person like anyone, unmarked, as they take sides and write
    arguments.
  - **The risk:** a pile-on under an answer; the expectation that the MP answers every comment; offices less willing to
    answer at all, which is the core of the value for MPs (1.3). The tone rule is connection and explanation, not
    control (`AGENTS.md`). Mitigations: the switch (8.23.5); no email to the MP about comments (8.23.9); nothing anywhere
    counts or lists comments an MP left unanswered.
- **Speaking as is decided by the server,** from who the account is and the target, never by a choice in the form: a
  representative cannot comment under their own organisation's statement as a person, and an office member cannot comment
  under their MP's response as a person. One account, one voice per text, and nobody can be shown to be both.

## 8.23.3 The text

- **Plain text, from 1 character up to the general cap on members' texts:** the runtime setting `Texts:MaxLength`
  (2 000 by default, 0 = no limit, with a technical ceiling that always holds), the same for arguments, organisations'
  statements and MPs' responses (8.2). Normalised as an argument's (`AppealRules.NormaliseText`) and encoded on output
  (`comment.empty`, `comment.too-long`). A long comment shows its first part (about 300 characters) and "Rodyti
  daugiau", as a long argument does. Paragraphs and lists as in every typed text (8.2, "A text's layout").
- **Editing until someone agrees.** The author may edit their comment ("⋯" → "Redaguoti", shown only while it is
  allowed) while nobody has said "Sutinku" to it: a "Sutinku" was given to its exact words, so after one the text stays
  (`comment.reacted`: "Šio komentaro redaguoti nebegalima: su juo jau sutiko kiti."). Only the author edits it
  (`comment.edit-author-only`), in the voice it was written in, by the rules of writing it now: the bill open, the
  target open to comments, the kind switched on, no writing restriction, a representative still of the organisation for
  its voice, the MP's account or office in a passkey session with the mandate not ended for the MP's; the length and
  pre-screening as for a new comment; a hidden one is the moderators' (`comment.hidden`). Checked where it is written,
  under the comment's lock, which "Sutinku" takes too. No earlier text is kept; the row keeps when it was last edited
  (`edited_at`); the audit records that an edit happened (`comment.edited`), never the text; the bill's last activity
  moves, and the edited comment is new again to readers (8.11.8). `PUT /api/v1/comments/{id}`.
- **Deleting one's own** while the bill is open (until its final vote): deleted for good with its "Sutinku", its open
  reports closed and their reporters told it was withdrawn (8.3), the deletion audited without the text
  (`comment.deleted`), as an argument's withdrawal (8.2). A comment in an organisation's name may be deleted by any
  current representative; one in the MP's name by the MP's account or an office member in a passkey session. A hidden
  comment cannot be deleted (`comment.hidden`). On a closed bill nobody deletes (8.23.7); a moderator can still hide, and
  deleting the account removes a person's own comments.
- **Pre-screening refuses personal data.** The rule-based pre-screening (8.3) refuses a comment at writing when it finds
  an email address, a phone number or a personal code, with a message that names what was found ("Komentare yra telefono
  numeris. Pašalinkite jį ir siųskite dar kartą."; error `comment.screened`, with a message per flag and one for
  several). A link does not refuse it. That keeps personal data off the page without anyone reading it, and keeps
  comments out of the list.
  - **The phone rule** matches only Lithuanian numbers: the 8-digit national number (first digit 3–9: a mobile
    6xx xxxxx, Vilnius 5 xxx xxxx, another area's two-digit code and six digits, the 7xx and 8xx services) after `+370`
    or `00370`, after the trunk prefix `0` (`06xx xxxxx`, `05 xxx xxxx`), or after the prefix `8` people still write
    (`86xx xxxxx`, `8 5 xxx xxxx`), with spaces, hyphens and brackets between the digits. It also matches a mobile
    number without any prefix: eight digits starting with 6, standing alone, written together (`61234567`) or grouped as
    people write them (`612 34567`, `6123 4567`, `6 1234 567`, with a space or a hyphen), not part of a longer number,
    not after a letter (`XIV-61234567`) or `Nr.`, not a sum (a thousands grouping such as `60 000 000` is none of those
    shapes, and a currency or percent sign beside it rules it out). A landline without a prefix (`5 212 3456`) stays
    unflagged: too many sums and numbers look like one. A date (`2026-11-20`), a year span (`2024-2028`), a sum
    (`25 000 000`), a law's or a document's number, a percentage and digits inside an `https://` or `http://` link are
    no phone number. One rule for every text it screens (`RuleBasedPreScreening.ContainsPhoneNumber`, unit tests named
    as the rule).
  - **Insults and spam are not caught by rules.** Nothing refuses or flags them before they are shown; reports are the
    only way they reach a moderator. This is the accepted risk of comments without moderation beforehand.
- **Links.** The text stays plain text, encoded on output; nothing a member types becomes markup (6.4). An address that
  starts with `https://` is rendered as a link (`CommentLinks`): the address shown as typed, its host always visible (an
  address longer than 60 characters is shortened after the host, never before it, with "…"),
  `rel="nofollow ugc noopener noreferrer"`, opening in a new tab, as an organisation's link is (8.14.14, which has no
  `noreferrer`). Nothing is fetched from it, by the server or by the reader's browser, until the reader presses it
  (8.22.1). Any other scheme (`http://`, `javascript:`, a bare "lrs.lt") stays text. The box's hint says so. What stands
  against link spam: reports (8.23.8), the hourly limit and the ceiling per text (8.23.4), the writing restriction (8.3),
  and the switch per kind (8.23.5). Arguments differ: a link in an argument makes it wait for a moderator, because an
  argument can reach MPs in a summary (8.3) and a comment never does.
- **No link cards.** No title, picture or summary is made from a link. Fetching any address a member typed would be a new
  outbound path anyone can aim (private addresses, redirects, huge or slow answers), against the allowlist of 6.4 and
  6.8; and a picture cannot be shown from another site (8.22.1).
- **Where it can be written:** only under a target that is shown and open to comments (a published argument; a statement
  shown on the bill's page; a published response, not a withdrawn one: `comment.target-closed`), on a bill that is open
  (`comment.bill-not-open`), whose record (if any) is not hidden, with that kind switched on. An unknown or hidden target
  is `comment.target-not-found`. A comment never creates the bill's record.

## 8.23.4 Limits

- **An hourly limit per account,** its own policy `WriteComment` (`RateLimits:WriteComment`, 20 an hour, deployment
  configuration as the other policies, 6.4), so comments do not use up the allowance for arguments and statements.
  Writing, editing and deleting all count against it.
- **A ceiling per person per text:** `Comments:PerPersonPerTarget`, a runtime setting, 0 = no limit, the default, read
  as `Appeals:ReasonsPerPerson` is (8.2): switched on when one person floods one text, it removes nothing and counts only
  shown comments (`comment.limit`); while 0 the box shows no counter. It is counted under the module's advisory lock
  keyed by the account and the target, in the writing transaction (6.8).
- **No bill lock.** A comment creates nothing on the bill's record and changes no count, so writing one is not
  serialised per bill.
- **A writing restriction** (8.3) blocks comments like everything else the person writes for others to read
  (`writing.restricted`), in their own name and in an organisation's or an MP's; the box shows the restriction in its
  place. Existing comments stay. It does not block "Sutinku" (8.23.10).

## 8.23.5 The switches

- **Three runtime settings:** `Comments:OnReasons`, `Comments:OnStatements`, `Comments:OnResponses`, booleans (5.6), all
  on by default, in their own group "Komentarai" on the settings screen (`Admin.Group.comments`, with
  `Comments:PerPersonPerTarget`), each with its label and one sentence of help. Changed by an administrator with a fresh
  passkey confirmation like every setting, audited; in effect within the settings' cache (30 s).
- **Off stops new comments on that kind; what was written stays readable.** The writing box is gone and writes are
  refused (`comments.off`); "Aptarimas (n)" still opens, "Sutinku", reports and moderation keep working.
- The methodology page ("Aptarimas ir „Sutinku“") and the moderation rules say that comments exist, what they and
  "Sutinku" are, and that neither is counted or sent to MPs. The switches' values are not listed there (they do not
  affect what MPs receive, 5.6).

## 8.23.6 Where comments show

- **On the bill's page only,** under each target: arguments (in "Argumentai"), statements (in "Organizacijų pozicijos")
  and responses (in "Seimo narių atsakymai"); and, for a member with a public profile, on that profile (8.25.2). Nowhere
  else: not on an MP's profile ("Atsakymai"), an organisation's profile, `/projektai`, the agenda, the front page.
- **"Aptarimas (n)" under the target:** a `<details class="discuss">` whose summary stands in the text's action row
  (8.16.1), the discussion opening below the row; no script needed. n is the shown comments; with none, "Aptarimas"
  without a number, and inside "Komentarų dar nėra.". Inside: the comments oldest first, each with its name above the
  bubble (the author's display name, a link with its card when they have a public profile, 8.25.4; or the organisation's
  name; or the MP's name), the text, and under it the voice's mark ("Organizacija" with the label, or "Seimo narys arba
  jo biuras") and the time (`Display.DayTime`); "Sutinka: …" with who agreed (8.23.10); the action row with "Sutinku"
  and "⋯", which holds "Pranešti", "Redaguoti" and "Ištrinti" for whoever may use them, and a moderator's "Moderuoti";
  then the writing box ("Komentuoti"). A comment's element id is `comment-{id}`. On a closed bill the box gives way
  to "Seimas dėl projekto jau balsavo: aptarimas tik skaitomas.".
- **Server-rendered and the same for everyone.** The list is part of the target's part as the server renders it (the
  arguments' and statements' islands prerender it; under responses it is an island of its own), so it opens and reads
  with JavaScript off. Which comments the caller may delete or edit, which they wrote and which they agreed with come from
  a never-cached answer (`GET /api/v1/bills/{number}/comments/mine`, `MyCommentsResponse`, `CommentState`), read in the
  browser only, as `MyBillState` does for sides. The writing box, "Sutinku" and the "⋯" actions are islands, rendered
  only once interactive (`InteractiveBase`, `<NeedsScript />`, `IslandsBeforeStartTests`); the box learns in the same
  answer whether the caller would write as a person, as the organisation or as the MP, and says so above itself. Reports
  are the existing report islands with the target `comment`. The caller's own hidden comments show to them alone (8.23.8).
- **One request for a list's discussions.** A list of texts (a bill's arguments, its organisations' statements, its MPs'
  responses) reads the first page of all its shown threads in one request (`GET /api/v1/comments/{kind}?id=…`,
  `CommentThreads`), which checks the bill once; a thread the address asks to show further is read by itself.
- **The first 10, then "Rodyti daugiau komentarų".** Beyond 10, the link adds 20 (`CommentLimits.Step`, 8.16.4) by the
  page's address (`?komentarai=`, one query parameter naming the target and how many to show, 8.23.14), with
  that target's "Aptarimas" rendered open. The list link works without script and has its own label (8.23.3).
  Once the island runs it reads the
  next 20 from the API (`GET …/comments/{kind}/{targetId}?limit=20&cursor=10`; a later page asked without a limit is 20
  too) and adds them in place, focusing the first item without scrolling. The link uses `<ShowMore>` (8.16.4),
  with "Rodoma 10 iš 32" beside it.
- **A link to one comment lands on it.** Every link the site makes to a comment (the member's page, 8.25.2; the follow
  emails, 8.25.5; the comments notice, 8.23.9; the moderators' list and panel, 8.23.8) carries one address parameter,
  `?komentaras={comment id}`, and keeps the fragment `#comment-{id}`, so the browser scrolls there without script. The
  server resolves it when it renders the page (`GET /api/v1/comments/{id}/place`, public: the target, the bill and the
  comment's place among the target's shown comments): the target is rendered even where it would wait behind "Rodyti
  daugiau argumentų" (an argument beyond the first two of its side, then first in its side's column, 8.12.1) or "Rodyti
  daugiau" (a response beyond the first ten); its "Aptarimas" is rendered open (set by the server, as the paging does;
  the islands get the same state as a parameter and never set it from code); the thread shows everything from the first
  comment through the linked one, on the thread's own pages, the first ten and then steps of 20 (the 25th shows 30, the
  31st 50; `BillTextLinks.ShownThrough`), then the usual "Rodyti daugiau komentarų"; the linked comment is marked
  (`comment--linked`, from the design tokens). A comment that is hidden, deleted, merged away, unknown, on another bill,
  or under a target no longer shown has no place: the page renders as usual, and the answer is the same 404
  (`comment.not-found`) for all of them, so nothing tells a hidden comment from none. Every such link is made by one
  helper (`BillTextLinks.ToComment`, in the contracts and the shared kernel), never by hand. **An argument the same
  way:** `?argumentas={reason id}` with `#reason-{id}` (`BillTextLinks.ToReason`) shows that argument even beyond the
  first two of its side, first in its side's column, and marks it (`said--linked`); it opens no discussion. The follow
  email about a followed member's argument uses it.
- **What bounds the page's weight.** Comments are rendered only under the targets the server renders: the arguments the
  page shows (two per side, 8.12.1, or as many as the address asks for), the statements, and the responses of the current page of ten;
  under each, at most the first ten comments. At the default cap that is up to about 20 KB of comment text per target,
  carried once more in the prerendered islands' persisted state; a page where every comment is at the cap is unlikely,
  and the cap is the lever if it happens. Arguments a later page brings in the island ("Rodyti daugiau argumentų") read
  their comments when their "Aptarimas" is first opened by the person (the island never opens one from code). A page
  whose address grows a side (`?argumentai-uz=22`, without script) renders the first ten comments under every argument
  it shows: the address asks for the long form. The public answers about a bill carry only each target's count. The
  bill's page with ten comments under every rendered target fits 390 px without horizontal scroll.
- **Surfaces (8.16).** The comments lie on the sheet that holds their target, below it and set in; the list is not a
  sheet (that would be a sheet in a sheet). The writing box opens inside it as a quiet sheet, as a report form does.
- **Width.** The arguments, statements and responses run at the page's full width below the bill's top part (8.12.1),
  so an "Aptarimas" opens in place under its argument, in that side's column, and nothing else moves; the reader stays
  where they pressed. On a screen narrower than 64rem the sides stand one above the other anyway.
- **A target that changes keeps its comments.** A statement or response changed later keeps them; each comment carries
  its time and the target says "Keista" with its date, so a reader sees a comment was written before the change. A
  response withdrawn and written again is the same row (8.5.5) and keeps them.

## 8.23.7 The discussion follows its target

A withdrawn target takes its discussion with it, a hidden one hides it, a closed bill's discussion is read-only, for an
argument, a statement and a response alike; a merged argument's comments are deleted (`CommentCascade`).

| The target or the bill | Its comments |
|---|---|
| An argument withdrawn by its author, or removed with its author's account | Deleted with it, as its "Man svarbu" marks are (8.2). |
| A statement withdrawn, or withdrawn by closing its organisation (8.14.14) | Deleted with it: a withdrawn statement is shown nowhere and never comes back. |
| A response withdrawn (still shown as "Atsakymas atsiimtas" with its versions, 8.5.5) | Still shown, read-only (`comment.read-only`; "Sutinku" included): the response itself stays on the page. A response written again opens it again. |
| An argument, statement or response hidden by a moderator; a blocked organisation's statements; a hidden bill's record; an argument or statement waiting for a moderator | Not shown, kept with their "Sutinku"; back as they were when it is restored, the block lifted or the waiting text published. |
| An argument merged into another (8.3) | Deleted: they remarked on a text no longer shown, and moving them under another text would put words under something they did not answer. The merge's confirmation says how many comments go with it. |
| The bill closes (its final vote) | Read-only: shown, no new comments, no "Sutinku" given or taken back, nobody deletes (6.8, 6.9). A report still reaches the list; a moderator may hide, restore and answer it with "Palikti". |

Those who commented are not told about any of these. Deleting one's account deletes one's own comments; comments in an
organisation's or an MP's name stay, without the account, as statements and responses do, unless the deletion closes
the organisation (its last representative's account, 8.14.14): closing withdraws its statements, and their comments go
with them.

## 8.23.8 Moderation

**Comments are not moderated before they are shown; after, they are.** Every comment can be reported; a reported comment
is in the moderators' list; a moderator can hide it with a statement, restore it, answer the report with "Palikti" and
restrict its writer, from the list and from the bill's page alike. Its writer sees a hidden comment under its text,
greyed (`comment--hidden`), with "Paslėpė moderatorius: {paaiškinimas}" and "Jį matote tik jūs.", from their own answer
only (`MyCommentsResponse.Hidden`, 8.3).

- **Nothing waits, nothing is listed unless reported.** A comment is shown at once (pre-screening refuses rather than
  holds, 8.23.3) and is never in the moderators' list unless reported.
- **Reports.** "Pranešti" on every comment but the caller's own in any voice (`MyCommentsResponse.Own`; the server refuses
  `report.own`), and not for a moderator in a staff-passkey session (8.3); the report target `comment`
  (`AppealLimits.ReportTargets`) with the general grounds (illegal, personal data, insult, spam, other). A report is
  taken only for a shown comment; everything else is as for every report: one open report per person and target, 20 an
  hour, "Ačiū. Moderatoriai peržiūrės.", nobody emailed when it is made, the list never shows who reported, and every
  member whose report was open is told the decision by email (`email.report-decided`, 8.3).
- **In the list:** a reported comment is an item of its own, a small component (`CommentQueueItem`, with the `Panel`
  parameter of 8.16, in `CommentModerationView`) showing the comment with its name and time, the reports' grounds and
  texts, and for context the target's text with its own name (an argument's author, the organisation, the MP) and the
  bill. Actions, each its own control (8.3):
  - **hide, with a statement, as an argument is:** a required statement, recorded and sent to the comment's author with
    the moderators' contact address (the Digital Services Act's statement of reasons): a person's own address; for a
    comment in an organisation's name, every current representative (8.14.14); in the MP's name, the MP's account's own
    address, as a hidden response is (8.5.5). The comment disappears from the page and from the count with nothing in its
    place, as a hidden argument does, and its "Sutinku" with the names goes, as hiding an argument deletes its "Man
    svarbu" (8.3);
  - **"Palikti"**: an optional note; the reports close; the comment's author is not told (they never knew of the report);
  - **restrict the writer** from writing (8.3), reached from the comment as from an argument or a statement
    (`GET …/writer`, `POST …/restrict-writer`): the moderator sees who wrote it as for a statement's writer, never an
    address, a side or a mark.
  Each is recorded in `moderation_action` (target `comment`), audited as `moderation.comment.*`; hide and "Palikti"
  answer the comment's open reports (6.8). An action on a comment in the wrong state is `moderation.wrong-state`.
- **Hidden comments** are on the list of hidden items (the queue's tab "Paslėpti", `/moderavimas?skiltis=paslepti`) with "Atkurti" (a required statement;
  told as a hide is), on an open bill and, by 6.9, on a closed one.
- **On the bill's page.** A moderator or administrator in a staff-passkey session gets "Moderuoti" in the "⋯" of every
  comment shown on the bill's page, with hide and restrict the writer, and "Palikti" when it has an open report. The state
  comes from a moderators-only answer, `GET /api/v1/moderation/bills/{number}/comments`, read only once the island runs in
  the browser; nobody else sees a control or anything from it. The "Moderavimas" panel lists the bill's reported comments
  with a link to each (8.23.14).
- **The public moderation rules** have a short part on comments: that they are not checked before they are shown, what
  may be reported, and that a hidden comment's author gets the statement.

## 8.23.9 What people hear

- **An argument's author.** A notice of its own kind, not a follow email (follow emails say nothing about other people,
  8.11.2): "there are new comments under your argument", the bill's number and title and the link of 8.23.6 to the first
  new comment the email reports, so the argument shows, its "Aptarimas" opens and that comment is marked; one no longer
  shown by then leaves the page as usual. Never a comment's text, its writer, how many, or anything about "Sutinku". It
  goes in the person's rhythm (8.24): with "Iš karto" at the next run of the follow job, in that run's one email to the
  person, naming each of their arguments that got a comment since the last run; with the daily or weekly choice, in the
  gathered email, as its own part ("Nauji komentarai po jūsų argumentais:"). One busy argument therefore makes at most
  one line per email, never a stream. Comments the author wrote themself, and comments deleted or hidden by the time of
  sending, do not count; nothing left, nothing sent. The Appeals module gives the commented arguments with their authors
  since a moment through a shared-kernel feed (`ICommentFeed`), as `IMpResponseFeed` does; what was told is recorded per
  run with no person in the record (8.23.14, 8.24).
- **The switch:** on the follows screen (8.21), the last of the switches, after "Kai sekamas narys paskelbia argumentą ar
  komentarą" (8.25.5): "Kai mano argumentą pakomentuoja", on unless turned off (`notice_setting.comments`, 5.4).
- **Organisations and MPs: no email.** Representatives see the page and follow their organisation's statements there; the
  MP mail stream has its own ceilings and rules (8.19), and a notice of comments under an answer is the pressure 8.23.2
  warns of.
- Nobody else is emailed about a comment or a "Sutinku", except a follower of a member with a public profile who allows
  it (8.25.5). The only other emails are a moderator's hide and restore (8.23.8) and the decision to those who reported
  it (8.3).

## 8.23.10 "Sutinku"

- **Public with names.** Beside the comment the number and the names of those who agreed, in the form "Sutinka: Rūta
  Mockutė, Tomas ir dar 3" (display names as their owners wrote them, the first two); "ir dar 3" is the summary of a
  `details.more` that opens the full list below the line, oldest agreement first. Names are display names, a link with
  its card and initials badge for a member with a public profile (8.25.4), plain text otherwise; a member without a display name shows as
  "Lietuva 2.0 naudotojas" and counts in the number. The list is server-rendered with the comment, the same for everyone.
- **A publication under the person's name, not a private mark.** Agreeing is a deliberate publication, as writing an
  argument or a comment is, so it needs no positions consent (8.2, 6.5). In its place, by the rule of 8.15 that what
  becomes public and under whose name is always said inline: while a person has no "Sutinku" anywhere, the first press
  opens an inline confirmation in place, "Jūsų vardas bus matomas prie šio komentaro. Sutinku / Atšaukti"; after that
  the button carries it in its hint ("Jūsų vardas bus matomas"). The button is a toggle: "Sutinku" in both states, its
  icon (`agree`) filled while agreed, `aria-pressed` (8.16.1); pressed again it takes the agreement back. Whether the
  person has one is read from the rows themselves (`MyCommentsResponse.AgreedBefore`), so nothing new is stored, in the
  browser or elsewhere (8.22.1).
- **One per person per comment,** not on a comment one wrote, in any voice (`agreement.own-comment`); taken back while
  the bill is open; frozen when the bill closes (`comment.bill-not-open`) and under a withdrawn response; no history of
  changes (the row exists or not). Gone with the comment when it is deleted (by whoever may delete it, or with its
  target, 8.23.7) and when a moderator hides the comment itself (8.23.8); kept, unseen, while only its target is hidden.
- **"Man svarbu" and "Sutinku" are opposites, and the site says so wherever both appear:** "Man svarbu" on an argument is
  private. It may be given on either side (8.2), so a mark alone does not tell its giver's side; but the figure beside an
  argument counts only the marks of people on that argument's side, so a public list of names beside it would tell sides
  by difference (the names whose marks are counted against those that are not, and how the list changes when someone
  changes side). "Sutinku" on a comment is public, under the name, and tied to no side. The methodology's "Kas matoma
  viešai, o kas ne" (`AppealText.resx` `Method.Privacy` and its part), the positions consent texts (`UiText.resx`
  `PositionConsent.Text`, which names "Man svarbu") and the terms and privacy texts say it in one sentence each.
- **Who marks:** any member who has accepted the terms. The MP's own account does not mark (`mp.takes-no-part`; 8.5.4: it
  has no "Man svarbu" either), not even under its own response. A representative or an office member marks as a person,
  under their own name, never as the organisation or the MP.
- **Never counted anywhere else:** not in the bill's counts, `/projektai`'s activity, a statistic, a summary, an email or
  anything to MPs; it changes no order (comments stay oldest first, 8.23.1) and triggers no notice (8.23.9). It is not
  listed on the person's public profile (8.25.2).
- **Limits:** the shared participation limit (`RateLimits:Participation`, 60 a minute, with sides, follows and "Man
  svarbu", 8.11.2) and the terms gate (8.22.2). A writing restriction does not block it: 8.3 says a restriction blocks
  "everything the person writes for others to read" and does not touch taking a side or "Man svarbu"; a name beside
  someone else's text is published, but it is not writing, and a restricted person's earlier texts stay public too.
- **The legal ground:** a public agreement with a remark on a bill is opinion data the person makes public themself.
  Whether GDPR Art. 9(2)(e) (data manifestly made public by the person) is the ground, and whether the inline sentence
  suffices, is for the lawyer's reading (12).
- **Data:** `appeals.comment_agreement` (`comment_id`, `user_id`, `created_at`; one row per pair), in the person's export
  (bill, the comment it was on, time), deleted with the account. **Readable by `lt2_readonly`** (`DatabaseRoles.ReadableTables`),
  by the rule the design uses: `reason` is readable (public content with its author, 6.9), while "Man svarbu"
  (`endorsement`) is withheld because, read beside the positions, it tells the side; an agreement is public under the name
  and tells no side, so it follows `reason`. (The comments table itself is withheld, because it holds the account behind
  an organisation's or an MP's comment, as `organisation_statement` and `mp_response` are.) API by the naming of "Man
  svarbu": `PUT /api/v1/comments/{id}/agreement` and `DELETE /api/v1/comments/{id}/agreement`; the island shows the
  caller's own marks from the never-cached `GET /api/v1/bills/{number}/comments/mine`.

## 8.23.11 Data and API

`appeals.comment`: `id` (UUID v7), `target_kind` (`reason`, `statement`, `response`), `target_id`, `bill_number` (for
freezing and for hiding with the record; statements and responses refer to their bill by number and have no record, so a
comment never needs one), `speaks_as` (`person`, `organisation`, `mp`), `organisation_id` or `seimas_person_id` for the
latter two, `author_user_id` (the person; for the other two the writer, never public, emptied with the account), `text`
(up to `Texts:MaxLength`), `status` (`published`, `hidden`), `created_at`, `hidden_at`, `edited_at` (8.23.3); an index on
(`target_kind`, `target_id`, `created_at`). A deleted comment is a deleted row: no per-person history beyond the rows, no
`revision`. Withheld from `lt2_readonly` (`DatabaseRoles.WithheldTables`): it holds the account behind an organisation's
or an MP's comment. In the account's export (the person's own comments, and those it wrote in an organisation's or an MP's
name, as statements and responses are); a person's own comments are deleted with the account. `comment_agreement` as in
8.23.10. Reports (`report`) take the target `comment` with `record_id` empty; moderation actions the same kind.

```
GET    /api/v1/comments/{kind}/{targetId}?limit=&cursor=   shown comments of one target, oldest first, a page at a time,
                                                       each with who agreed (public)
GET    /api/v1/comments/{kind}?id=…&id=…                 the first page of several targets' threads, in the order asked, a
                                                       target not shown left out; at most 100 (public)
GET    /api/v1/comments/{id}/place                     where a shown comment stands, for a link to it (public; 404 otherwise)
POST   /api/v1/comments/{kind}/{targetId}              text (member; WriteComment; refused when off, screened, closed)
DELETE /api/v1/comments/{id}                           whoever may delete it (8.23.3), while the bill is open; WriteComment
PUT    /api/v1/comments/{id}                           its author edits it until someone says "Sutinku" (8.23.3); WriteComment
PUT    /api/v1/comments/{id}/agreement    DELETE /api/v1/comments/{id}/agreement     "Sutinku" (8.23.10)
GET    /api/v1/bills/{number}/comments/mine            the caller's deletable, editable, own and hidden comments, own marks
                                                       and voice per target (never cached)
GET    /api/v1/moderation/comments                     reported comments (the list's part) and hidden ones
POST   /api/v1/moderation/comments/{id}/hide   …/restore   (statement)      …/keep   (optional note)
GET    /api/v1/moderation/comments/{id}/writer         POST /api/v1/moderation/comments/{id}/restrict-writer
GET    /api/v1/moderation/bills/{number}/comments      the bill page's moderators-only answer
```

`{kind}` is `reason`, `statement` or `response`. The thread's first page holds at least ten (`limit` below ten is read as
ten), a later page 20 when no `limit` is asked (`CommentRules.Limit`); the answer's `NextCursor` says where the next page
starts. The public answers about arguments, statements and responses carry each target's comment count (`comments`). A
public answer carries no account's id; beside a name it carries only the profile id of a member who has a public profile
(8.25.4). The terms gate covers every write (8.22.2); none is exempt. `GET|PUT /me/notices` carries `comments`.

## 8.23.14 Further rules

1. **The paging parameter** of 8.23.6 is `komentarai`, its value the target's id (32 hexadecimal digits) and how many to
   show, joined by a hyphen (`?komentarai=0193…a1-20`); one target per address, with the rest of the page as it was.
   The server renders that "Aptarimas" open.
2. **The count on the public answers** (8.23.11) is a field `comments` on each argument, statement and response (the number
   of shown comments), so "Aptarimas (n)" shows for targets whose thread is not rendered (arguments a later page brought).
3. **An MP whose mandate has ended** writes no comment as the MP, as no response (8.5.2): `response.mandate-ended`. The MP's
   own account of a former MP still takes no part elsewhere (8.5.4).
4. **The notice of 8.23.9 in the rhythm of 8.24:** one kind of the follow job, `CommentNotices` (an `IMemberNoticeKind`,
   Notifications). Each run records, under the bill, one `notify.bill_event` of kind `comment` per argument that got a
   comment from someone else in its window: from where the run before that recorded any stopped (the first run, the last
   hour; never more than three days back) to two minutes before the run (`FollowRules.CommentsWindow`), so a comment
   committed just after a run's read is read by the next one; an email holds one line per argument, with the day of the
   latest comments, however many runs found some; key: the argument's id and the run's moment; no person. Whoever's
   choice serves the pass takes it: "Iš karto" in that run's email, the daily or weekly choice in the gathered one, where
   it is its own part ("Nauji komentarai po jūsų argumentais:"), one line per argument. When the email is made the window
   is read again, so an argument whose comments are all deleted or hidden by then, or that is no longer shown, is left
   out. One thing alone is the email `email.comment-notice`. Each line links to the first new comment it reports, the
   earliest still shown in its windows when the email is made (`BillTextLinks.ToComment`, 8.23.6).
5. **A comment's moderation notices** reuse the existing kinds: to a person `email.moderation-notice` (notices
   `comment-hidden`, `comment-restored`, with the moderators' contact address), to an organisation's representatives
   `email.organisation-notice` (`comment-hidden`, `comment-restored`), to the MP's account `email.mp-response-notice`
   (`comment-hidden`, `comment-restored`). "Palikti" tells the author nothing (8.23.8).
6. **Moderating a comment whose target is not shown** (hidden, waiting, a hidden record): hide, restore and "Palikti" work on
   the comment itself whatever its target's state; the comment shows again only when both are shown.
7. **The "Moderavimas" panel** on the bill's page lists the bill's reported comments with a link to each
   (`?komentaras={id}#comment-{id}`, 8.23.6); the actions are on the comment.
8. **A link to a comment or an argument** carries `?komentaras=` or `?argumentas=` (8.23.6): the page shows the target,
   opens its "Aptarimas" through the linked comment on the thread's own pages (10, 30, 50, …), and marks it; a comment not
   shown changes nothing on the page.
