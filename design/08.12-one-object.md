# 8.12 One object: every bill is open, nobody starts an appeal

Where 8.1–8.11 say otherwise, this section holds.

Nobody proposes, describes, approves, requests or opens anything before people can take a side. For the user the bill
and the appeal are one thing: the bill's page. "Kreipimasis" is the word for what people on a bill ask of MPs and for
what the summaries carry.

## 8.12.1 What a bill offers

- **Every active bill is open.** On any bill in the catalogue that the Seimas has not finished (no final vote, not
  rejected, not withdrawn: the rule 8.1 step 6 closes a record by) a member can take a side, write and mark arguments,
  follow, share and report, at once. Nobody proposes, starts or approves anything first.
- **One page,** `/seimas/{number}`, server-rendered and readable without JavaScript; taking a side, the arguments and
  the moderators' panel are WebAssembly islands. Its parts are listed under "The order and the width" below.
  `/seimas/{number}/kreipimasis` redirects permanently (301) to `/seimas/{number}`,
  `/seimas/{number}/kreipimasis/istorija` to `/seimas/{number}/istorija`, and `/kreipimaisi/naujas` to `/projektai`
  (8.12.5). Comments stand under each argument, statement and response, inside that part, with no part of their own
  (8.23.6).
- **Before anyone has taken part** the page shows the same controls with zero counts and one line, "Dėl šio projekto dar
  niekas nenurodė, ko prašo Seimo narių." The public answers for such a bill return this empty open state, not "not
  found".
- **A finished bill** nobody took part in shows the bill only (and MPs' responses written while it was open); its public
  answer is "not found" (`record.not-found`). One with participants is closed as in 8.1 step 6. Closed by the final vote:
  frozen counts ("Seimas balsavo galutinai; skaičiai užfiksuoti {date} ir nebesikeičia.") beside the Seimas result
  ("Seimo sprendimas", with how each MP voted on the vote's page). Closed by a moderator because the Seimas rejected the
  bill or its initiators withdrew it: frozen counts ("Skaičiai užfiksuoti {date} ir nebesikeičia.") beside, where the
  result stands, "Seimo sprendimas" with "Seimas projektą atmetė {yyyy-MM-dd}." or "Projektas atsiimtas" with
  "Iniciatoriai projektą atsiėmė {yyyy-MM-dd}.", and the link "Šaltinis" to the lrs.lt source (opened as any external
  link, `rel="external noopener"`); no MPs' votes. The header carries "Uždarytas" either way, and the public answer
  carries `closedReason` (`vote`, `rejected`, `withdrawn`), `closedDecidedOn` and `closedSourceUrl` beside `closingVoteId`
  and `closedAt`.
- **Its name.** Since nobody starts an appeal, the code calls the per-bill row the bill's record: the entity
  `BillRecord`, the table `appeals.bill_record`, columns `record_id`, error codes `record.*`, the stored kind `record`.
  "Appeal" in the singular names no object in the code. The module is `Appeals`, its schema `appeals`, and the settings
  keys are under `Appeals:`. A bill is never called a record and a record never a "project": the bill is the
  `parliament.bill` row. Where sections 5 to 8 say "the `appeal` row" or "an appeal's …" of the object, they mean the
  record.
- **The record.** The Appeals module keeps its `bill_record` row per bill as its own record of what people did on the
  bill (the `parliament` tables are the import's). It is created when first needed: the first side, argument or
  description, or a moderator's action on the bill. Creation takes the bill's lock and the unique index, so two people
  arriving together make one row; the lock is taken only when the record is created, so taking part on a bill that has
  its record is never serialised per bill. Taking a side checks the positions consent before the record is created, so
  a refused attempt creates nothing. The row has no initiator and nothing public says who was first.
- **Statuses:** `open`, `closed` and `rejected` (hidden by a moderator). A closed record's reason is apart from its
  status (`closed_reason`: `vote`, or `rejected` or `withdrawn` by a moderator, 8.1 step 6); the reason `rejected` (the
  Seimas rejected the bill) is not the status `rejected` (hidden).
- **Rules as in 8.2, 8.3 and 6.x:** the positions consent, one position per person per bill, arguments for either side,
  "Man svarbu", withdrawal, the per-person limits, reports, writing restrictions, pre-screening of arguments, what is
  public and what is private.
- **The side buttons** work as in the front page's row (8.7): the pressed one, pressed again, takes the side back; there
  is no "Atsiimti". The line under them reads "Jūs prašote balsuoti už. Tai matote tik jūs." (or "prieš"); the pressed
  button's title says "Paspaudę dar kartą, atsiimsite." Before the island has started and while a press is sent, the
  side buttons are disabled, as every island control is, but not dimmed: full colour and words, the pair marked
  `aria-busy`, the "wait" cursor and a slow dark sheen across them (none with reduced motion). With JavaScript off they
  keep the dimmed disabled look beside the "needs JavaScript" note. On hover a side's fill darkens a tenth; its words
  stay white.
- **"Data neteisinga?"** (8.4) is in the side column's "Stadijos Seime", right under the stages and their planned date.
  It shows for a participant on an open record and only while a planned date is shown. It lies inside the stages'
  sheet, so the form it opens is a quiet sheet (8.16).
- **"Iš kur ši data?"** (a hint) and the link to the agenda page "Darbotvarkė lrs.lt" (8.4; only an `https` address, and
  not for a date a moderator set) stand in "Stadijos Seime", in one small line right under the stages and their planned
  date, above "Data neteisinga?". They show wherever the sheet is, in the side column or full-width, whenever the bill
  has a planned date; the header keeps the stage and "Planuojama …" tags only.
- **The order and the width.**
  - **The header:** the way back "Projektai / Įstatymo projektas XVP-0412" ("Įstatymo projektas" and the number; no
    label line of its own), the title, then one compact line: the stage tags with "Planuojama …" (or the last stage voted, and
    "Uždarytas" on a closed record), then "Sekti" (the compact button, 8.11.2) and "Sekėjai: n" with the hint "Kas yra
    sekimas?" (8.11.2). The header stands one step (`--sp-4`, 16px) above the page's first part; the parts below keep
    the page's usual distance (`--sp-7`). The MP's profile and an organisation's page have the same header and the same
    step.
  - **The main column** (5/8 of the width, beside the side column): "Apie ką projektas" (8.13); "Ko prašo Lietuva 2.0
    naudotojai": both sides with the counts for accounts older than 30 days, the side buttons, the "not a poll" note
    right under them, then "Riba" with the threshold (8.12.2) and the share line (8.11.5); the Seimas result of a closed
    bill. **The side column:** "Stadijos Seime" first (each stage's result, or "dar nebalsuota", the planned stage
    showing "Planuojama …", "Iš kur ši data?" with the agenda link and "Data neteisinga?" under them), the moderators'
    panels (staff only), then "Suvestinėse Seimo nariams", the summaries that held the bill (8.7). The bill's e-seimas
    address is on the page once: in "Apie ką projektas" ("Projekto tekstas e. seime", or "Oficialus tekstas, iš kurio
    parengta" when the description was made from that same address, 8.13), not in "Stadijos Seime".
  - **Then at the page's full width:** what is new since the reader's last visit (8.11.8), MPs' responses (8.5.6),
    organisations' statements (8.14.3), the members' arguments. Responses and statements show only when there is
    something to show; a linked MP's account still gets its form (the responses' island brings the part's sheet with
    it), and a representative their organisation's. The arguments always show: they hold "Parašyti argumentą".
  - **Full-width at the bottom:** all Seimas votes on the bill, newest first, with the same result column as the Seimas
    vote history (8.18). Each row carries its Vilnius date beside its stage and kind tags. The vote's wording links to
    its detail page. The bill's title is used when the vote has no wording. The data source line follows. A page with no
    side column (a record hidden by the moderators, or a finished bill nobody took part in) shows "Stadijos Seime" full-width above the votes;
    having no "Apie ką projektas", it carries the e-seimas link ("Projektas e-seime") under the stages, so the page
    never lacks it.
  - **On a narrow screen** (one column from 54rem down) the side column comes last: the header, "Apie ką projektas",
    "Ko prašo", the Seimas result of a closed bill, what is new, the MPs' responses, the organisations' statements, the
    arguments, the Seimas votes, then the side column ("Stadijos Seime", the moderators' panels, the summaries) and the
    data source line. The header still says the stage and the planned date at the top.
  - **The markup's order.** The side column is last in the page's markup (only the data source line follows it) and a
    wide screen places it back beside the main column (one grid for the page's parts: the main column and the side
    column in its first row, every other part across both columns below the taller of the two), so a phone needs no
    reordering by style; a reader of the markup meets the side column, a landmark of its own (`aside`), after the page's
    other parts.
- **The arguments' length.** Each side shows its first two arguments, in the side's order (the counted "Man svarbu"
  marks, most first, then the oldest first). Under each side's own column stand "Rodoma 2 iš 15" and "Rodyti daugiau
  argumentų" (named apart from a long argument's own "Rodyti daugiau", 8.2), the site's one "show more" (8.16.4): a
  link to the same page with that side grown by 20 (`?argumentai-uz=22`, `?argumentai-pries=22`; every other parameter
  of the address kept). Without script, and before the island starts, the server renders the grown page, with the discussions under every argument it shows (8.23.6, "What bounds the page's weight");
  once the island runs, the island reads the side's next 20 from the API and adds them in place, focusing the first added
  without scrolling. Each side has its own link since the columns stack on a phone. The bill's public answer carries each side's first two and each side's
  count only; the rest comes a page at a time (`GET /api/v1/bills/{number}/record/reasons`, at most 100 an answer, 8.8),
  so a bill with many arguments is never sent whole.
- **A linked argument (`?argumentas=`, 8.23.6) and the one just published** stand first in their side's column,
  marked, when their pages would put them further on; a later page that brings them again adds nothing twice.

## 8.12.2 The threshold and what MPs receive

- **One threshold for all bills:** `Digest:ThresholdDefault` (100), read when a summary is made, never below
  `Digest:ThresholdMinimum` (50).
- A moderator can set a bill's own number, with a public statement (8.4); it then stays until a moderator changes it.
  Who set it is `default` or `moderator`; the page then says "Šio projekto ribą nustatė moderatorius." with the
  statement.
- **Once a bill has been in a summary really sent to MPs** it counts as having reached its threshold, whatever the
  threshold or the participants do later (`bill_record.first_summarised_at`).
- Everything else in 8.4 holds: the schedule, the ceiling, "changed notably", hold and release, the urgent summary, the
  dry run and the public copies. A summary holds published arguments whether or not a moderator has seen them, and is
  reviewed before it goes (8.19.2).
- A bill enters the moderators' queue as "under its threshold and on the agenda" only when it has at least one
  participant.
- **A reached threshold is said in words, not measured.** Under its threshold the bill's page shows the ruler and "n
  dalyvių · riba N" (the ruler's fill a lighter shade of the links' green, at least 3:1 against its track in both
  themes), with the hint "Kas yra riba?". Once the threshold is reached the ruler goes and two lines say what is true:
  - "Riba pasiekta – balsuoti prašo {n} dalyvių." (the number's own form: "1 dalyvis", "2 dalyviai", "10 dalyvių"); on
    a closed record "prašė". A bill that counts as reached only because it was in a summary really sent, its participants
    now under its threshold, says instead "Projektas jau buvo suvestinėje Seimo nariams – balsuoti prašo {n} dalyvių."
    ("prašė" on a closed record; owner, 2026-11-21).
  - Under it, the first that holds: the day the next summary that would hold the bill is planned, "Suvestinė Seimo
    nariams planuojama {yyyy-MM-dd}.", and while sending is off (8.19.1) "Suvestinė planuojama {yyyy-MM-dd}; kol vyksta
    bandymas, Seimo nariams ji nesiunčiama."; else the last summary that went with it, "Suvestinė Seimo nariams
    išsiųsta {yyyy-MM-dd}." or "Bandomoji suvestinė sudaryta {yyyy-MM-dd}; Seimo nariams ji nesiųsta."; else, on an open
    record, "Kitos suvestinės Seimo nariams data dar nežinoma." A closed record shows only the last summary, if any.
  - **The day** is a prepared summary's own day when one holds the bill (its bills are fixed, 8.19.2); otherwise the day
    the rules of 8.4 give now (`DigestRules.NextSendingDay`): the weekly summary of the first week from today that has a
    known sitting and is not prepared yet, when the bill is planned that week or changed notably since its last summary
    (a bill never in one always has); the before-vote summary of its planned sitting at a voted stage, unless that
    week's Monday summary holds it; whichever goes first. No day when the bill is held, a time setting is off, or no
    sitting is known yet (the agenda is published about 10–12 days ahead). It is "planuojama", not "bus siunčiama":
    moderators may hold the bill or decline the summary, and the agenda moves. Never a countdown.
    `GET /api/v1/bills/{number}/record/digests/next` answers it; the page reads it with the record, and a threshold
    first reached by a side taken on the page asks it then.
  - The share line (8.11.5) then says nothing about the threshold.
  - **The rows of a list** (the front page and the agenda, 8.7, 8.11.1; `/projektai`, 8.12.5) say it without the
    threshold's number and name the summary's day instead, the first that holds (owner, 2026-11-21): "suvestinė planuojama
    {yyyy-MM-dd}", the same day the bill's page shows; else the last summary really sent to MPs that held it, "suvestinė
    išsiųsta {yyyy-MM-dd}"; else "riba pasiekta". A closed record names only the last one sent. The rows have no trial
    variants: a planned day is shown whatever sending is (8.19.1), and a dry run never counts as sent. On the agenda's rows
    the line is "{n} dalyvių · suvestinė planuojama {yyyy-MM-dd}", each part kept whole. The rule is one pure function
    (`RowSummaryRules`); the host reads the days once per list for the reached bills shown (`IDigests.SummaryDaysAsync`)
    and the list answers carry the result as `summary` (`{kind: planned|sent, day}`, null otherwise) on each bill's
    numbers (`GET /api/v1/agenda`) and row (`GET /api/v1/appeals`). A member's row that reaches the threshold by the
    member's own side asks the next day then, as the bill's page does.

## 8.12.3 Moderation after the fact

Nothing waits for approval before it is shown, except what pre-screening or a report flags (arguments).

- **The queue holds:** flagged and reported arguments, reports about a bill's description (8.13), flags about dates,
  requests for an urgent summary, bills under the threshold on the agenda, organisations and statements (8.14.14), MPs'
  responses (8.5.5) and reported comments (8.23.8).
- **Actions on a bill**, from the queue and from the "Moderavimas" panel on the bill's page, each with a statement and
  audited: write or correct the description (on any active bill; 8.13), confirm an AI summary or remove a description
  (8.13), set the threshold, correct the planned date or stage, hold or release sending, approve an urgent summary,
  close a bill the Seimas rejected or its initiators withdrew and undo that close (8.1 step 6:
  `POST /api/v1/moderation/records/{id}/close` and `…/reopen`), hide and restore. The same moderator writes and
  publishes a description; no second moderator.
- **The panel** reads `GET /api/v1/moderation/bills/{number}`, which answers for a bill without a record too. Before an
  action that needs the record's id, the page calls `POST /api/v1/moderation/bills/{number}/record`, which finds or
  creates the record; creating it alone shows nothing new, is not an action and is not audited. The description is
  written by bill number: `PUT /api/v1/moderation/bills/{number}/description`. "Palikti" is
  `POST /api/v1/moderation/records/{id}/keep`. The panel shows on an open and on a closed record, with only what applies
  in its state (8.3); it also lists the bill's reported comments, each a link to it (8.23.8).
- **Hiding a bill's record** closes taking part on it: the page shows the bill only, under "Dalyvavimas uždarytas",
  saying that taking part is closed by the moderators, with the statement. Sides and arguments are kept unseen, as
  decided in 8.3, and restoring brings them back (open, or closed if the record had closed). The public answer has the
  status `rejected` with only the latest hide statement, no counts and no arguments; the caller's own answer is empty;
  `/projektai` leaves the bill out; the agenda says "Dalyvavimas uždarytas" with a link to the bill.
- **Arguments are moderated on the bill's page too.** In a staff-passkey session a moderator or an administrator sees,
  on every argument on the bill's page, behind "Moderuoti", the actions the queue offers for that argument in its
  state: hide (a required statement), merge into another argument on the same side (choosing the target; marks move
  with it), publish one that waits, and restrict its author; each opens only its own fields, as on the queue's screen
  (8.3), and uses the queue's own endpoints. The "Moderavimas" panel also lists the bill's arguments that wait, carry a
  pre-screening flag or have an open report, which other visitors do not see, with the same actions. What the controls
  need comes from the moderators-only answer `GET /api/v1/moderation/bills/{number}`, which carries the bill's waiting
  and published arguments with what the queue shows for each (flags, reports, whether seen, merge candidates); the
  argument list asks for it only once it runs in the browser, so prerendering is the same for everyone. Nobody else sees
  a control or anything from that answer. Each action is recorded, audited and told to the author exactly as from the
  queue, and the page then shows the result. Restoring a hidden argument is on the list of hidden items.
- **Descriptions:** an `ai-draft` description stays `ai-draft` when corrected, and an `ai` one becomes `ai-draft` when
  a moderator confirms it or corrects its text (8.13).
- **Nothing done to a record is emailed** (hide, restore, threshold, description, reopening): it has no author. A close
  as rejected or withdrawn reaches the bill's followers by the follow email (8.6), not as a moderation notice. Only an argument's
  author is emailed, and those emails link to `/seimas/{number}`.
- A report on the target `record` is a report about the bill's description and is accepted only while the record has
  one.
- Date flags: participants only (someone with a side on the bill; `flag.participants-only`).
- The moderators' author screen lists the person's arguments only; a writing restriction blocks writing arguments and
  date flags.

## 8.12.4 What a record does not have

- No proposals, requests, approvals or initiator role: nobody chooses a bill's threshold, proposes description changes
  or is emailed as a record's author, and nothing is counted per hour of openings. Moderators' tools have nothing to
  restrict for a record (`restrict` is offered on an argument only).

## 8.12.5 Lists

- **`/projektai`** ("Projektai": the menu item, the page's heading and title) lists active bills, with a search by
  number or title over all active bills, and the tabs "Svarstomi" and "Uždaryti" (`?skirtukas=uzdaryti`). The search
  keeps bills with every word of the query, ignoring case, in the number or the title. The closed tab lists closed
  records, latest closed first, with their frozen counts. `/kreipimaisi` answers 301 to `/projektai` with its query
  kept. The page works without JavaScript and its answer is public and cacheable. Nothing stands under the list: no
  note, no hint and no "not a poll" notice (owner, 2026-11-21); the bill's page and the methodology say what the numbers
  are. The list's sheet has no heading of its own (the page's title, the tabs, the search and the orders stand above
  it on the ground), so the page's title and the tab row under it stay at the top of the screen; the lead, the tabs of
  the list, the search and the orders scroll away (8.16.5).
- **The orders.** The page offers three, as plain links after "Rikiuoti:": "pagal aktyvumą" (`rikiuoti=aktyvumas`, the
  default), "pagal planuojamą datą" (`rikiuoti=data`, soonest first) and "pagal naujausią veiklą"
  (`rikiuoti=naujausia`); any other value falls back to the default. The API's `GET /api/v1/appeals` takes `sort` =
  `activity` (the default), `participants`, `arguments`, `planned` or `latest`.
  - Activity is the bill's participants on both sides together plus its published arguments, a plain sum; marks and
    reports do not count.
  - Each order breaks ties by activity, then by planned date (soonest first, none last), then by number; by planned
    date, bills without one come last, and bills with no activity come last, by planned date.
  - **Last activity** is the latest of: a side taken, changed or withdrawn; an argument published or changed in public;
    a comment written or changed in public; an organisation's statement made public or changed; an MP's response made
    public or changed. It is one time per bill, kept on its record (`appeals.bill_record.last_activity_at`) and written
    in the same transaction as each of those (Appeals, `RecordActivity`); nothing about who acted is kept. Taking the
    same side again is no activity; an argument restored by a moderator is not "published" again; a statement or
    response on a bill without a record marks nothing, since nothing is created for it. Bills without any come last
    (then the usual ties). In that order each row shows "Paskutinė veikla {yyyy-MM-dd HH:mm}".
- **The filter "Planuojami per 14 dienų"** (`planuojami=taip`; `planned=true` in the API) keeps bills with a planned
  date within 14 days from today (Vilnius time), a fixed number in the code, not a setting. It is a box in the search
  form, right above "Rodyti", and applies with it, keeping the order (owner, 2026-11-21: no line of its own above the
  list); the orders keep it and the search.
- **Each row** shows the title, the number, "dalyvių {n} · argumentų {m}" (or "dar niekas nedalyvauja"), "riba {N}"
  when anyone takes part and the threshold is not reached (once it is, the summary's day or "riba pasiekta", 8.12.2), the
  planned date, and the sides' numbers "už 6 800 · prieš 4 280" beside its bar, one number
  a side as the agenda's rows have them (owner, 2026-11-21: the counts for accounts older than 30 days are on the
  bill's page).
- **Its length.** The page shows the first 20; "Rodyti daugiau" adds 50 in place by the address (`?rodyti=70`), as the history of votes does (8.18).
  "Rodoma 20 iš 135" says how many are shown. One page holds at most 200; past that "Tolesni projektai →" starts the next page there (`?nuo=200`). The API's
  `GET /api/v1/appeals` takes `q`, `planned`, `tab`, `sort`, `cursor` and `limit` (20 by default, 1 to 200; a value it
  would not take falls back, never an error). The record id in the list and the agenda is null for a bill without a
  record. The list is composed in memory, not paged in the database: the bills, their titles and planned dates are the
  Parliament module's tables and the sides and arguments the Appeals module's, and no module reads another's tables.
- **The agenda (8.11.1)** shows both sides for every bill; a bill nobody took part in says so and links to its page.
- **The front page (8.7, 8.11.1)** has "Artimiausiu metu Seime"; `/projektai` is the list of bills by activity.
- **The menu.** From 54rem (864 px) up, the menu's items are in the secondary type size (15 px instead of 17) with
  tighter gaps, and no item breaks inside; nothing is dropped or shortened. At 1280 px the signed-in menu then sits on
  one line beside the brand. Nothing forces one line: a narrower window wraps the menu under the brand, so no width
  scrolls sideways.

## 8.12.6 What the tests hold

- A member takes a side and writes an argument on any active bill without anyone having started or approved anything;
  two members arriving at the same moment land on the same record.
- No page, API answer or text mentions proposing, starting or asking for an appeal.
- Nothing shows who took part first on a bill.
- The signed-in menu is on one line at 1280 px, and the pages have no horizontal scroll at 390 px (`LayoutTests`).
