# 8.4 Summary emails to MPs

**Who.** Every sitting MP with an official address in open data (8.5.1). No one chooses recipients, so all MPs and
factions are treated on equal terms. MPs who have not joined LT2 are included.

**When: automatic rules, with corrections by moderators.** Whether a summary is really sent is the setting
`Digest:Sending` (8.19.1): while it is `off`, the default, every summary is made, reviewed and published as a dry run
and sent to nobody, and the site says so ("Kol vyksta bandymas, suvestinės sudaromos ir skelbiamos viešai, bet Seimo
nariams nesiunčiamos.").

| Rule | Default | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| A bill enters summaries when it reaches its threshold | The same for all bills, 100 participants (both sides together), never below the minimum (50), read when a summary is made; a moderator may set a bill's own number, never below the minimum (8.12.2) | `Digest:ThresholdDefault`, `Digest:ThresholdMinimum` |
| **Weekly summary** | Monday 07:00 Vilnius time (or `off`), in weeks when the Seimas sits. Lists eligible bills that are on that week's agenda or changed notably since the last summary. No such bill, no email. | `Digest:Weekly` |
| Changed notably | A bill never yet in a summary, or one whose participants (both sides together) rose or fell by at least 10 % since the last summary that held it | `Digest:NotableChange` |
| **Before-vote summary** | 12:00 (or `off`) on the working day (Monday to Friday; public holidays are not known) before a sitting whose agenda has an eligible bill at a known stage (presentation, consideration or adoption), unless that week's Monday summary already covered the same sitting | `Digest:BeforeVote` |
| Review before it goes | Each weekly and before-vote summary is prepared 24 hours before its time (Monday 07:00 on Sunday 07:00; 12:00 on the working day before a sitting on the day before at 12:00): its bills and arguments are fixed then, moderators are told and may hide an argument, hold a bill, approve it or decline it; the numbers are read when it goes (8.19.2) | `Digest:ReviewHours` |
| Ceiling per MP | Two summaries a week | `Digest:MaxPerWeek` |
| Party group numbers | Shown for a side when at least 10 of its participants said they support the MP's party, and the rest of the side is none or also at least 10; 0 = no floor, any group of one or more | `Digest:PartyGroupMinimum` |
| Re-sending after an item slips | Only in the next regular summary; never an extra email because a date moved | – |

**The threshold.** One number for all bills, `Digest:ThresholdDefault`, read when a summary is made and never below the
administrator's minimum. The bill's page shows it with the progress ("612 iš 100"), which also gives people a reason to
share. Rules around it:

- It is **public**: everyone sees the number, who set it (`default` or `moderator`) and the progress towards it.
- It can never be below the administrator's minimum: raising the minimum lifts every bill under it.
- A bill that has been in a summary really sent to MPs stays eligible whatever its participants do later. A dry run does
  not count.
- **A moderator may set a bill's own number (8.12.2)**, with a public statement shown on the bill's page; it then stays
  until a moderator changes it.
- **Anyone can object to it, outside the application.** Because the number is public, people can write to the
  moderators at the contact address when it keeps one side or the other from MPs; there is no objection form or queue in
  the product. A moderator keeps the number or sets the bill's own, and a change is shown on the page with its reason.
- There is no automatic override before a vote. As a safety net, a bill with participants that is still under its
  threshold when it reaches the agenda appears in the moderators' queue, so a person looks at it even if nobody
  objected.

Every value in the Setting column is a runtime setting (5.6): an administrator changes it on the settings screen under
"Suvestinės" (the thresholds under appeals), with no deploy. A change applies from the next summary run, never to one
already prepared, is audited, and shows on the public methodology page (`/metodika`), because these numbers decide which
bills MPs hear about.

Plenary sittings are normally on Tuesdays and Thursdays, so in practice an MP gets one email on Monday and, when
something relevant is planned for Thursday, one on Wednesday.

**When something is off.** The planned date comes from scraping the upcoming agenda on lrs.lt, which is published about
10–12 days ahead and changes often; items are also added on the day. So:

- **an unreadable page changes nothing.** The agenda watcher refuses a page it cannot fetch, one where not one sitting
  date is found (a changed layout), and one with sitting dates but no bill at all while planned items exist: a page that
  was fetched is kept with its failure, the run is recorded as failed (or refused) and logged as an error for the alerting
  (9.4), and the last good dates and the moderators' corrections stay (6.8, 8.3). It never acts on a guess;
- any participant (someone with a side on the bill) can flag "the date is wrong" or "this is being voted sooner", with a
  link to the source and, optionally, the date they say is right ("Tikroji data (jei žinote)", a text field
  `yyyy-MM-dd`, stored with the flag and shown to the moderators in the queue); the control ("Data neteisinga?") is
  offered only while the page shows a planned date and sits in the side column's "Stadijos Seime", under the stages and
  their planned date (8.12.1);
- **where the date comes from is said beside it:** on the bill's page one hint "Iš kur ši data?" in "Stadijos Seime",
  under the stages and their planned date (8.12.1; 8.15: the draft agenda of Seimas sittings published on lrs.lt about
  10–12 days ahead, such dates often move; "Plačiau" to `/metodika#planuojamos-datos`), or, for a moderator's date, that
  a moderator corrected it; and a link "Darbotvarkė lrs.lt" to the agenda page the date was read from (the address of
  the watcher's last good read, kept with each snapshot), shown only for an https address. The bill's own e-seimas link
  is its document, which shows no date, so the page does not offer it as the source;
- a moderator can correct the planned date or stage, **hold** sending for a bill (for example while a surge is checked),
  or approve one **urgent summary** for a bill that reached the agenda at short notice (one per bill a week), with a
  fresh staff-passkey confirmation (6.2). An urgent summary goes at once, still goes to all MPs, still shows both sides,
  and may exceed the weekly ceiling by one.

People flag what is wrong and a moderator makes the change, because a mistaken send to 141 MPs cannot be recalled.
Nobody but a moderator controls timing.

**What an MP sees.** One email for all bills, most relevant first: bills on the agenda in the coming days (by date),
then the rest by participation. Bills where the MP's committee leads would come first, but the import does not know a
bill's lead committee, so that group is always empty. For each bill:

- number, official title, stage, "Planuojama …" with the date (never a countdown);
- how many ask to vote for and how many against, with the counts for accounts older than 30 days;
- when at least `Digest:PartyGroupMinimum` participants on a side (10 by default; 0 = any number of one or more) said
  they support the MP's party: those numbers. Taking part is private: nobody can see who took a side at all, and no list
  ties a side to a person inside the system, so a small group number reveals little from within the system. The rule is
  a pure function (`GroupRules`), the value is read when the summary goes (8.19.2), and the email and the stored
  delivery row carry what it allowed then; the public copy carries no party numbers;
- the two most endorsed published arguments per side, with their "Man svarbu" counts and without their authors' names;
  only counted marks choose and are shown, those of people asking for that argument's side (8.2): chosen when the
  summary is prepared, each count read when it goes (8.19.2); the same in the email, the public copy and the stored
  snapshot, so a summary holds no person. A side with no published argument says so. An argument longer than about 300
  characters plus 100 is shortened in the email at a word boundary, as on the page, with "…" and a plain line "Visas
  argumentas: {link}" to that argument on the summary's public copy; the snapshot keeps the whole text and the public
  copy shows it behind "Rodyti daugiau" (8.19.3);
- a surge note when one applies;
- the MP's own published response ("Jūsų atsakymas: …" with its date), or a link to answer once for everyone;
- a link to the bill's page with all the numbers and arguments.

Every email ends with: what the numbers are and are not (self-selected users, not a poll), a link to the public copy of
the summary, a link to change the frequency or stop, and one sentence on taking over the profile (8.19.3). A sample is in
appendix A.

**Deliverability and trust.**

- MP mail has its own sender address and may have its own SMTP account, and its own daily ceiling, so a problem with it
  cannot block sign-in codes (8.19.3).
- Plain, light HTML with a text part. No tracking pixels and no click tracking: an MP's office must be able to trust the
  email, and we do not need to know who opened it.
- `List-Unsubscribe` with one-click, plus the preferences link.
- A permanent refusal by the receiving server puts the address on the suppression list automatically (8.19.6).
- Before the first summary is really sent, each MP receives one **introduction letter** written and signed by a person:
  what the summaries are, how often they come, how to stop them, how to take over the profile (8.19.5).
- **Not built.** A dedicated sending subdomain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC at `reject`, and adding complaints to the
  suppression list, wait for an email provider (8.19.6).

**Preferences without an account (8.19.4).** The link in each email opens a page for that MP: default (weekly and
before votes), weekly only, before votes only, or stopped; and an extra recipient such as an assistant, confirmed from
the official address. The link's token allows only this page and the one-click stop. Whether an MP has stopped the
summaries is not shown publicly; the public copy says only how many MPs a summary was sent to.
