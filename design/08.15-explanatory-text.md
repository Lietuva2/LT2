# 8.15 Explanatory text on screens

- **What a screen shows without being asked:** its title, at most one short paragraph that says what this is, the
  fields with their labels, and the action. Every other explanation is one click away.
- **One click away means one of three places.** A hint (below), for a term or a number. On the same page, collapsed
  behind a short link that names what opens ("Kaip tai veikia", "Ką reiškia ši žyma?"; a bare "Plačiau" only where it
  is the only one on the screen). Or on the methodology page (`/metodika`) or the moderation rules, reached by a short
  link to the part that says it; the part exists and has an `id`. A text that explains how the site works in general
  belongs on the methodology page and is not repeated on screens.
- **What stays inline, always:** what becomes public and under whose name; what cannot be undone; a consent and what
  it covers; the notice that the data is invented; "this is not a representative poll" wherever the numbers are;
  that summaries are a trial and nothing is sent; status and error messages; labels; a field's hint when it is needed
  to fill the field (a format, a limit); and what this design requires beside a piece of data (a description's source
  label and date, the count of accounts older than 30 days, a moderator's statement).
- **One pattern for collapsed text:** the browser's own `<details>` with the class `more` and a link-looking summary
  (`<details class="more stack stack--tight"><summary class="linkish small">…</summary>…</details>`). Opening it needs
  no script, so it works on server-rendered pages, with JavaScript off, and inside an island before it starts. An island
  never sets its open state from code. When an island starts it replaces the prerendered markup, which would close what
  the person had opened; one small script (`lt2-disclosures.js`, on every page) remembers which disclosures
  (`details.more`, and the action row's `details.discuss`, 8.16.1) were opened and opens them again. It remembers for
  the page being read only (emptied whenever the path changes), so coming back to a page opens nothing of an earlier
  visit; and Back or Forward keeps the reader's place while Blazor patches the page in (`lt2-return.js`, in memory
  only).
- **The methodology page holds what is not on the screens,** among it the parts "Planuojamos datos"
  (`#planuojamos-datos`) and "Kas matoma viešai, o kas ne" (`#privatumas`), what the representatives and the
  organisation codes do not reveal in "Organizacijų pozicijos", the rows "Kas gautų" and "Vieša kopija" in the summary
  rules, the row "Riba" (`/metodika#riba`), and a lead that describes the whole site.
- **New screens follow the rule.** A text that explains instead of labels goes behind a link, into a hint or to the
  methodology page from the start.
- **A hint: the short answer without leaving the page.** It is the first place to use for a term or a number:
  - **What it is:** a word or a short question on the screen ("Kas yra riba?", "Kas yra „Man svarbu“?") that opens a
    small panel beside it with the answer in one or two sentences and, only where there is more to say, a link
    "Plačiau" to the part of the methodology page or the rules. The answer says what the thing is and what it does
    first, and a number after it.
  - **How it opens:** on hover and on keyboard focus after a short delay, and on a press or a tap, which also keeps
    it open; it closes on Escape (the focus going back to the trigger), on a press elsewhere, on a second press of the
    trigger and when the pointer and the focus have left both the word and the panel. The pointer can move into the
    panel and use its link. On a phone a tap opens it and a tap elsewhere closes it; the panel never reaches beyond the
    screen's edges.
  - **How it is built:** a button and the browser's own popover (`Hint`), so a press opens it with JavaScript off too
    (the panel then appears in the middle of the screen); one small script (`lt2-hints.js`) adds the hover, the delay
    and the place beside the word. One component for every hint; an island never opens it from code. A hint is
    phrasing content and may stand inside a paragraph. Its trigger looks different from a link that leaves the page (a
    dotted underline), and screen readers are told it opens a description.
  - **What goes where:** a term or a number whose answer fits two sentences gets a hint; a longer explanation, a list of
    steps or a form stays collapsed on the page (`details.more`); how the site works in general stays on the
    methodology page. No link leaves the page only to explain a term. In a list, the hint is said once beside the list,
    not in every row.
  - **A number that is a setting is said as one.** A value an administrator or a moderator can change (the threshold
    above all) is worded as what holds now, never as a fixed fact: "Dabar – 100". A bill with its own threshold shows
    that bill's number in the hint; in a list, the hint says the general number and that a row shows its bill's own.
  - **The threshold's answer:** "Riba – dalyvių skaičius (abi pusės kartu), kurį pasiekęs projektas gali patekti į
    suvestines Seimo nariams. Dabar – {n}." ("gali patekti", because reaching the threshold only makes a bill eligible;
    a summary holds it by the rules of 8.4, and a moderator's hold keeps it out.) The methodology's row "Riba" starts
    with the same sentence, then says that the administrator can change the number and that a moderator can set
    another for a bill, not lower than the minimum.
- **One exception:** the front page's introduction for a visitor (8.7). A new person has to learn what the site is, so
  it may be as long as that takes, in short headed steps, not a wall of text.
- The start shown to a member who has not taken a side yet falls under the same exception (8.28.3).
