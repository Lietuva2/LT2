# 8.3 Moderation

Nothing waits for approval before it is shown, except arguments that pre-screening flags or people report (8.12.3).

- Queue: arguments flagged by pre-screening or by reports, or not yet seen; bills' records that were reported (a report
  on a bill is about its description, 8.13), flagged about their date, surging (6.6) or held; requests for an urgent
  summary; bills under their threshold whose bill has reached the agenda, only when they have at least one participant
  (8.12.2); and comments with an open report (8.23.8; a comment is never in the queue otherwise). A closed record and
  its arguments come into the queue only when reported (6.9). A bill's record in the queue leads with the bill's
  number and its title as in every list (8.13.1), linked to the bill's page. Each note on an item says what happened and what the
  moderator can do (for example: the bill is on the Seimas agenda, planned for a date; it has n of its threshold's
  participants and so will not enter a summary; the moderator may approve one urgent summary, or do nothing).
  Each of the queue's lists shows at most a hundred items, oldest first, with how many wait in all; an item handled
  leaves the list, so the next come up. None of them is paged.
- **The queue's page is tabs** (`/moderavimas`, owner, 2026-11-25): one page, one tab per list, each list loaded only
  when its tab is opened. The tab is in the address (`?skiltis=`), so a refresh, the back button and a link keep it.
  Two groups in one row, a gap and a thin `--rule` line between them:
  - **work**, in this order: Projektai (`projektai`, the bills' records), Argumentai (`argumentai`), Organizacijos
    (`organizacijos`), Pozicijos (`pozicijos`, organisations' statements), Atsakymai (`atsakymai`, MPs' responses),
    Profiliai (`profiliai`), Komentarai (`komentarai`). Each shows how many wait in a small neutral filled chip, only
    when something waits; an empty tab has none;
  - **reference**: Be aprašymo (`be-aprasymo`, below), Paslėpti (`paslepti`, the list of hidden items) and Apribojimai
    (`apribojimai`, writing restrictions). Their counts are plain muted text
    in brackets ("Be aprašymo (240)"), never a chip.

  The counts come first, in one moderators-only answer (`GET /api/v1/moderation/counts`, only numbers), each counted
  by its own query with the same filter as its list, not by reading the lists; a tab's count
  goes down without a reload when an action takes an item out of its list. Without `?skiltis=` the page opens on the
  first work tab that has something waiting, or on Projektai when none has; never on a reference tab. A narrow screen
  scrolls the tab row sideways, the groups kept apart; a wide one wraps it when it does not fit, the reference tabs on the
  next line, with no rule at a line's start or end. The summaries, feedback and the agenda's entry stay pages of their
  own, linked above the tabs.
- **Entering the agenda** ("Seimo darbotvarkė", `/moderavimas/darbotvarke`; 8.4, "Entering a day's agenda"): one form
  for one sitting day: the day (a text field `yyyy-MM-dd` with `DatePick`), the agenda document's address, the bills as
  lines (a plain text box, no `TextLayoutHint`: it is not a text for readers), "Peržiūrėti" and, once a preview is shown
  for exactly that text, "Įrašyti". The preview is a list of the bills (number, stage, title, "bus sukurtas" for a bill
  new to the site) and the day's counts (added, stage changed, unchanged, taken off the day, with the numbers of those
  taken off); refused lines are listed with their line number and why. Under the form, the days from today that have
  entered rows, each with how many bills, when it was last saved and its document. Moderators and administrators in a
  staff-passkey session.
- **Bills without a description** ("Projektai be aprašymo", the queue's tab `be-aprasymo`; owner, 2026-11-25): moderators cannot write every bill's description, so this list says where one is most wanted. It holds
  active bills (an open record or none) without a description, or with an AI summary nobody has read (8.13), that
  people already pay attention to: at least one participant, published argument or follower. A bill nobody touched is
  not on it, whatever its planned date. The order is the sum of participants, published arguments and followers,
  highest first; then the soonest planned date (none last), then the number. Each row: the number, the title as in
  every list (8.13.1), the three counts, the planned date when there is one, the label of an AI summary, and the
  bill's page as the link, where "Rašyti aprašymą" is (8.12.3). At most a hundred rows, with how many there are in all,
  like the queue; a bill described leaves it. It shows only numbers already public on the bill's page (8.11.2, 8.12.1),
  nothing about who. Moderators only (`GET /api/v1/moderation/undescribed`).
- **Pre-screening** flags an email address, a phone number, a personal code and a link (comments are refused for the
  first three instead, 8.23.3). An argument with fewer words than the runtime setting `Appeals:MinReasonWords` (3 by
  default, 0 = off; a word is a run of letters or digits, `ReasonWords`) is too short: the writing form first asks in
  place ("Argumentas labai trumpas. Parašykite, kodėl prašote balsuoti už ar prieš – tokį argumentą kiti galės pažymėti
  „Man svarbu“.", "Papildyti" back to the text, "Vis tiek skelbti"); posted anyway, the server flags it `too-short`
  ("Per trumpas" in the queue) and it waits for a moderator like any flagged argument. Only arguments: a short comment
  is normal. An edit re-screens the new text in full (its flags are the new text's), and a waiting argument keeps
  waiting until a moderator decides, whatever the edit; the edit form does not ask first.
- Actions on a bill, each with a statement and audited: write or correct the description (on any bill, a finished one too, 8.13),
  confirm an AI summary nobody has read (an optional note) or remove a description (8.13), set the bill's own threshold,
  correct the planned date or stage, hold or release sending, approve one urgent summary, keep a bill as it is
  ("Palikti"), close a bill the Seimas rejected or its initiators withdrew and undo that close (8.1 step
  6), hide and restore. On arguments: publish, hide, restore, merge into another (endorsements
  move with it), restrict the author from writing or lift that (below). "Palikti" on a published argument (the publish
  action, which marks it seen) is offered only when it does something: it has an open report, or no moderator has seen
  it yet (keeping it then marks it seen and takes it out of the queue); a seen argument without a report does not offer
  it. Comments offer it only when reported; the queues of statements and responses hold only items it answers.
- **"Palikti" on a bill** (owner, 2026-11-26) answers every reason the bill is in the queue except a hold, and is offered
  only while one of them holds: open reports and date flags close; a surge is cleared, and only a surge counted from
  positions taken after it brings the bill back; under its threshold on the agenda, the bill is kept for that planned
  date and stage and comes back only when either changes, or when it reaches half its threshold after being kept below
  that. A held bill stays in the queue until sending is released ("Atnaujinti siuntimą"); holding is a choice. The bill's
  page and the summaries show a surge as before: keeping changes only the queue.
- **When the agenda watcher cannot read the Seimas page** (no sitting date found, or no bill while planned items exist)
  the run is recorded as failed and logged as an error for the alerting, and the planned dates stay as they were (6.8);
  a moderator corrects a planned date by hand ("Pataisyti planuojamą datą"), so nothing is sent on a wrongly read date.
- **On a closed record** a moderator may only keep it when reported, correct or remove its description, hide, restore
  or keep its arguments, and undo a close a moderator made (rejected or withdrawn, never the Seimas's vote, 8.1 step 6);
  publishing a waiting argument, merging and the other actions on a bill are refused (`moderation.wrong-state`, 6.9).
  Closing is offered on an open record only (again `moderation.wrong-state` otherwise). On a hidden record only restoring
  it is allowed.
- **On the bill's page.** A moderator or an administrator in a staff-passkey session sees, on the page of a bill whose
  record is open (also a bill nobody has taken part in) or closed, a "Moderavimas" panel with the actions that apply to
  it in its state ("Palikti" only while a report or date flag is open; on a closed record those above, "Atšaukti
  uždarymą" among them), and "Moderuoti" on each argument (8.12.3). A hidden record's page shows neither: it is restored
  from the list of hidden items. The state comes from a moderators-only answer
  (`GET /api/v1/moderation/bills/{number}`); nobody else sees the panel or anything from that answer. After an action
  the page shows the result without reloading: the panel and the page's other parts read again in place.
- **On a vote's page.** A vote's classification is corrected there (7.2), with the same "Moderavimas" part, menu and
  statement; votes never enter the queue.
- **The screen.** On every moderation screen alike (the queue, the hidden items, the bill's panel, "Moderuoti" under an
  argument, a statement, a response or a comment, organisations and profiles): **one filled button for what the item
  needs most in its state**, the reason it is there ("Palikti" on a bill while a report, a date flag, a surge or being
  under its threshold on the agenda holds, and on other items while a report is open; else "Atnaujinti siuntimą" on a
  held bill; "Patvirtinti aprašymą" while an AI summary nobody has read is shown and nothing above holds; "Skelbti" or "Paskelbti" for a waiting argument or
  statement; "Atkurti" on a hidden comment or profile text; otherwise none), and beside it **"Kiti veiksmai"**
  ("Veiksmai" when there is no filled button) opening the rest as a menu. The menu is the browser's own popover, as the
  action row's "⋯" (8.16.1): it opens before the island runs and its trigger is never disabled, while its items stay
  disabled until then like any island control; a press elsewhere or Escape closes it. A bill's record groups its items
  under small headings: **Aprašymas** ("Rašyti aprašymą" or "Taisyti aprašymą", "Pašalinti aprašymą", "Patvirtinti
  aprašymą" when it is not the filled button), **Seime** ("Pataisyti planuojamą datą", "Keisti ribą", "Uždaryti:
  projektas atmestas ar atsiimtas"; on a record closed for rejection or withdrawal, "Atšaukti uždarymą"), **Suvestinės**
  ("Sulaikyti siuntimą" or "Atnaujinti siuntimą" when it is not the filled button, "Patvirtinti skubią suvestinę"), then, set apart after a rule,
  **"Paslėpti"**. An argument, a comment, a statement, a response, an organisation and a profile have one kind of action
  and no headings: merge and restrict the author, then hiding (or blocking an organisation) set apart at the end, and after it,
  for an administrator only, the lock of the author's account ("Užrakinti autoriaus paskyrą", below).
  Choosing an item closes the menu and opens only that action's fields below, where the row stood, with one sentence on
  what it does, and the focus moves to the form's heading; one action is open at a time per item; only the actions that
  apply to the item's state are offered; the confirmation stays visible after the action closes. The fields: keep, an
  optional note; hide, a required statement; threshold, the number and a statement; planned date, the date, the stage
  and a statement (the stage is asked only here); hold or release, a statement (while held, the bill enters no summary
  to MPs); urgent summary, a statement (one extra summary outside the schedule for this bill, used when the vote comes
  before the next regular summary; at most once per bill per week) and, when the staff passkey was last confirmed more
  than ten minutes before, the passkey again on the same press (6.2), the statement kept; description, the text (starting from the shown
  one), the AI draft from pasted official text when a provider is configured (8.11.4), and a required statement;
  confirm (only an AI summary nobody has read), an optional statement; remove the description, a required statement; close
  (8.1 step 6), what happened (the radio "Seimas projektą atmetė" or "Iniciatoriai projektą atsiėmė"), the day of that
  decision (a `yyyy-MM-dd` text field with a calendar button, not in the future), the source (required: https on `lrs.lt` or a host under it,
  at most 500 characters, the rule of the description's "Oficialaus teksto adresas") and a required statement, with the
  sentence that the counts freeze, nobody can take or change a side, the page shows what happened with the day and the
  source, and followers are told; undo the close, a required statement (the record is open again with its live counts;
  nobody is told); for reasons: publish, hide, merge (choosing the target), restrict the author.
  Each explanation says who reads it: the author by email, "Paaiškinimas autoriui (privalomas)" (an argument's hiding,
  merging and restoring, a comment's, a member's "Apie mane", restoring an MP's response; an organisation's actions name
  its representatives, "Paaiškinimas (bus išsiųstas organizacijos atstovams)"); everyone on a page, "Viešas paaiškinimas
  (privalomas)" with the page under it (hiding a bill's record and its own threshold, "Rodomas projekto puslapyje.";
  hiding an MP's response, "Rodomas atsakymo vietoje."; blocking an organisation, "Rodomas organizacijos puslapyje. Jį gaus
  ir organizacijos atstovai el. paštu."; writing, correcting and removing a description, "Rodomas keitimų istorijoje. Jį
  el. paštu gaus ir apie aprašymą pranešę nariai.", and confirming one, "Viešas paaiškinimas (nebūtinas)" with the same
  line, 8.13; owner, 2026-11-25); or staff only, "Paaiškinimas moderatoriams (privalomas)" with "Mato tik moderatoriai ir
  administratoriai." (closing and undoing the close, the planned date, holding and releasing, an urgent summary,
  restoring a record, a vote's classification). A description's statement is a typed text (8.2), so its box carries the
  layout hint. Where no author receives it, a missing one is refused "Parašykite paaiškinimą."; where one does,
  "Parašykite paaiškinimą autoriui." (owner, 2026-11-22).
- **A list's length.** Each list of the queue (bills' records, arguments, organisations, statements, MPs' responses,
  reported comments) and of hidden responses and comments shows at most its first hundred in its own order (the queue
  the oldest first, the hidden ones the latest hidden first); when more wait it says so above the items, "Rodoma 100 iš
  240. Kiti rodomi, kai šie bus sutvarkyti.", so a cut list is never read as the whole. The answers carry the total
  beside the items. The hidden bills and the hidden arguments each show the latest 200 hidden, without a total.
- **Hiding a bill's record closes taking part on it (8.12.3).** The page shows the bill and says that taking part is
  closed by the moderators, with the statement. The sides and arguments are kept: nobody can see them, and keeping them
  lets a mistaken hide be undone. There is one record per bill, so hiding does not free the bill for another.
- **Restoring.** Moderators have a list of hidden bills (bill, when, who hid it, the statement) and of hidden reasons.
  "Atkurti" brings a bill's record back as it was, open (or closed, if it had closed) with its sides, reasons and marks.
  A statement is required and the action is audited. There is one record per bill, so nothing else on the bill can block
  a restore.
  - Hidden reasons have the same list and "Atkurti", while their bill's record is shown (open or closed; not while the
    record is hidden). A restored reason comes back without its "Man svarbu" marks, which hiding deleted (they do not
    count towards anyone's limit), and its author is told.
- **Restricting an account from writing.** With the per-person limits switched off (8.2), this is the tool for the one
  person who misuses that. A moderator restricts the author of a reason, reached from that reason in the queue or on the
  bill's page (and the writer of a comment or a statement from that text, 8.23.8, 8.14.14), either until a date
  (inclusive, Vilnius time) or until lifted; any moderator or administrator may set any length and lift it, each only in
  a staff-passkey session (6.2) and each with a written statement. One restriction holds at a time
  (`restriction.exists`): to change its length, a moderator lifts it and sets a new one. A dated restriction ends by
  itself when it is checked on use; no job runs.
  - It blocks everything the person writes for others to read: writing and editing a reason, flags about planned dates,
    organisations' statements, MPs' responses, changing the display name, comments and the "Apie mane" text (8.14.14,
    8.5.5, 6.2, 8.23.4, 8.25); not "Sutinku" on a comment, which puts a name, not a text, beside it (8.23.10). The API
    refuses these with `writing.restricted`; the pages show, in place of the writing box or form, that writing is
    restricted, until when, and the reason.
  - It does not touch signing in, taking or changing a side, "Man svarbu", reporting, consent, export, deleting the
    account or sessions, and never changes a count. The person's earlier reasons stay; hiding one is its own action.
  - The person is told by email (outbox, `email.writing-restriction`): the statement, the end date or "until lifted",
    and the moderators' contact address for objecting (there is no objection form in the product). Lifting early tells
    them too.
  - It is not public: no page, API answer or summary shows anyone else that an account is restricted, and the person's
    reasons look the same as everyone's.
  - When restricting, the moderator sees the display name, that person's reasons, and earlier moderation actions on
    them, earlier restrictions included; never the address, the person's side or a mark. The account is never named by
    its id: the moderator's screens reach it through the reason, and a restriction has its own id for lifting.
  - Setting and lifting are recorded like every moderation action (moderator, target, statement, end date) and in
    `audit`, with nothing about sides.
  - Deleting the account deletes its restrictions with everything else. No hash of the address is kept: a person can
    register with another address anyway.
- **A full lock of an account.** The restriction above leaves signing in alone; when that is not enough (an account that keeps
  misusing the site), an **administrator**, never a moderator, locks the account (a required statement, a staff-passkey session
  with a fresh confirmation, 6.2), in either of two ways. **From the item**: wherever a moderator can restrict the author (an
  argument, a comment, an organisation's statement, from that text) and on a reported profile, an administrator's "Kiti
  veiksmai" also holds **"Užrakinti autoriaus paskyrą"**, last of all, after hiding (the heaviest action, set apart with it); a
  moderator who is not an administrator does not see it and the server refuses it. It opens a form with the sentence below and
  the statement; the server finds the person from the item (kind and id; for a statement, the person who wrote it, as the
  restriction does), so no address is typed, sent or shown. An MP's response is not among the places (the restriction does not
  reach it), and a closed bill's argument offers hiding only, so no lock. **By address**, the secondary way on the "Paskyros"
  screen (`/administravimas/paskyros`, the account item's "Paskyros"), for when the account's owner wrote to the team from their
  address: the address typed, "Jei paskyros savininkas parašė komandai iš savo adreso". The screen's first list is of the locked
  accounts (below). It is one transaction: the account's mark `locked_at` is set with the statement, every session of the
  account ends and its open sign-in codes are used up. While it stands:
  - no sign-in works, and every answer is the one for an address nobody holds (6.2): a code request answers 202 and sends
    nothing, a typed code is refused as any wrong code (`auth.code-invalid`), a passkey as any failed assertion
    (`passkey.invalid`); nothing says whether an address is registered or locked;
  - the account's public profile (8.25) is not shown: its page and API answer 410 as for a profile switched off, its name
    beside texts is no link, it cannot be reported or followed; the profile switch itself is left as it was, so unlocking
    brings it back;
  - the account's follows send nothing (8.24); its texts, sides and marks stay as they are, and hiding a text is the
    moderators' separate action; the moderators' statements of reasons and the reporters' notices (8.3, below) still reach it,
    since the Digital Services Act asks for them;
  - the person gets one email (`email.account-notice`, `account-locked`, through `EmailLayout`) with the statement, the way to
    write to the team (`Moderation:ContactAddress` when set) and that, since a locked person cannot sign in and so cannot
    delete the account, a request to erase the account and its data is made by writing to the team.
  An administrator unlocks it with a statement (`account-unlocked`, one email; the person signs in as before): by "Atrakinti"
  beside the account in the screen's list of locked accounts, or by address. The list shows each locked account's display name
  ("Lietuva 2.0 naudotojas" when it has none), the time it was locked and the statement given, newest first, and never an address
  or anything else of the person; it is read by administrators only, and the statement is kept with the mark and cleared when
  unlocked.
  A staff account cannot be locked while it holds an active staff role (`account.lock-staff`: reset it first, 6.2), and an
  administrator cannot lock their own account (`account.lock-self`). Locking a locked account and unlocking one that is not
  locked are refused (`account.locked`, `account.not-locked`). Both actions are audited (`account.locked`,
  `account.unlocked`: the administrator, the statement, how many sessions ended; the target is the account's id, never an
  address); the screen names no account beyond the address the administrator typed and the names in its list, and a moderator's
  screens show no lock.
- Every action is logged in `audit`. An argument's author receives the statement of reasons by email; a bill's record has
  no author, so actions on it are emailed to nobody (8.12.3). Closing a rejected or withdrawn bill is not a moderation
  notice: its followers hear that the bill was rejected or withdrawn by the follow email, as of a vote (8.6), without the
  statement or the moderator.
- A report button is on every argument, on a bill's description, and on organisations, statements, responses, comments
  and a member's "Apie mane" (8.14.14, 8.5.5, 8.23.8, 8.25.6; Digital Services Act notice-and-action applies to any
  hosting service, however small). Never on one's own text: an argument or a comment one wrote, a comment or statement
  of an organisation one represents, a response or comment of the MP one speaks for, one's own profile; the menu leaves
  it out (from the caller's own never-cached answer, once the island runs) and the server refuses it where reports are
  written (`report.own`: "Savo teksto pranešti negalima."). A moderator or administrator in a staff-passkey session
  sees only the moderators' actions in a text's "⋯", not "Pranešti"; outside a staff session they are a member like any
  other.
- **The reporter is told the decision (Digital Services Act art. 16(5)).** When a moderator decides on reported content
  (keeps it, hides it, merges it, corrects or removes a description, blocks an organisation or withdraws its
  confirmation), every member whose report on it was open gets one email through the outbox (`email.report-decided`,
  `ReportDecidedTemplate` through `EmailLayout.Build` with the footer), for every report target: argument, comment,
  organisation's statement, MP's response, description, organisation, profile "Apie mane". Subject "Jūsų pranešimas
  išnagrinėtas"; the text by its kind and bill ("argumentą projekte {numeris} „{pavadinimas}“"), or an organisation's or
  a profile's public name; what became of it; the moderator's explanation whenever there is one (the statement of a
  hiding, the same public-facing statement the author got, or the optional note of "Palikti", whose field reads
  "Paaiškinimas pranešusiems (nebūtinas)" with the hint "Jį gaus pranešimą pateikę nariai el. paštu."); a link while
  the text still stands; the moderation rules. Never the moderator's name or id, never other reporters, nothing of the
  author beyond what is public. A text its author withdraws (an argument, a comment, a statement) tells its reporters
  "Turinį atsiėmė pats autorius", and a comment that goes with the text it stood under (withdrawn or merged) tells its
  reporters so. Reports closed by an account's deletion (the reporter's or the author's) send nothing. Hiding or
  restoring a whole bill's record answers the reports on its description the same way.
- **The author sees their own hidden text.** On the bill's page the author of a hidden argument or comment, and the
  representatives of an organisation whose statement is hidden, see it in its place (an argument in its side's column, a
  comment under its text, a statement in the organisation's own panel), quiet (greyed, dashed), with "Paslėpė
  moderatorius: {paaiškinimas}" (the statement already emailed to them) and "Jį matote tik jūs."; no actions, since a
  hidden text is the moderators' to restore. Nobody else sees it and the served HTML is the same for everyone: the text
  and the statement come only from the caller's own never-cached answer (`MyBillRecordResponse.HiddenStatements` with
  the hidden reasons in `MyReasons`, `MyCommentsResponse.Hidden`, `MyBillOrganisationResponse.HiddenStatement`), drawn
  once the island runs. A text shown nowhere because its whole record or its target was hidden follows the target: the
  record's hidden page shows the author nothing of it, and a comment under a hidden text is not shown. An MP's hidden
  response shows its statement in its place to everyone (8.5.5).
- **A summary holds published arguments, seen by a moderator or not.** A person still passes everything that reaches
  MPs: each summary is prepared a day ahead and the moderators review it, hiding an argument there with the normal
  action (8.19.2).
- Rules for moderators are public, like the statistics methodology.
