# 8.13 What the bill is about: the description, its AI summary and the readable title

An AI summary may be shown without a person reading it first, labelled so.

- **One description per bill**, at most 3 000 characters (owner, 2026-11-25: 1 500 proved too short for what the
  documents say), on the bill's page under "Apie ką projektas", kept on the bill's record (8.12.1) with
  its history (`revision`). The history page, "Aprašymo keitimų istorija" (`/seimas/{n}/istorija`, linked as
  "Keitimų istorija"), lists the edits newest first; each shows what it changed against the text before it, word by
  word, with the text before the edit whole under "Rodyti tekstą prieš pakeitimą", a first text whole and a removal
  struck through (8.16.2). A moderator's entry also shows a change of the official text's address ("Oficialus tekstas,
  iš kurio parengta: A → B", "nenurodytas" for none), a confirmation (" · aprašymas patvirtintas be pakeitimų") and
  the moderator's public statement under "Paaiškinimas", laid out as typed (8.2): what they read, what they left out
  and why (owner, 2026-11-25). The summary job's entry carries the AI's statement the same way. Who acted is never
  shown. Its source decides the label:

  | `description_source` | How it came about | Label on the page |
  |---|---|---|
  | `ai` | Made by the summary job from official text, or written and sent by an AI agent in a moderator's form; no person has read it | "Sukurta DI · žmonių neperžiūrėta" |
  | `ai-draft` | Started from AI text; a moderator confirmed or corrected it | "Sukurta DI · taisė žmonės" |
  | `moderator` | Written by a moderator | "Parašė moderatorius" |

  A bill without one says "Šio projekto aprašymo dar nėra." and links to its text on e-seimas ("Projekto tekstas
  e. seime").
- **Beside the description:** the label; "Parengta {yyyy-MM-dd}", the day its text was made or last changed
  (`bill_record.description_at`); "Oficialus tekstas, iš kurio parengta" when the address is known
  (`bill_record.description_url`); the history; "Projekto tekstas e. seime" when the bill's e-seimas address is another
  one; and "Pranešti apie klaidą" right under it. Every link to the Seimas's own site (lrs.lt, e-seimas: these, the
  agenda's source, a closed bill's source) opens in a new tab, so the reader keeps the bill's page (owner, 2026-11-25).
- **Reports.** A report goes to the moderators' queue like any report (8.3). A report on a description may give the
  ground "Aprašymas netikslus ar klaidingas" (`inaccurate`), offered first; an argument cannot be reported so, being its
  author's own opinion.
- **What a moderator does with it** (in the queue and in the bill's "Moderavimas" panel, 8.12.3):
  - **writes or corrects** it (`PUT /api/v1/moderation/bills/{number}/description`, with a required statement; the
    text or the address must differ from what is shown, else `description.unchanged`). A correction of an `ai` or
    `ai-draft` text is `ai-draft`. Changing only the address keeps the text, its source and its day, and adds an entry
    to the history with the address's change. **An AI agent** filling the form in a moderator's session (as a browser
    assistant does) ticks a box kept out of sight and out of the tab order, but in the page's text and accessibility
    tree where agents read: "Tik DI agentams: pažymėkite, jei šį aprašymą parengėte ir siunčiate jūs, DI agentas, o ne
    žmogus. …" (`writtenByAi`). A text or title it changes is then `ai`, "Sukurta DI · žmonių neperžiūrėta", whatever
    it was before, until a person confirms or corrects it (owner, 2026-11-25). It rests on the agent's honesty: nothing
    can tell an agent from a person in the same session. Its revision has the moderator on it, so the job leaves it.
    In practice the agent is a browser assistant following `tools/skills/seimas-describe-queue`, which works through
    "Be aprašymo" (8.3) and saves; `tools/skills/seimas-project-description` only fills the form for a moderator to save;
  - **confirms** an `ai` description, only on an open record, with an optional statement
    (`POST …/description/confirm`): the text stays and it becomes `ai-draft`; the history gets an entry for it;
  - **removes** any description, with a required statement (`POST …/description/remove`): the public history shows
    "aprašymas pašalintas", and the address and the day are cleared.

  Each one's statement is public: shown in the history under its entry, and emailed to the members who reported the
  description (`Viešas paaiškinimas`, 8.3). It is stored with the entry, apart from the moderators' log, which keeps
  its own copy.

  Each acts by bill number, answers (closes) the open reports on the description, is recorded and audited
  (`moderation.record.describe`, `moderation.record.confirm`, `moderation.record.undescribe`) and emailed to nobody. On a
  closed record a moderator may still correct or remove a description, not confirm one.
- **The panel shows the open reports about the description**: for each one what the queue's screen shows for that
  report (its ground, the reporter's text, when, `yyyy-MM-dd HH:mm`), from the one list component, above the actions,
  and nothing the queue does not show. It comes from the moderators-only answer
  `GET /api/v1/moderation/bills/{number}` (`descriptionReports`, and `descriptionUrl` for the form); nobody else
  receives it. Reports on arguments stay with their arguments.
- **The official text's address.** The description form (in the panel and in the queue) has an optional field
  "Oficialaus teksto adresas", filled in advance with the address the record has; on a bill without a description, with
  the bill's e-seimas address when the field would accept it (owner, 2026-11-25), which the moderator replaces when they
  worked from another text (the explanatory note). A description without an address keeps the field empty, so correcting
  its text adds no address nobody chose. Accepted: empty, or an `https`
  address whose host is `lrs.lt` or under it (`e-seimas.lrs.lt`, `www.lrs.lt`), at most 500 characters, with no user
  name or password in it; anything else is refused with a message at the field (`description-url.invalid`). Both forms
  check it in the browser with the same validator as the endpoint, and the module checks the same rule again (a copy,
  held to the validator by a unit test), so no other way of saving skips it. It is stored trimmed; what the field holds
  becomes the record's `description_url`, and an empty field, only spaces or a request without the field clears it. A
  moderator correcting an AI summary keeps its address by leaving the field alone. Confirming keeps the address,
  removing clears it. The address is not personal data and is in the audit details as `descriptionUrl`.
- **"Touched by a person"** is read from the public history: a description revision with a person on it (written,
  corrected or removed). The job writes its revision without a person. After a person has touched a description the job
  never writes it again, a removed one included; a confirmed text is a description, so the job leaves it too.
- **What an AI summary is:** two to four plain Lithuanian sentences on what the official text says the bill changes.
  No evaluation, no arguments for or against, nothing that is not in the source; when the source is not enough, no
  summary is stored. With it come the readable title (8.13.1), the document it rests on and a public statement for the
  history, in the answer's fixed form (8.11.4). The model is `Ai:DraftModel` (`claude-sonnet-5-5`).
- **The job's prompt** is `src/Modules/Lt2.Appeals/Ai/description-summary.prompt.md`, embedded in the module beside the
  moderator's draft prompt (`description-draft.prompt.md`, 8.11.4); neither has a route of its own ("public" means in
  the repository). A request carries its purpose (`DraftSource.Purpose`), which picks the file: the job sends the
  summary's, the moderator's form the draft's. The two have the same placeholders, the same rules and the same
  one-sentence "not enough" answer, "Iš pateikto teksto neaišku, ką projektas keičia.", and differ in two places:
  - the opening says what the text is for (the prompts are in English, the answer in Lithuanian): "You are preparing a
    brief, neutral description of a Seimas bill for Lietuva 2.0. Visitors will see the description immediately without
    human review.";
  - one more rule, right after the "not enough" rule: "If you are unsure whether the source makes clear what the bill
    changes, write only that same sentence. An inaccurate description is worse than no description."
- **What is sent to the provider:** the bill's number and title and the official documents, as in 8.11.4. No personal
  data, nothing users wrote.
- **The summary job** (`DescriptionSummaryJob`, worker) runs hourly at :40 (Vilnius time) and once at start-up:
  - it takes active bills without a description (no record, or an open one), those with a planned date first (soonest
    first), then those with the most participants, then by number; at most `Ai:SummariesPerDay` a Vilnius day (default
    30; 0 switches the job off), counting the tries that asked the provider (an unavailable text costs nothing);
  - it gets the official documents through one interface, `IBillTextSource`: the bill's explanatory note and its text,
    each with its address;
  - a bill none of whose documents can be read, or fit within 20 000 characters (8.11.4: one that would pass the limit
    is left out, not cut), waits seven days; so does a bill whose answer is the "not enough" sentence, and nothing is
    stored. A refusal, an answer that is not a valid description, or a provider error is retried the next day. One row
    per bill in `appeals.summary_attempt` (5.3) holds the last try, how it ended and when the next may come;
  - a summary is stored under the bill's lock as the description with the source `ai`, the address of the document the
    answer names (the explanatory note when it alone was enough, otherwise the bill's text; only when the address rule
    of "Oficialaus teksto adresas" accepts it), the day, and the answer's statement in the history; if a person wrote or
    removed the description while the provider was
    answering, the summary is dropped. The job creates the bill's record when it stores a summary on a bill without one
    (8.12.1); an unavailable or insufficient text creates nothing;
  - every summary made is audited as `description.summary.made`, actor empty with the role `system`, target the bill,
    details the bill and the model, never the text; and logged with the bill and the model.
- **Off and silent.** The job checks, in this order, `Ai:SummariesPerDay` = 0, no reader (`IBillTextSource.CanRead`
  false), no provider; it then makes nothing and logs the reason once, again only when the reason changes. Nothing
  offers or promises a summary while any of the three holds: the methodology page says how AI summaries are made only
  while all three allow them (`AiSummaries` in `GET /api/v1/appeals/rules`).
- **The provider is off until configured:** the worker gets `Ai__Provider` (`LT2_AI_PROVIDER`, `claude`) and the
  `ai_api_key` secret in compose, both empty by default; when both are set the provider's host joins the worker's
  outbound allowlist, as on the web host (6.4).
- **Not built.** No reader of the bills' official texts exists: `IBillTextSource` has only an "unavailable"
  implementation (`UnavailableBillTextSource`), because e-seimas has no API and its page structure must be confirmed
  against live responses first (7.1). So the job makes nothing, the draft without pasted text is not offered, and the
  moderator's form (pasted official text, 8.11.4) is the way to a description. Everything that uses a reader's
  documents (the choice of document, its address, the statement) is built and tested with a stand-in reader.
- **Not in what MPs receive.** A summary to MPs and its public copy carry the number and the official title, never a
  description (8.4): `DigestCandidate` and the summary snapshot carry none, and a test finds an AI summary's text in
  neither the snapshot, the public copy (page and API) nor the moderators' summaries screen. If that ever changes, an
  `ai` text stays out until a person has confirmed it.
- **The link preview** (8.22.5) uses the description only when a person wrote or checked it, never an `ai` text.
- **The methodology page** (`/metodika#descriptions`) says how descriptions are made, what the labels mean and how to
  report an error, and names both prompt files by their paths in the repository, always.
- **Locally**, the demonstration data (`--demo-appeal`, stand-in data only, refused wherever the demonstration record on
  XVP-0412 is refused) gives XVP-0533 an invented `ai` description (not made by any AI) with the bill's e-seimas
  address, so the label, the report and the moderator's actions can be seen without a provider key; a bill whose
  description a person has touched is left as it is.

## 8.13.1 The readable title

(Owner, 2026-11-24.) The official titles say which article of which law changes ("… 20 straipsnio
pakeitimo įstatymo projektas"), not what changes. A bill's record may carry a readable title beside its description.

- **What it is:** "Trumpas pavadinimas", one line of at most 90 characters in plain neutral Lithuanian saying what the
  bill changes ("Didinamas neapmokestinamųjų pajamų dydis"): no evaluation, nothing that is not in the source, no
  "įstatymo projektas". It is made only from the official text, like the description, never by rewording the official
  title.
- **Kept with the description**, as one thing: `bill_record.plain_title`, under the same `description_source`, label,
  day and history (a revision holds both, and the history page shows what changed in each), the same "Pranešti apie
  klaidą" (`inaccurate` covers both) and the same moderator's actions. A readable title needs a description; a
  description may have none. Removing the description removes both. The official title stays the Parliament module's,
  as the Seimas supplies it.
- **The AI makes both in one answer:** the moderator's draft (8.11.4) fills "Trumpas pavadinimas" and the description
  together, and the summary job stores both. Both prompts ask for the title and the description in one fixed form; an
  answer without a valid title (empty, longer than 90 characters, more than one line) is a failure like any invalid
  answer, and the "not enough" answer stores neither.
- **The moderator's form** has "Trumpas pavadinimas" above "Apie ką projektas", with its hint ("Iki 90 ženklų: kas
  keičiasi, paprastai ir neutraliai, be vertinimų. Sąrašuose rodomas virš oficialaus pavadinimo."), a character count,
  and the draft's label as the description field has it. A moderator may leave it empty or write one by hand.
- **In lists of bills** (Projektai, "Mano projektai", the front page, the agenda, the votes, an MP's votes, the
  statistics list): the readable title is the row's link, in place of the official title, with the official title in
  fine print under it. The number's tag and the row's other tags (stage, vote kind, dates) keep their line above the
  title, as today, on every screen and in every list, so the rows of all lists keep one shape. A bill without one shows
  the official title as the link, as today. An `ai` title is shown so too: the official title is always under it, and
  the bill's page carries the label.
- **On the bill's page:** the heading is the readable title, followed by "Oficialus pavadinimas: {official title}" and
  the label of its source. Without one, the official title is the heading.
- **Off the site, only what a person wrote or checked:** the link preview (8.22.5) and members' emails may use a
  readable title whose source is `moderator` or `ai-draft`, never an `ai` one. Summaries to MPs and their public copy
  carry the official title only (8.4).
- **Search:** every search of bills matches the number, the official title and the readable title, never the
  description (8.12.5, "How bills are searched").
- Mockups: `design/ui/pavadinimai-*.html` (Projektai, a search, the bill's page with the moderator's form, the agenda,
  the votes, the front page, closed bills), with screenshots in `design/ui/pavadinimai/`.
