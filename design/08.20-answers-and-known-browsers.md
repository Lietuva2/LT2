# 8.20 What participants hear about answers, and known browsers

## 8.20.1 "Seimo narių atsakymai": the fourth email

- Of the notices in 8.6, this is the fourth beside what happened to a person's own argument (moderation notices),
  "planned" and "voted" (the follow emails of 8.11.2, which a person with a side gets because taking a side follows the
  bill): **"MPs responded"**. **Not built.** "The bill was changed between stages": it needs the bill's text versions,
  which the import does not read (8.13).
- **The event:** an MP's response (8.5.5) on a followed bill was published, or published again with another stance.
  `appeals.mp_response` keeps `announced_at`, when the stance now shown became public (the first publication, or a later
  one with another stance than the response had, shown or withdrawn); a changed explanation with the same stance is not
  an event, and a withdrawal is not an event. The Appeals module gives the published ones of a period through
  `IMpResponseFeed` (SharedKernel), leaving out responses on a bill whose record the moderators hid.
- **In the person's email rhythm (8.24).** With "Iš karto" an answer is told at the follow job's next run, within about
  a quarter of an hour, in that run's one email to the person; with the daily or weekly choice, in the gathered email.
  `Follows:ResponsesAt` (08:00) is the hour of the daily and weekly emails. An answer first seen more than three days
  after it became public is recorded and not sent, as a vote; nothing is dropped for a missed run.
- **What the email says:** the lines list the MPs whose responses became public or changed stance: name, faction (the
  one the profile shows, `IMpDirectory.FactionsAsync`), the stance ("balsuos už", "balsuos prieš", "dar
  neapsisprendė") and the link to the bill's part "Seimo narių atsakymai". Never the explanation's text, as with
  organisations' statements.
- A response hidden, withdrawn or changed again by the time of sending is left out; if none remain, no email goes.
- **To whom:** members who followed the bill before the response became public, under the rules of 8.11.2 (one email
  per event per person, nothing about the person's side or other people, never about something from before they
  followed). Each follower's list holds only the answers that became public after they followed, so two followers of
  one bill may get different lists. The MP's own account and office members get the email without their own MP's line,
  and none when nothing else remains (Identity says whom an account speaks for, `IMpLinks.SpeaksForAsync`).
- **The switch:** a fourth one on the account page, "Kai Seimo nariai atsako dėl sekamo projekto", like the other three,
  on at first, kept in `notice_setting` (`responses`). The email ends with the link to the switches, like the others.
  The setting `Follows:ResponsesAt` stands on the settings screen under "Laiškai sekantiems".
- Recorded in `bill_event` with kind `response`, one per answer (key: `FollowRules.ResponseKey`, the Seimas person number
  and the moment the stance now shown became public), only for a followed bill; no person and no text in it; with the
  per-rhythm marks of 8.24.

## 8.20.2 Known browsers

- A browser that has signed in to an account keeps a one-year cookie `__Host-lt2.device` (`Secure`, `HttpOnly`,
  `SameSite=Lax`) with a random identifier. `identity.known_device` keeps its keyed hash, the account, first and last
  seen. Rows are per account and cookie, so one browser can be known to several accounts with one cookie. The cookie
  gives no access by itself: it only chooses which code allowance and which codes apply (`KnownBrowserRules`). The
  hash's key is a secret of its own, `device_hash_key` (`Security:DeviceHashKey`, web only), not the IP key, which may be
  rotated (6.5); without it no browser is known and the web host says so once at start.
- A known browser has its own five codes an hour and ten a day for that account (`SignIn:CodesPerHour`,
  `SignIn:CodesPerDay`, counted separately for each known browser), outside the limit shared by all other browsers, so
  whoever holds the cookie (a shared computer keeps it after signing out) gets no more tries a day than the shared limit
  allows. A code it asked for (`sign_in_code.known_device_id`) is accepted only from it; requests and wrong guesses from
  elsewhere neither use it up nor cancel it.
- A known browser tries a typed code against its own newest code and the newest code any browser may use; a wrong code
  counts as an attempt against each code it was tried against, so the shared code keeps its five attempts. A request
  from a known browser replaces only its own earlier code; one from elsewhere only the shared one.
- The cookie is set on a successful sign-in by code or passkey (a staff or passkey session started in place included)
  and at the end of registration, and renewed on later sign-ins. A code sign-in of a pending account sets no cookie: the
  browser becomes known when the registration is completed. Signing out keeps it: that is its purpose.
- **The person sees and removes them.** The account page, beside the sessions, lists "Žinomos naršyklės" with first
  and last seen and "Pamiršti" (`GET /api/v1/me/known-browsers`, `DELETE /api/v1/me/known-browsers/{id}`); the browser
  asking is marked "Ši naršyklė". Forgetting one makes that browser a stranger again. At most 20 per account; those seen
  longest ago go first. A row not seen for a year is deleted by the daily clean-up. Forgetting a browser, the cap and
  the yearly clean-up delete its codes with it, so a forgotten browser's code never becomes one any browser may use.
- They are in the person's export and go with the account. The table is withheld from the read-only role.
- The cookie is strictly necessary (it keeps a stranger from locking the person out), so it needs no consent; the
  privacy notice lists it (8.22.3).
