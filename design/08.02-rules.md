# 8.2 Rules

- One record per bill, created when first needed (8.12.1); one position per person per bill; sides are "for" and
  "against". Abstaining is not a request to an MP, so it is not offered.
- Positions are private. Arguments are public with their author's display name. Nothing public says who took part first
  on a bill.
- A reason belongs to one side and is plain text. **Its length is a runtime setting, not a hard limit:** the members'
  texts setting `Texts:MaxLength` (5.6: characters, 2 000; 0 = no limit), which also covers organisations' statements
  (8.14.3) and MPs' responses (8.5.5), checked where each text is written, so every caller meets it (6.8), and its
  refusal names the number that holds. One technical ceiling holds whatever the setting says, 20 000 characters: a guard
  against a runaway request, not a rule; the shared validators check only that. A lower number later shortens and
  removes nothing already written. The writing boxes' counters follow the setting and only count characters at 0; the
  methodology's "Ribos vienam žmogui" says the length that holds.
- **"Rodyti daugiau".** On the bill's page, an organisation's page, an MP's profile and the summaries' public copy a long
  member's text shows its beginning, cut at a word boundary, then "…" and "Rodyti daugiau": a reason beyond about 300
  characters plus a margin of 100; an organisation's statement or an MP's response beyond about 1 500 plus 500 (so with
  the default setting they are never cut). It is the browser's own `details.more` (8.15), so it opens without
  JavaScript and an island never opens it from code; opened, the "…" steps aside, the rest follows the beginning, and
  the same control, still in the page and focusable, reads "Rodyti mažiau" under the rest and closes it again (the
  summary holds both texts from the resources, the one not in force `display: none`; flex `order` draws it after the
  rest). One pure function cuts (`TextExcerpt`, one source file compiled into SharedKernel and the browser's assembly),
  one component shows (`LongText`). The moderators' queue and panels show every text whole; the email to MPs shortens
  reasons the same way, with a link (8.19.3); the moderators' summaries screen shows the part common to every MP
  (8.19.2).
- **A text's layout: paragraphs, links and lists, no formatting.** People type plain text; there is no Markdown, no
  formatting toolbar and no editor library, because several texts go out by email, text pasted from Word would turn
  stray `*` or `_` into formatting, rendering markup from users widens what an attacker can try (6.4), and bold or
  headings would make one side's arguments look louder than the other's. What a person needs is read from the plain
  text by fixed rules:
  - **Paragraphs:** a line break starts a new paragraph; empty lines between them add nothing.
  - **Links:** an address starting with `https://` is a link in a comment (8.23.3) and in "Apie mane" (8.25.3), and
    stays text in an argument, a statement, a response, the description and the letter (8.23.3 keeps a link in an
    argument a reason to wait for a moderator).
  - **Lists:** two or more lines in a row that begin with "- ", "– ", "• " or "* " are a bulleted list; two or more
    that begin with a number of one to three digits and ". " or ") " ("1. ", "2) ") are a numbered list, the numbers
    shown as typed (never renumbered). The space may be left out: "-", "–", "•" or the number right before a word
    ("-pirma", "1.pirma", "2)antra") counts as a marker when a letter or an opening quote or bracket follows it, so "-5 %"
    keeps its minus, "1.5" and "10.30" stay numbers and "--" stays as typed; "*" always needs its space, so "*žodis*"
    stays a word between stars. The marker is removed, the rest of the line is the item. One such line alone stays a
    paragraph, so a sentence that begins with a dash is not made a one-item list. One level only: indentation is ignored
    and no list sits inside another. A line that does not begin with a marker ends the list.
  - Nothing else: no bold, italics, headings, quotes or tables; `*žodis*` stays as typed.
  - **Where it holds:** every text a person types and the site shows as paragraphs: arguments, organisations' statements,
    MPs' responses, comments, "Apie mane", the bill's description and the introduction letter (8.19.5), on their pages,
    the summaries' public copy and the moderators' screens. One pure function splits a text into paragraphs and lists
    (`TextLayout`, one source file compiled into SharedKernel and the browser's assembly, as `TextExcerpt` is), one
    component shows it (`TypedText`; `LongText` lays out the whole text and then cuts it). Lists in a short preview line
    (a member's page's list of texts, a name's card, the history of a description's edits) stay one line.
  - **What it does not change:** the text is stored exactly as typed; the length, the counters, pre-screening and
    `TextExcerpt` work on that stored text (a list's markers count as characters). A shortened text shows its beginning
    with the same layout, so a list cut by "Rodyti daugiau" stays a list on both sides, the numbers as typed. The
    moderators' screens show the same layout. In email only the introduction letter is laid out: its plain part stays as
    typed, its HTML part shows the paragraphs and lists. An argument quoted in a summary's email keeps its lines as typed
    in both parts (it stands inside the email's own list of arguments).
  - **The writing box** says it in one line under the field (`TextLayoutHint`), a field's hint needed to fill it (8.15),
    under the argument, comment, statement, response, "Apie mane", description and letter boxes: "Sąrašą rašykite
    eilutėmis, pradėtomis „-“ arba „1.“." The letter's own hint says "Kiekviena eilutė – atskira pastraipa. Iki 10 000
    ženklų."
- **No limit per person by default.** How many reasons a person may write on a bill, and how many "Man svarbu" marks
  they may give on a bill, are two runtime settings for all bills (`Appeals:ReasonsPerPerson`,
  `Appeals:EndorsementsPerPerson`, each 0 to 100); 0 means no limit, and both default to 0, so a limit is switched on
  only when the need shows. While a setting is 0 the pages show no counters and no "you have N left". A limit switched on
  later applies to new reasons and marks only: nothing already written or marked is removed, and a person over the limit
  cannot add until they are under it (`reason.limit`, `endorsement.limit`). Only reasons that are shown or waiting for a
  moderator count; hidden, merged and withdrawn ones do not. The hourly per-account rate limits (6.4) apply as well.
- **An author can withdraw their own reason** while the bill is open: it is deleted with its "Man svarbu" marks and
  disappears from the page; open reports on it are closed; a summary already made keeps its frozen text, which carries no
  name. The audit log records the withdrawal (`reason.withdrawn`) without the text or the side. On a closed bill a
  reason cannot be withdrawn (the page there is the record beside the Seimas vote); a moderator can still hide it, and
  deleting the account removes it.
- **An author can edit their own reason until someone reacts.** The text and the side it was written for may be
  changed ("⋯" → "Redaguoti", shown only while it is allowed) while the bill is open, the reason is shown or waiting, and
  nobody has reacted to it: no "Man svarbu" (any mark, counted or not) and no comment under it (any comment, hidden ones
  included). Those reactions were given to its exact words, so after one the words stay; the author can still withdraw
  it (`reason.reacted`: "Šio argumento redaguoti nebegalima: į jį jau sureagavo kiti. Galite jį atsiimti."). A writing
  restriction blocks editing as it blocks writing. The new text keeps the rules of writing: the length, and pre-screening
  (a flagged edit waits for a moderator again); the similar reasons are not offered again. The moderator's "seen" goes
  with the old words, so the edited reason is in the queue again as not yet seen (8.3). Checked where it is written,
  under the reason's lock, which "Man svarbu" and a comment under it take too, so an edit and a reaction never cross. No
  earlier text is kept (nobody had reacted to it); the audit records that an edit happened (`reason.edited`), never the
  text or the side; the bill's last activity moves when the edited reason is published, and the edited reason is new
  again to readers (8.11.8). `PUT /api/v1/reasons/{id}`.
- **A reason may be written for either side, whatever the author's own position.** The writing box asks for the side:
  it preselects the author's own side when they have taken one (read only from their own, never-cached answer), and
  preselects nothing when they have not, so they must choose. A person who has not taken a side may write reasons for
  either side or for both. Writing a reason never creates or changes a position, and nothing public (page, API, cached
  answer, summary) tells whether a reason is on its author's side, so a reason does not reveal its author's position.
- Writing a reason does not need the positions consent (6.5), because no position is stored: writing a reason is the
  author's own deliberate publication. The writing box says plainly that the reason is public with the author's name
  and the side chosen, the author can withdraw it, and the privacy and methodology texts say the same.
- No reason is marked as anyone's but its author's: nothing ties a reason's side to its author's side.
- **"Man svarbu" on either side, counted only on one's own.** A member may mark arguments of either side, with or
  without a side of their own. Marking needs what taking a side needs: accepted terms and the positions consent (6.5; a
  mark is about a political question; the first mark without it asks for it in place, with the side buttons' form and
  "Sutinku ir pažymiu „Man svarbu“"; the form's text, version `2026-11-18`, names the marks as well as the sides), never
  on one's own argument (below), within `Appeals:EndorsementsPerPerson`, which counts all of a person's marks on the
  bill, counted or not.
  - **A mark counts only while the argument's side is its giver's current side** (`AppealRules.MarkCounts`). Everything
    that uses marks uses only counted marks: the "Man svarbu · N" figure, the order of the arguments, the arguments a
    summary chooses (8.4, 8.19) and the counts in it, the moderators' screens. It is computed from the current position
    when the marks are read (a join, never a stored flag), so it cannot drift.
  - Taking, changing or withdrawing a side deletes no mark; marks only start or stop counting. Withdrawing the positions
    consent deletes positions and marks, not reasons; deleting the account, a moderator hiding an argument and merging
    follow their own rules (8.3).
  - **The giver's own view:** in their never-cached answer (`/record/mine`, `Uncounted`) their marks show pressed; one
    that does not count carries a short note under the argument's row, which the button is described by: without a side
    "Bus skaičiuojama, kai prašysite balsuoti už." ("… prieš."), with the other side "Neskaičiuojama: jūs prašote
    balsuoti prieš." ("… už.") The served page is the same for everyone.
  - Marks stay private: no names, for anyone (8.23.10 says why).
  - A query for how often marks do not count (per bill and in total, counts only) is in `agents/features.md`.
- **Never on one's own argument.** The marks choose which arguments are sent to MPs (8.4), so an author does not mark
  their own: refused where the mark is written (`endorsement.own-reason`, "Savo argumento „Man svarbu“ žymėti
  negalima."), and the button beside one's own argument is disabled with that sentence as its hint, known from the
  person's never-cached answer (the served page stays the same for everyone). When a moderator merges an argument into
  another (8.3), a mark that would move onto its giver's own argument is dropped; the others move. "Sutinku" has the
  same rule (8.23.10: not on a comment one wrote).
- **A mark moves nothing on the page.** The arguments are ordered by their marks when the page is read; after the
  person's own action (a mark, its removal, a new or withdrawn argument) the island shows the new numbers in place and
  keeps the shown order, a new argument last, and the pressed button gets the focus back without scrolling. The order by
  marks returns when the page is read again.
- Pages, summaries and emails head reasons as "argumentai už / prieš" (reasons to vote for or against), never as the
  reasons of the people on that side.
- Published numbers: both sides, always together, always with the count for accounts older than 30 days. Group numbers
  (the supporters of the MP's party on a side) only when the group has at least `Digest:PartyGroupMinimum` people (8.4;
  a runtime setting, default 10, 0 = no floor), and, shown beside the side's count, only when the rest of that count
  has at least as many too (or none), so the rest cannot be worked out by subtraction (`GroupRules`); an empty group is
  never shown.
- Wording follows `AGENTS.md`: numbers are "Lietuva 2.0 naudotojai", never "the public" or "voters"; differences are
  shown neutrally.
- What the bill is about is one description per bill, written or corrected by a moderator with a statement (8.12.3,
  8.13) or made by the summary job from official text, labelled by its source ("Sukurta DI · žmonių neperžiūrėta",
  "Parašė moderatorius", "Sukurta DI · taisė žmonės"; 8.13) and kept with its public edit history. A bill without one
  shows its official title and the link to the Seimas page.
