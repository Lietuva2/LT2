# 8.14 Organisations on a bill: a voice, not control

## 8.14.1 Why a voice and not control

Some bills have organisations that have worked on the issue for years: associations, unions, business and patient
groups. They bring knowledge, and they bring their members, which the site needs. They do not get control of a bill
(its threshold, its timing), for three reasons:

- An organisation engaged in an issue is on one side of it. The threshold and the timing decide what MPs hear about
  **both** sides (8.4).
- Several organisations care about the same bill, often on opposite sides. Control has no answer to who decides. A
  voice works for any number of them.
- MPs and the public must be able to trust that no participant shapes the numbers. "All MPs and factions are treated
  on equal terms" (8.4) has to hold for organisations too.

So an organisation speaks in its own name, in its own place on the bill's page, and can bring people. It decides
nothing about the bill's record.

## 8.14.2 What an organisation is

There is no list of who qualifies and nobody approves an organisation before it appears; moderators act afterwards
(8.14.9).

- **A public profile** (`/o/{slug}`): name, website, a short description in its own words, whether it declares itself
  a registered lobbyist, its legal entity code if it gives one, and the bills it has statements on. Any kind:
  associations, companies, parties, communities, groups without legal form.
- **Registered by a member, shown at once.** A signed-in member registers an organisation and is its first
  representative. At most `Organisations:PerAccount` (3) per account keeps one person from flooding the list.
- **Confirmed or not, and the page says which.**
  - An organisation starts as "Nepatvirtinta".
  - It is confirmed without anyone's approval when a representative proves an email address on the domain of the
    organisation's website, with a code sent there as at sign-in. The label then says "Patvirtinta per el. pašto
    adresą @domenas" and names the domain, so a reader sees exactly what was confirmed. Addresses of public mail
    services confirm nothing.
  - An organisation without a domain of its own can ask a moderator, who confirms it by hand and records what was
    checked ("Patvirtino moderatorius"). Audited.
  - Confirmation changes the label only. An unconfirmed organisation can do everything else.
  - **The chip says "Patvirtinta"; how, one press away.** Wherever the label is a chip (the bill's organisations'
    statements, the organisation's page, the list of organisations, the representative's own panels, the moderators'
    queue) a confirmed organisation's chip reads "Patvirtinta" and is a hint (8.15, the browser's popover: a press, a tap
    or the keyboard opens it, without JavaScript too) whose answer is the whole label, "Patvirtinta per el. pašto adresą
    @domenas" or "Patvirtino moderatorius", so the domain is still there to read. "Nepatvirtinta" is shown whole (the
    agenda's rows show only that one). A place that gives the chip no id falls back to the whole text as its title and
    for screen readers. Where the label is running text, not a chip (a member's followed organisations, a comment by an
    organisation, emails), it is whole. This is a hint per chip, which 8.15 otherwise keeps to one beside a list: here it
    holds the chip's own value, not an explanation of a term.
  - It proves control of a domain, not that the organisation is who it says. A look-alike name or domain is
    impersonation: a ground for a report and for blocking (8.14.9).
- **Representatives.** A representative can add other members as representatives and remove them. A representative
  acts for it in an ordinary signed-in session; no passkey is required. Their own side on any bill stays private and
  separate; nothing ties it to the organisation. Representatives' names are not public: a statement is the
  organisation's.
- **Nothing is sold.** No payment buys a profile, a place, an order or a mention. Organisations appear on equal terms.

## 8.14.3 The statement on a bill

- **One statement per organisation per bill**, the same shape as an MP's response (5.3 `response`):
  - a stance: asks to vote for, asks to vote against, or remarks without a side;
  - a text of plain text as long as the members' texts setting allows (`Texts:MaxLength`, 2 000 by default, 5.6;
    shown whole up to about 1 500 and longer behind "Rodyti daugiau", 8.2), and one link to the organisation's full
    position;
  - published at once, like an argument: pre-screening flags it, anyone can report it, a moderator can hide it with a
    statement (8.12.3);
  - edits keep their history; it can be withdrawn while the bill is open and is frozen when the bill closes.
- **Where it shows.** On the bill's page in its own section, "Organizacijų pozicijos", after the MPs' responses and
  before the arguments, at the page's full width: for and against side by side, remarks below, each in the order
  published. Both sides are always shown together, including an empty side. The section shows only when an
  organisation has a statement shown on the bill, or for a representative of an organisation, whose way to publish,
  change or withdraw its statement is there; otherwise the page has no such section (the list of organisations is
  linked from the footer, the account page and the methodology). A representative also sees there a statement a
  moderator hid, quiet, with the moderator's explanation. The agenda's and the front page's rows (8.7) name the
  organisations with a public statement on the bill with their stance, in the same order, never with a number.
- **Never mixed into the numbers.** A statement is not a position, is not counted among participants, and does not
  bring a bill closer to its threshold. Statements carry no "Man svarbu" and no count of their own, so there is no
  second popularity number beside the users' counts. Members comment under a statement ("Aptarimas (n)", 8.23), the
  organisation's own comments there shown under its name; 8.23.1 says why the number of comments is not a popularity
  count.
- An organisation writes no arguments (8.2) in its name. Its representative may write arguments as a person, like any
  member.

## 8.14.4 What MPs receive

Nothing. No summary (8.4), public copy or other email to MPs names an organisation or a statement, and a statement never
makes a bill eligible for a summary: eligibility counts users only.

**Not built.** A line in each bill's part of the summary, after the arguments: which organisations ask to vote for,
which against, and which sent remarks; names only, each linking to the statement on the public page, no statement text
in the email; only statements public when the summary is prepared (8.3, 8.19.2); only confirmed organisations a
moderator has not kept out of summaries (8.14.9); frozen in the summary's snapshot and shown on its public copy (8.7).

## 8.14.5 A goal to share towards

- An organisation with a stance for or against may set one public goal on that bill: a number of participants, shown
  in its statement with the progress, for example "Siekiame 1 000 dalyvių · dabar 640".
- It counts participants on the organisation's side. Both sides' counts stay on the same page above it, as always.
- It is the organisation's own target for its members and supporters. It does not change the threshold, the timing or
  anything MPs receive, and it does not appear in summaries.

## 8.14.6 Bringing members

- **An organisation's invitation link** (6.2), which lands a new member on the bill (8.11.5: the bill's address with the
  code after the `#`).
- **No record ties a person to the organisation.** A registration through an organisation's link raises that link's
  counter and stores nothing else about where the account came from. The organisation sees the counter: how many
  registered through its link, nothing more. The administrators' invitations page shows registrations per day for
  organisations' codes as for every other code; on a day with few registrations an administrator could match a new
  account to a code by its registration date.
- **Following.** A member can follow an organisation (8.11.2) and is emailed when it publishes a statement. Who
  follows is private like every follow, from the organisation too. How many follow it is shown on its profile, as for
  everything that can be followed (8.11.2), and not beside its statements on a bill's page, which carry no number of
  their own (8.14.3).

## 8.14.7 What an organisation does not get

- The threshold, the timing of summaries, holding or releasing, an urgent summary. It can flag a date or ask for an
  urgent summary as any member can (8.4).
- The bill's description. It can report an error like anyone (8.13).
- Any moderation right.
- Any data about people: no list of members or supporters, no count of "its" members on either side, no group
  numbers. The rule that group numbers need a minimum group (8.2, `Digest:PartyGroupMinimum`) is **not** extended to
  organisations: membership of a union, a religious community or a political organisation is special-category data,
  and the site does not collect it.

## 8.14.8 Several organisations on one bill

- Any number, on either side, each with its own statement and goal. No organisation leads, owns or closes a bill,
  and none can keep another out.
- Organisations acting together each publish their own statement and may link to the same joint text.
- The page treats all of them alike: same size, same order rule, no highlighting.

## 8.14.9 Keeping it honest

- Statements are moderated after the fact, by the same rules as arguments. The public moderation rules (8.3) have a
  part for organisations.
- **What a moderator can do.** Not refuse a registration: there is none to refuse. Afterwards, each with a statement,
  audited and undoable:
  - **block an organisation:** its profile says so, its statements are hidden and its representatives cannot publish;
    lifting the block brings the statements back;
  - **hide one statement**, as an argument is hidden (8.12.3).
- **Not built.** Keeping an organisation out of summaries to MPs (it would stay on the site as it was; its
  representatives told, with the statement): no summary names an organisation (8.14.4).
- A new organisation and each statement not yet seen appear in the moderators' queue to be looked at, not approved,
  like arguments (8.3). Reports have the ground "Apsimeta kita organizacija".
- **Is the name on its website?** The moderator's look at a new organisation includes its website: the queue shows the
  link, and the moderator opens it. This catches a real but unrelated domain given for someone else's name, which the
  email check passes. It is not an automatic gate: names appear abbreviated, in other grammatical cases, inside a logo
  or on pages built by scripts, so a gate would fail honest organisations, and whoever owns a look-alike domain puts the
  name there anyway.
- **Not built.** An automatic check that fetches the website and flags "pavadinimas svetainėje nerastas" in the queue,
  deciding nothing, like pre-screening. It would make the servers fetch addresses users give, so it would need its own
  protection and an exception to the outbound allowlist (6.4).
- The methodology page explains what a statement is, that it is not counted, what "Patvirtinta" does and does not mean,
  and that nothing is paid for.

## 8.14.10 Data, in outline

All are tables of the Appeals module (`appeals`, 5.3).

| Table | Holds |
|---|---|
| `organisation` | `id`, `slug` (unique, never changes or is reused), `name`, `name_key` (the name without case and extra spaces; unique among `active` ones), `description`, `website`, `legal_code`, `declared_lobbyist`, `registered_by_user_id` (emptied with the account; never public), `registered_at`, `status` (`active`, `blocked`, `closed`), `confirmed_by` (`domain`, `moderator` or null), `confirmed_domain`, `confirmed_at`, `confirmed_by_user_id` and `confirmation_note` (a moderator's hand), `domain_barred` (a moderator withdrew a confirmation: only a moderator confirms it), `blocked_at`, `blocked_by_user_id`, `block_statement`, `closed_at`, `seen_at` (null while new or changed: in the queue), `screening_flags`, `changed_at`. |
| `organisation_representative` | `id`, `organisation_id`, `user_id`, `granted_by_user_id` (whose link brought them), `granted_at`, `ended_at`; one current row per person and organisation. Deleted with the account. |
| `organisation_invite` | `id`, `organisation_id`, `token_hash` (SHA-256 of the one-time link's token; the token itself is shown once), `created_by_user_id`, `created_at`, `expires_at` (seven days), `used_at`. |
| `organisation_code` | `id`, `organisation_id`, `domain` (the website's host), `code_hash`, `created_at`, `expires_at`, `failed_attempts`, `consumed_at`, `requested_by_user_id`. Never the mailbox: it exists only in the outbox row until the email is sent. |
| `organisation_statement` | `id`, `organisation_id`, `bill_number` (a statement never creates the bill's record), `stance` (`for`, `against`, `remarks`), `text`, `link`, `goal` (1 to 1 000 000, not with remarks), `status` (`pending`, `published`, `hidden`, `withdrawn`; one `pending`, `published` or `hidden` row per organisation and bill), `screening_flags`, `seen_at`, `published_at`, `first_public_at` (when it first became public; the follow emails go by it), `edited_at`, `withdrawn_at`, `written_by_user_id` (the writer or last changer; never public; emptied with the account). |

The organisation's invitation code is an `identity.invitation` of the kind `organisation` (`organisation_id`,
`organisation_off`; 5.2) and a follow of an organisation a `notify.follow` of the kind `organisation` (5.4); nothing in
`appeals` refers to either.

Earlier names and websites, and earlier versions of a statement, are rows of `revision` (entity `organisation`, fields
`name` and `website`; entity `statement`, field `version` holding the replaced version as JSON), all without a person.
Reports (`report`) take the targets `organisation` and `statement` (its `record_id` is then empty), and moderation actions
(`moderation_action`) the same target kinds.

## 8.14.11 Before organisations are named to MPs

Organisations are on the site only: nothing about them reaches an MP (8.14.4, 8.19).

## 8.14.14 How it works in detail

**Registering and the profile.**

- **Registering** (`/organizacijos/nauja`, a signed-in member): name (3 to 120 characters; no other active organisation
  has the same name, compared without case), description (up to 500 characters of plain text, optional), website
  (optional; `https`, at most 200 characters), legal entity code (optional; 7 to 9 digits), "Esame registruoti
  lobistai" (a tick, the organisation's own declaration). The address `/o/{slug}` is made from the name and does not
  change when the name does. At most `Organisations:PerAccount` (3) organisations registered per account, counting
  those the account registered that are not closed (a blocked one counts). A person under a writing restriction (8.3)
  cannot register an organisation or publish for one, and the restriction email says so. Pre-screening reads the name,
  the description and every statement as it reads arguments; for the name and the description it flags, never hides:
  the organisation is shown at once and its flags are shown in the moderators' queue.
- **Names.** "No other organisation with the same name" counts only active ones, so a blocked or closed one frees its
  name; lifting a block is refused (`organisation.name-taken`) when another took the name meanwhile.
- **Changing the profile.** Any representative. The earlier names and websites are in a public history on the profile,
  newest first, each change as "A → B" (a short text, not compared word by word). A new name or website, or a new
  description that pre-screening flags, puts the organisation back in the moderators' queue to be looked at; a new
  website removes a confirmation by domain unless the new host is the confirmed domain.
- **Pages.** `/organizacijos`: the public list of active organisations in Lithuanian alphabetical order, each with its
  label, with a search by name that keeps names with every word of the query, at most 500 shown. `/o/{slug}`: the
  profile with its statements by bill. No item in the menu; the list is linked from the section on the bill's page, the
  footer, the account page and the methodology page.
- **Users' links** (the website, a statement's link) are shown with their host, `rel="nofollow noopener ugc"`, in a new
  tab; never fetched.

**Confirmation by domain.**

- A representative gives an email address; it must be one plain mailbox (one `@`, no quoting, comments, routing (`%`,
  `!`) or address literals), on the website's host (without `www.`) or a name under it, and not on the fixed list of
  public mail services kept in the code. A code is sent there under the rules of sign-in codes (six digits, ten minutes,
  five attempts, single use, stored only as a hash). Entering it confirms the organisation and stores the domain and the
  time. The mailbox is not stored: only the domain.
- The confirmed domain stored and shown ("@domenas") is the website's host without `www.`, also when the address was on
  a name under it (`info@mail.lgs.lt` confirms `lgs.lt`). Hosts are compared in their IDN (punycode) form in lower case.
- **Shared platforms.** Beside the fixed list of public mail services, the code keeps a fixed list of shared platforms
  (social networks, site builders, blog and code hosts such as `facebook.com`, `wordpress.com`, `github.io`,
  `sites.google.com`). A website on either list, or on a host without a dot, cannot be confirmed by domain; the
  representative is told to ask the moderators.
- **Code limits.** `SignIn:CodesPerHour` and `SignIn:CodesPerDay` count per organisation (the endpoint also has the
  per-address-of-the-request limit of sign-in codes). Unlike sign-in, a representative over the limit is told so
  (`confirm.too-many`): they are a known member, and nothing about an address is revealed. A new code replaces the
  earlier one; a code sent for an earlier website, or before a moderator withdrew a confirmation, confirms nothing. An
  organisation already confirmed (either way) is not offered a code.
- A moderator may **confirm by hand** (a required note of what was checked) and may **withdraw** any confirmation with a
  statement; after a withdrawal only a moderator can confirm that organisation.

**Representatives.**

- A representative makes a one-time link and gives it to a colleague; a signed-in member who opens it and agrees
  becomes a representative. The link is a random token whose hash only is stored; it works once, within seven days,
  while the organisation is active and has fewer than ten representatives. Its secret is the typeable one-time code of
  6.8: the representative who makes the link sees the code beside it ("Be nuorodos: puslapyje {page} įvedamas šis
  kodas:") and may pass either on; `/organizacijos/kvietimas` opened without the link asks for "Kvietimo kodas". The
  token goes in the request body (`POST /api/v1/organisation-invites/view` and `…/accept`), never in an address. The
  site emails nothing here, and nobody is looked up by email.
- Representatives see each other's display names; nobody else does, staff included, except that a moderator reaches a
  statement's writer to restrict them, as with an argument (8.3). At most ten per organisation.
- Representatives are removed by the representative row's id, never an account id; a representative cannot remove
  themself (they leave). The last one cannot leave and can only **close** the organisation (not while it is blocked):
  its statements on open bills are withdrawn, those on closed bills stay as they were, its codes and links stop
  working, and the profile says when it closed and shows no statements; it is left out of `/organizacijos`. Closed
  organisations keep their representatives' rows, so "Mano organizacijos" says "Uždaryta". Deleting the account of
  the last representative closes it the same way, also when it is blocked.
- The account page (`/paskyra`) lists "Mano organizacijos"; the export has the person's representative roles and the
  statements they wrote; `written_by_user_id` and the other references to the account are emptied when the account
  goes.

**Statements.**

- On an active bill whose record is not hidden or closed (or that has no record yet), a representative publishes the
  organisation's one statement from the "Organizacijų pozicijos" section of the bill's page: stance (`for`, `against`,
  `remarks`), text (not blank, up to `Texts:MaxLength`, 5.6), one link (optional; `https`, at most 500 characters,
  shown with its host), and with `for` or `against` an optional goal (1 to 1 000 000 participants).
- It behaves as an argument does: shown at once unless pre-screening flags it, then it waits for a moderator; "not yet
  seen" until a moderator looks. A changed statement is screened again and is "not yet seen" again; if the change is
  flagged, the statement waits (its earlier version is not shown meanwhile).
- It can be changed and withdrawn while the bill is open, and is frozen when the bill closes. Every earlier version that
  was public is public, under the statement on the organisation's profile, newest first, the statement in force first
  with its own changes, each version with what it changed against the one before it (8.16.2).
- **Withdrawing** keeps the row as `withdrawn`, shown nowhere, and a new statement may follow. A hidden statement cannot
  be changed or withdrawn by the organisation (`statement.hidden`): it is a moderator's to restore.
- **The goal** shows as "Siekiame {N} dalyvių · dabar {n}" ("dalyvio" after a number ending in 1 but not 11), where n
  is everyone on that side as counted on the page, the organisation's representatives like anyone.
- A blocked or closed organisation's statements are shown nowhere; there is no separate hiding of an organisation, the
  block is it. The section says that statements are not counted among participants, and each statement carries the
  organisation's label (8.14.2).
- **On the bill's page** the section is shown with the open or closed record and on an active bill without one, and
  only with a statement or for a representative (8.14.3); a hidden record shows nothing of it, and a finished bill
  nobody took part in shows the bill only, so a statement on such a bill is seen only on the organisation's profile.
- A statement refers to its bill by number and never creates the bill's record, so it cannot change anything the record
  counts: the bill's counts, threshold progress, lists, agenda and summaries are the same with and without statements.

**Moderation.**

- The queue lists active organisations never looked at since registration or a new name or website (or a flagged new
  description), any organisation with an open report, and statements of active organisations that wait, or are
  published and not yet seen or reported. The queue's and the hidden list's organisation parts load in the browser only.
- Actions, each with a statement, recorded, audited (`moderation.organisation.*`, `moderation.statement.*`) and emailed
  to each current representative separately with the moderators' contact address: block and lift the block; hide and
  restore a statement; publish a waiting one; confirm by hand; withdraw a confirmation; "Palikti" (on a published
  statement: seen, reports closed).
- A statement still waiting when its bill closes can no longer be published (`statement.waiting-bill-closed`); the
  queue offers only "Palikti" (it stays unpublished and leaves the queue unless reported; the representatives are told)
  and hiding.
- Blocked organisations and hidden statements are on the list of hidden items with "Atkurti": for a blocked
  organisation it lifts the block (statement required); for a hidden statement it restores it while its bill takes
  statements.
- **Blocked** organisations: their representatives cannot publish, change, withdraw, make links, confirm or close;
  they can see their management controls, remove another and leave (not the last). The organisation is left out of
  `/organizacijos`; its profile says it is blocked, with the moderators' statement, and shows no statements. Lifting
  the block returns exactly what was shown.
- Anyone signed in can report an organisation or a statement; the grounds include "Apsimeta kita organizacija". The
  "Moderavimas" panel on the bill's page has its own moderators-only answer
  (`GET /api/v1/moderation/bills/{number}/statements`) and lists the bill's statements that wait, are flagged, reported
  or not yet seen; the others are behind a fold.
- The methodology page and the moderation rules have their parts (8.14.9).

**Privacy.**

- No answer, page, email or log line names an organisation's representatives to a visitor, a member or a moderator, or
  ties a representative's own side to it. No page, answer, log line or email shows who registered through an
  organisation's link, who follows it, or how "its" members took sides.
- The public histories (`revision`) carry no person, since the read-only role reads that table. Members' actions on
  organisations (registering, changing, confirming, links, statements) are recorded in the module's own tables and not
  audited, so no audit row ties a person to an organisation; moderators' actions are audited.
- The read-only role cannot read any of the five organisation tables: `organisation` and `organisation_statement` hold
  who registered and who wrote, beside the organisation.
- **Rate limits** (6.4): registering 5 an hour per account; changes, statements, links and replacing the invitation
  code under the limit of writing arguments (20 an hour).

**The API.** Under `/api/v1/organisations` (the list, a profile, registering, changing, the caller's own management
answer `…/{slug}/mine`, confirmation, links, representatives, leaving, closing, the invitation code),
`/api/v1/bills/{number}/organisations` (the bill's statements, the caller's own `…/mine`, and
`…/{slug}/statement` to publish, change or withdraw), `/api/v1/organisation-invites/view` and `…/accept`,
`/api/v1/me/organisations`, and for moderators `/api/v1/moderation/organisations` (the queue, `…/hidden`, and the
actions on an organisation), `/api/v1/moderation/statements/{id}/…` and `/api/v1/moderation/bills/{number}/statements`.
The services are `IOrganisations` and `IOrganisationModeration` in the Appeals module.

**The organisation's invitation code** (8.14.6).

- One per active organisation: an `identity.invitation` of the kind `organisation` (5.2, 6.2) with the organisation's id
  (an id the Identity module only stores) and no account (`issued_by_user_id` stays empty), in the format and under the
  rules of personal codes, with its own uses setting (`Registration:OrganisationCodeUses`, 0 = no limit, the default),
  which limits each code, so a replacement starts a new allowance. It is made when a representative first opens their
  part of the profile in the browser while the organisation is active (a unique index allows one current code per
  organisation); a blocked organisation that never had one gets it once the block is lifted. It belongs to the
  organisation, not to the representative who made it, and stays when that person leaves or deletes their account.
- **The level** of an organisation's code is that of a personal code of the representative who first asked for it
  (their level plus one); a replacement keeps it. Nothing of that person is kept on the code.
- **The representatives' part** of the profile, "Organizacijos kvietimo nuoroda": the registration link, one link per
  bill with a published statement (newest first, the bill's address with the code after the `#`, 8.11.5), each with
  "Kopijuoti nuorodą", the number ("Su organizacijos kodu užsiregistravo: N", all its codes together) and "Pakeisti
  kodą nauju" (the count carries over). Without a usable code the part says why instead of showing links. It is filled
  in only once the island runs in the browser, never prerendered. API: `GET /api/v1/organisations/{slug}/invitation`
  (never cached) and `POST …/invitation/replace`. The Appeals module says whether the caller represents the organisation,
  whether it is active and which bills it has public statements on (`IOrganisations.CodeAccessAsync`); the Identity
  module keeps the code (`IOrganisationInvitations`).
- **Nothing ties the new account to the organisation.** The pending account holds no organisation's code: the server
  checks the code and keeps nothing of it, and the registration form keeps the typed code in the tab's session storage
  (`lt2.kodas`) and sends it again with the completion; completing on another device asks for the code again. The
  completed account keeps no code (`invitation_id` and `invitation_code` stay empty), only the level in
  `profile.invitation_level`, which holds the level for every code. The code's counter and daily totals rise, and that
  is all. The registration form says that whoever gave the code sees that it was used, not by whom.
- **Two switches.** `invitation.organisation_off` (null, `blocked` or `closed`) follows the organisation: blocking
  switches the code off, lifting the block on, closing off for good. It is separate from the administrator's
  `switched_off_at`: lifting a block clears only `blocked`, so an administrator's switch-off stays. An administrator may
  switch an organisation's code on again unless it was replaced; while the organisation is blocked it still does not
  work. Either way a registration gets the usual one message (`invitation.invalid`). The Appeals module switches the
  codes through `IOrganisationCodeSwitch` (SharedKernel, implemented by Identity) inside its own transaction.
- **Replacing** is refused while the organisation is blocked or closed (`invitation.organisation-inactive`) or the
  administrator has the code off (`invitation.organisation-switched-off`).
- **The administrators' invitations page** names an organisation's code by the organisation's name, shows its kind as
  "organizacijos kodas" and, while its organisation is blocked or closed, says so; it lists them among the codes that
  brought the most people and can switch one off and on like any code. Like every code it has its per-day bars there;
  the organisation itself sees only the one number, which is not public and is never broken down: not by bill, not by
  day, not by side.

**Following an organisation** (8.14.6).

- "Sekti" / "Nebesekti" on the profile of an active organisation, for a member; a visitor sees the link to sign in. A
  `notify.follow` of the kind `organisation` with the organisation's id as the key; following reads organisations
  through `IOrganisationDirectory` (SharedKernel, implemented by Appeals). Only an active organisation can be followed
  (`organisation.not-found` otherwise, as for an unknown one); one already followed stays in the account page's list
  with the bills and MPs, marked "Užblokuota" or "Uždaryta", until unfollowed. It is in the export and goes with the
  account.
- **When a statement becomes public** (`first_public_at`): when it is published at once, when a moderator publishes it
  after it waited, or when a hidden one that was never public is restored; changes never move it. A statement withdrawn
  and published anew is a new statement and is told again.
- **The event** is a `notify.bill_event` of the kind `statement` under its bill, keyed by the statement's id: recorded
  when the job first sees it, sent to followers whose follow is older than both that moment and `first_public_at`,
  skipped when `first_public_at` is more than three days before the moment it was first seen, and skipped when, by the
  time it would be sent, the statement is no longer public or its organisation is blocked or closed. The job looks back
  fourteen days.
- **The email** (once per statement, never for a change; through the email rhythm, 8.24) names the organisation with
  its label in brackets, the bill's number and title, the stance in words ("prašo balsuoti už", "prašo balsuoti prieš",
  "pastabos, be prašymo balsuoti už ar prieš"), the bill's page and the account page, both full addresses
  (`SiteLinks.Absolute`); never the statement's text, the follower's side or any count. Its switch on the account page
  is `notice_setting.statements` (on unless turned off).
- Private like every follow: no page, answer, email or log shows who follows an organisation, the organisation
  included. How many follow it is shown on its profile (8.11.2).

**Emails to representatives.** The confirmation code, and what a moderator did (blocked, block lifted, "Palikti",
confirmed, confirmation withdrawn, statement published, kept, hidden or restored, a comment in its name hidden or
restored, 8.23.8), each to every current representative, through `OrganisationTemplates`; nothing names another
representative or a person's side.
